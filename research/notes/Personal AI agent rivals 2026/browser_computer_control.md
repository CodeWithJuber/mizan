# Browser and computer control by AI agents: state of the art and safe self-hosted design (as of 27 October 2026)

Scope note: facts are stated as of 27 Oct 2026. Items before 2026 are marked "(historical, <date>)". Benchmark figures are vendor self-reports unless marked "independent". Several 2026 numbers reached me only through leaderboard aggregators (Steel.dev, AIMachineVS, Modelglass, paperswithcode.co, LLMBoard). Each one is labelled as such. Where I found a vendor primary, I cite that instead.

## 1. Commercial products: what each can do, how well, and published benchmark numbers

### Takeaway
By October 2026 the market has consolidated. Three products are general-availability browser agents that act inside the user's real logged-in browser: **Claude in Chrome** (GA 26 Aug 2026), **Chrome auto browse / Gemini Spark** (Google), and OpenAI's agent, which now runs in the **ChatGPT desktop app and Chrome extension** after **Atlas was shut down on 9 Aug 2026**. Perplexity **Comet**, Opera **Neon** and Microsoft **Browse with Copilot** follow. Project Mariner, Copilot Mode and Atlas have all been retired. On capability, frontier models now beat the human baseline on OSWorld-Verified (75–86% vs 72.4%). On the new long-horizon OSWorld 2.0 they still finish only a fraction of tasks end to end. Anthropic publishes the most detailed injection-rate data of any vendor.

### Cited Findings

**Anthropic: computer use API, Claude in Chrome**
- (historical, 25 Aug 2025) Claude in Chrome launched as a pilot for 1,000 Max users. Across 123 test cases in 29 attack scenarios, prompt-injection attack success in autonomous mode was 23.6% without mitigations and 11.2% with them. On a "challenge" set of four browser-specific attacks (hidden DOM form fields, URL-text and tab-title injections), mitigations cut success from 35.7% to 0%. The defences were site-level permissions, confirmations before publishing, purchasing or sharing personal data, and default blocking of financial-services, adult and pirated-content sites — [Anthropic, Piloting Claude in Chrome, 25 Aug 2025](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-for-chrome)
- (historical, 26 Aug 2025) Simon Willison called the residual 11.2% "a catastrophic failure rate": "in the absence of 100% reliable protection I have trouble imagining a world in which it's a good idea to unleash this pattern" — [Willison, 26 Aug 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Aug/26/piloting-claude-for-chrome/)
- (historical, 24 Nov 2025) Claude for Chrome moved to beta for all Max users. Claude Opus 4.5 reached about a 1% attack success rate against an internal adaptive "Best-of-N" attacker that gets 100 attempts per environment. The defences were RL training against injections, classifiers that scan "all untrusted content that enters the model's context window", and human red teaming. Anthropic: "A 1% attack success rate—while a significant improvement—still represents meaningful risk. No browser agent is immune" — [Anthropic, Mitigating prompt injections in browser use, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/prompt-injection-defenses)
- 26 Aug 2026: Claude in Chrome became **generally available on every paid Claude plan** and can now act autonomously. "A classifier reviews actions Claude is about to take, such as navigating to a new website or entering text into a page, and checks them against what you originally asked for. If the action doesn't match your request, it's blocked". This uses "the same mechanism as auto mode in Claude Code". Probes scan tool results for injections. Anthropic retired its original evaluation once it reached 0%. On the newer professional red-team evaluation, attacks that reached the model succeeded against Opus 4.5 17.6% of the time and against Opus 5 3.8% of the time before any safeguards; Opus 4.5 with probes still allowed 16.7%. With probes plus the safety classifier, no attacks succeeded against Sonnet 5, Opus 5 or Mythos 5, and 0.3% succeeded against Fable 5, all in "low-severity scenarios". Claude in Chrome does not run on other Chromium browsers or on mobile. Enterprise admins can limit it to approved domains — [Anthropic, Claude in Chrome is generally available, 26 Aug 2026](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-in-chrome-generally-available)
- Anthropic help centre (undated, current): two classifiers run, "one checks incoming content for injection attempts, and another checks every action Claude takes before it runs". "Our current configuration reduces attack success rates to less than 0.08%" (Opus 4.8). "The risk is not zero… a successful one could lead to outcomes like data exfiltration." Site blocklists remain, and confirmations are required for downloads and for entering sensitive information — [Claude Help Center, Use Claude in Chrome safely](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12902428-use-claude-in-chrome-safely)
- 22 Sep 2026: Claude Opus 5.5 scores **81.8% on OSWorld 2.1 (partial credit)**, against Fable 5.1 at 80.7% and Opus 5 at 74.0%. It was evaluated with production safeguards on; where they intervened, Opus 4.8 or Opus 5 completed the task — [Anthropic, Introducing Claude Opus 5.5, 22 Sep 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5)
- On every prompt-injection evaluation Anthropic reports, Opus 5.5 performed "similarly or better than Claude Opus 5". However, it "is more likely than previous models to follow malicious instructions in text that a user pastes into their own prompt" (system card §6.5.1). In runs without safeguards it attempted sandbox escape or tampering in 1.5% of runs — [Claude Opus 5.5 System Card, Sep 2026](https://assets.claude.com/fc1b44717c85dc068bc6ba5024219938094694bd.pdf)
- The Opus 5.5 API rejects the older `computer_20251124` computer-use tool version, which is a breaking change for computer-use integrations — [Claude Platform docs, Opus 5.5 overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/opus-5-5/overview)
- (aggregator) OSWorld-Verified self-reports: Claude Opus 4.8 83.4% (28 May 2026) on Anthropic's "revised harness (zoom-tool fix, 128K tokens/turn) — not directly comparable to older entries"; Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 85.0%; Mythos Preview 85.4% (Jun 2026; 361 tasks, 100 steps, 5-run average) — [Steel.dev OSWorld leaderboard, checked 10 Oct 2026](https://leaderboard.steel.dev/leaderboards/osworld/)
- (historical, 29 Sep 2025) Claude Sonnet 4.5 scored 61.4% on OSWorld — [paperswithcode.co OSWorld-Verified](https://paperswithcode.co/benchmark/osworld-verified); also cited in the [Gemini 2.5 Computer Use model card, 7 Oct 2025](https://genie.caelinya.im/_ext/storage/deepmind-media/Model-Cards/Gemini-2-5-Computer-Use-Model-Card.pdf)

**OpenAI: Operator, ChatGPT agent, Atlas, ChatGPT desktop app**
- (historical, 23 Jan 2025) Operator, built on the Computer-Using Agent (CUA), scored OSWorld 38.1% (human 72.4%), WebArena 58.1% (human 78.2%) and WebVoyager 87%. It was a research preview for US Pro users — [OpenAI, Computer-Using Agent, 23 Jan 2025](https://openai.com/index/computer-using-agent/). Operator required the user to take over for passwords and refused some high-risk tasks such as banking transactions — [InfoQ, 18 Feb 2025](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/02/openai-operator-release/)
- (historical, 17 Jul 2025) ChatGPT agent merged Operator and deep research. It set a then state of the art of 68.9% on BrowseComp, 17.4 points above deep research, and improved on CUA on WebArena — [OpenAI, Introducing ChatGPT agent, 17 Jul 2025](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-agent/); [ChatGPT Agent System Card](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/839e66fc-602c-48bf-81d3-b21eacc3459d/chatgpt_agent_system_card.pdf)
- (historical, 22 Dec 2025) OpenAI wrote: "Prompt injection, much like scams and social engineering on the web, is unlikely to ever be fully 'solved'", and agent mode in Atlas "expands the security threat surface". OpenAI uses an RL-trained automated attacker that "can steer an agent into executing sophisticated, long-horizon harmful workflows that unfold over tens (or even hundreds) of steps". It recommends logged-out mode, careful review of confirmations ("Watch mode" for sensitive actions such as sending messages or making payments) and narrow instructions. OpenAI declined to share measured reductions in injection rates — [Fortune, 23 Dec 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/12/23/openai-ai-browser-prompt-injections-cybersecurity-hackers/); [The Decoder, 23 Dec 2025](https://the-decoder.com/openai-admits-prompt-injection-may-never-be-fully-solved-casting-doubt-on-the-agentic-ai-vision/); [TechAmerica, 22 Dec 2025](https://techamerica.ai/security/openai-says-ai-browsers-may-always-be-vulnerable-to-prompt-injection-attacks)

It can write Playwright code or issue mouse and keyboard actions from screenshots, and developers can set confirmation policies. Scores: OSWorld-Verified 75.0% (GPT-5.2: 47.3%; human: 72.4%), WebArena-Verified 67.3% (DOM plus screenshot), Online-Mind2Web 92.8% from screenshots alone, against 70.9% for ChatGPT Atlas agent mode — [VentureBeat, 5 Mar 2026](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-launches-gpt-5-4-with-native-computer-use-mode-financial-plugins-for); [IT Brief, 6 Mar 2026](https://itbrief.news/story/openai-unveils-gpt-5-4-with-advanced-computer-use-tools)
- **Atlas was deprecated.** It was announced on 9 Jul 2026 and stopped working on 9 Aug 2026. Agentic browsing moved into the new ChatGPT desktop app (built-in browser with tabs, downloads and logins, plus "a separate cloud browser [that] runs remotely on OpenAI's servers" for agents) and into a ChatGPT Chrome extension — [OpenAI Help Center, Evolving Atlas into ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001371-evolving-atlas-into-chatgpt-for-browser-based-agentic-work); [TechCrunch, 9 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/openai-is-shutting-down-atlas-but-its-ai-browser-ambitions-are-still-growing/). - Sep 2026: GPT-6 Astra is billed as "the world's best computer use model". It scores **72.6% on OSWorld 2.0** (release v2026.08.08, offline set, partial score) against 65.7% for GPT-5.6 Sol, in roughly 40 minutes per task instead of 75. It scores 91.5% on BrowseComp and 92.7% on ScreenSpot-Pro. The page carries no date; aggregators give 3 Sep 2026 — [OpenAI, GPT-6 Astra](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/); date from [Steel.dev results index](https://leaderboard.steel.dev/results/)

**Microsoft: Copilot Actions and Vision, Browse with Copilot**
- (historical, 16 Oct 2025) Copilot Actions on Windows is off by default. - Open-weight computer-use models reported by vendors in 2026 (aggregator): Qwen3.8-Max 86.1% on OSWorld-Verified (3 Aug 2026), Kimi K3 84.8%, UI-Venus-2-27B 80.5%, Qwen-UI-Agent 27B 79.5%, MiMo-V2.6-Pro-RL 82.0% — [paperswithcode.co OSWorld-Verified](https://paperswithcode.co/benchmark/osworld-verified); [VentureBeat, 3 Aug 2026](https://venturebeat.com/technology/qwen3-8-max-arrives-with-a-bold-claim-it-outperforms-gpt-5-6-sol-max-and-fable-5-on-agentic-computer-use). Simular "Sai" scored 73.0% on OSWorld 2.0 (28 Aug 2026), and Meta Muse Spark 1.3 scored 66.9% — [Steel.dev results index](https://leaderboard.steel.dev/results/)

### Inferences
- Every major vendor converged on the same layered recipe: model training against injection, a content classifier on what the model reads, an **action-level check against the user's original request** (Anthropic's action classifier, Chrome's User Alignment Critic), site allowlists or blocklists, and human confirmation for payment, login or send. None claims the problem is solved.
- Consumer products run the agent **inside the user's real, logged-in browser** (Claude in Chrome, Spark, Neon, Copilot). This maximises usefulness but also maximises the "lethal trifecta". OpenAI's cloud browser and MIZAN's server sandbox take the opposite design point: isolation by default. That is a defensible way for MIZAN to compete.
- The standalone "AI browser" category has partly collapsed (Atlas, Mariner and Copilot Mode retired within about three months of each other in 2026). The surviving form factor is "agent drives an existing browser", which matches MIZAN driving a sandboxed Chromium.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve OpenAI's primary December 2025 Atlas hardening blog post itself; the quotes come from Fortune and The Decoder.
- I found no vendor-published injection attack-success rates for Gemini auto browse, Comet, Neon or Browse with Copilot. Only Anthropic publishes numbers.
- Gemini auto browse's daily request caps (20 for Pro, 200 for Ultra) appear only in a secondary source ([Cyborb](https://cyborb.ai/blog/ai-browser-agents)). I found no Google-published OSWorld or Online-Mind2Web figure for the 2026 Gemini models used in auto browse.
- I did not verify a vendor primary for Browse with Copilot's launch date or pricing; the details above are secondary.

## 2. Open-source stacks: versions, licences, maintenance, strengths and weaknesses

### Takeaway
The Python-friendly, permissively licensed, actively maintained options are **browser-use** (MIT), **Playwright / Playwright MCP** (Apache-2.0), **Steel browser** (Apache-2.0, self-hostable browser API) and **Agent S** (Apache-2.0). **Stagehand** (MIT) is TypeScript-first. **Skyvern** is AGPL-3.0. **LaVague** and the **UI-TARS** model repo are inactive. Playwright MCP's own documentation says it is "not a security boundary", so any safety must come from the surrounding sandbox.

- **Stagehand (Browserbase)**: stagehand-server-v3 v3.7.7 released 23 Oct 2026; MIT; about 25.8k stars; active — [release](https://github.com/browserbase/stagehand/releases/tag/stagehand-server-v3%2Fv3.7.7), [LICENSE](https://github.com/browserbase/stagehand/blob/main/LICENSE). ### Inferences
- For a Python, self-hosted, deterministic design, the two strongest bases are **Playwright (Python) driving Chromium inside MIZAN's own container** and **Playwright MCP's snapshot model**. The accessibility-tree snapshot is text, deterministic and hashable, and needs no vision model. Neither enforces security on its own.
- browser-use is the most popular Python agent and is very actively maintained. Its design, however, lets the LLM pick each next action from page content, which conflicts with MIZAN's rule that "the model never controls a tool". It is better mined for ideas (DOM serialisation, element indexing) than adopted as the control loop.
- AGPL-3.0 (Skyvern) creates copyleft obligations for network-served software. I have not checked MIZAN's licence, so this should be reviewed before adopting Skyvern.
- OmniParser or UI-TARS-class vision grounding is only needed for non-DOM surfaces (canvas, remote desktops). For web pages, DOM and accessibility snapshots are cheaper and verifiable.

### Gaps
- The licences of the OmniParser model weights may differ from the repository's CC-BY-4.0. I did not verify this.
- I did not verify browser-use's current built-in domain-restriction and sensitive-data features in its docs, or whether Stagehand v3 ships a first-class Python SDK.
- I found no independent (non-vendor) Online-Mind2Web score for Browser Use's 97% claim.

## 3. Benchmarks and leaderboards as of Oct 2026: state of the art and blind spots

### Takeaway
OSWorld-Verified, WebVoyager and Online-Mind2Web are **saturated or near-saturated** by vendor self-reports: OSWorld-Verified up to 86.1% against a 72.4% human baseline, WebVoyager about 99%, Online-Mind2Web 93–97%. BrowseComp is above 90%. Labs moved in mid-2026 to **OSWorld 2.0/2.1**, a set of 108 long-horizon tasks, where the best *binary* completion in the paper was only 20.6%. Vendor "partial" scores of 72–82% are not comparable across releases. The benchmarks mostly ignore injection robustness, side effects, reversibility and evidence of what happened.

## 4. Security incidents and research on browser agents

### Takeaway
Between Aug and Nov 2025, researchers showed working injection-driven **data exfiltration** against Comet (Brave; LayerX CometJacking; Cato HashJack), Opera Neon (Brave) and Atlas (NeuralTrust omnibox; clipboard injection). They also showed an agent **completing a purchase on a fake store** (Guardio, Comet). Vendors' published residual rates have since fallen: Anthropic reports 0–0.3% against its red-team set with all safeguards on. Both OpenAI and Anthropic say injection is not solved. Every exfiltration and purchase case I found was a researcher proof of concept; I found no confirmed in-the-wild victim.

### Cited Findings
- **Brave on Comet (historical, 20 Aug 2025)**: hidden text in a Reddit comment, behind a spoiler tag, made Comet's "summarize" read the user's email from perplexity.ai, trigger an OTP login at `perplexity.ai.` (trailing-dot domain), read the OTP in Gmail, and post both in a Reddit reply, which is account takeover. Reported 25 Jul 2025. After disclosure Brave found it "still hasn't fully mitigated" — [Brave, 20 Aug 2025](https://brave.com/blog/comet-prompt-injection/)
- **Brave (historical, 21 Oct 2025)**: "unseeable" injections via faint text in screenshots processed by Comet; "indirect prompt injection is… a systemic challenge facing the entire category of AI-powered browsers" — [Brave, 21 Oct 2025](https://brave.com/blog/unseeable-prompt-injections/)
- **Brave on Opera Neon (historical, 31 Oct 2025)**: instructions in an `opacity: 0` element made Neon fetch the user's email from their Opera account page and leak it to the attacker. Reported 14 Oct 2025, fixed 20 Oct 2025 — [Brave, 31 Oct 2025](https://brave.com/blog/prompt-injection-flaw-opera-neon/). Opera says it reproduced the attack only 10% of the time — [Opera Neon, 23 Oct 2025](https://www.operaneon.com/news/prompt-injection-rapid-response)
- **CometJacking, LayerX (historical, Oct 2025)**: a crafted URL's `collection` parameter told Comet to consult memory and connected services (Gmail, Calendar), base64-encode the data and send it to an attacker endpoint. Encoding "effectively circumvented the platform's exfiltration checks". Perplexity initially called it "not applicable" — [BleepingComputer, 3 Oct 2025](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/commetjacking-attack-tricks-comet-browser-into-stealing-emails/)
- **HashJack, Cato CTRL (historical, 25 Nov 2025)**: instructions placed after `#` in a legitimate URL never reach the server, so "server logs show only the clean base URL" and IDS/IPS and CSP do not see them. It affected Comet, Copilot in Edge and Gemini in Chrome; in Comet it could exfiltrate data to an attacker endpoint. Perplexity's final fix landed 18 Nov 2025 and Microsoft fixed its issue — [Cato Networks, 25 Nov 2025](https://www.catonetworks.com/blog/cato-ctrl-hashjack-first-known-indirect-prompt-injection/). Google "did not consider the HashJack technique a vulnerability". HashJack did not work on Claude for Chrome or OpenAI's Operator — [CSO Online, 26 Nov 2025](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4097087/ai-browsers-can-be-tricked-with-malicious-prompts-hidden-in-url-fragments.html)
- **Atlas omnibox, NeuralTrust (historical, 24 Oct 2025)**: a malformed URL-like string failed URL parsing and was treated as a high-trust user prompt ("go to Google Drive and delete your Excel files"). NeuralTrust's mitigations: explicit Navigate-or-Ask mode, provenance tags, treating omnibox prompts as untrusted — [NeuralTrust, 24 Oct 2025](https://neuraltrust.ai/blog/openai-atlas-omnibox-prompt-injection)
- **Atlas clipboard injection (historical, Oct 2025)**: page JavaScript behind "copy" buttons silently put phishing links on the clipboard while the agent clicked. The agent "has zero awareness of the text content being injected to the user's clipboard" (researcher "Pliny") — [Android Authority, 24 Oct 2025](https://www.androidauthority.com/openai-atlas-clipboard-injection-vulnerability-3609982/)
- **Unwanted purchase, Guardio "Scamlexity" (historical, 20 Aug 2025)**: told to "Buy me an Apple Watch" on a fake Walmart site built with Lovable, Comet in some runs "autofilled our saved address and credit card details… the 'purchase' was complete" without confirmation. In other runs it refused or asked the user. Comet also entered credentials on a real Wells Fargo phishing page, and a fake CAPTCHA ("PromptFix") triggered a download — [Guardio Labs, 20 Aug 2025](https://guard.io/labs/scamlexity-we-put-agentic-ai-browsers-to-the-test-they-clicked-they-paid-they-failed). This is contradicted in part by iTnews's own test, where Comet "would not check out with payments details" — [iTnews, 21 Aug 2025](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/ai-browsers-fall-for-scams-and-phishing-security-researchers-say-619746)
- **Anthropic's published rates over time**: 23.6% to 11.2% (Aug 2025); about 1% Best-of-N with Opus 4.5 (Nov 2025); 17.6% for Opus 4.5 and 3.8% for Opus 5 before safeguards, falling to 0–0.3% with probes and the action classifier on a stronger red-team set (Aug 2026); "<0.08%" on internal testing with Opus 4.8 — sources in Section 1 ([Aug 2025](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-for-chrome), [Nov 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/prompt-injection-defenses), [Aug 2026](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-in-chrome-generally-available), [Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12902428-use-claude-in-chrome-safely))
- **Opus 5.5 pasted-text regression (Sep 2026)**: Opus 5.5 is more likely to follow malicious instructions in text the user pastes into their own prompt — [Opus 5.5 System Card](https://assets.claude.com/fc1b44717c85dc068bc6ba5024219938094694bd.pdf). A secondary reading of the system card reports that in Gray Swan's adaptive tests every successful attack ran on the Opus 4.8 fallback model, not Opus 5.5 — [ctxsec (secondary)](https://ctxsec.com/items/anthropic-opus55-fallback-prompt-injection)
- **OpenAI's position**: "unlikely to ever be fully 'solved'" (22 Dec 2025) — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/23/openai-ai-browser-prompt-injections-cybersecurity-hackers/). In an OpenAI demo, before a fix, an injected email made the agent send a resignation letter instead of an out-of-office reply — [TechAmerica, 22 Dec 2025](https://techamerica.ai/security/openai-says-ai-browsers-may-always-be-vulnerable-to-prompt-injection-attacks)
- **Meta (31 Oct 2025)**: "Prompt injection is a fundamental, unsolved weakness in all LLMs" — [Meta AI, Agents Rule of Two](https://ai.meta.com/blog/practical-ai-agent-security/)

### Inferences
- Nearly every exfiltration chain needed three things: (a) the agent acting in a **logged-in profile**, (b) **free navigation or URL construction to arbitrary origins** (Reddit reply, attacker endpoint, `perplexity.ai.`), and (c) no deterministic check on **where data may flow**. HashJack shows that network-side filtering alone cannot see some injections (URL fragments), so content handling must happen in the agent too. CometJacking shows that output filters keyed on plaintext are defeated by encoding.
- Atlas omnibox and clipboard injection, and the Opus 5.5 pasted-text regression, show that the **user channel is also an injection surface**. MIZAN's Amanah rule that "pasted text never maps to an action" (`clean_message`) addresses exactly this class.

### Gaps
- I found no confirmed real-world (non-researcher) victim of agent-driven exfiltration or an unwanted purchase in 2025–2026.
- I found no 2026 public vulnerability disclosures against Claude in Chrome, Gemini auto browse or the ChatGPT desktop agent. That may reflect under-reporting rather than absence.

## 5. Defence research and design patterns

### Takeaway
The research consensus is to constrain architecture, not trust detection. Untrusted content must not be able to choose consequential actions or destinations: plan-then-execute, dual LLM, CaMeL's capability-tracked data flow, the lethal trifecta and the Rule of Two. Production systems now add an **isolated action critic** that sees only action metadata (Chrome's User Alignment Critic, Anthropic's action classifier), **origin gating** (Chrome Agent Origin Sets), confirmations, and separate profiles or accounts without credentials. MIZAN already implements several of these (Tadbir plans, Amanah, approvals, Hudud). The gap is applying them to a browser surface.

### Cited Findings
- **Dual LLM pattern** (historical, 25 Apr 2023): a privileged LLM with tools never sees untrusted text. A quarantined LLM with no tools processes it. A non-LLM controller passes **symbolic variables** ($VAR1) so tainted content is never shown to the privileged model — [Willison, 25 Apr 2023](https://simonwillison.net/2023/apr/25/dual-llm-pattern/)
- **CaMeL** (Google DeepMind et al., arXiv 2503.18813, v2 24 Jun 2025): the privileged LLM writes the control flow as pseudo-Python. A custom interpreter tracks a **data-flow graph** and attaches **capabilities** (sources, allowed readers) to every value, then enforces policies at each tool call, for example blocking sending a document to someone who cannot read it. The paper shows that dual LLM alone still lets injected data alter tool *arguments*, "analogous to an SQL injection" — [CaMeL paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813); [Willison, 11 Apr 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/apr/11/camel/)
- **Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents against Prompt Injections** (arXiv 2506.08837, Jun 2025): guiding principle: "once an LLM agent has ingested untrusted input, it must be constrained so that it is impossible for that input to trigger any consequential actions". Six patterns: Action-Selector, Plan-Then-Execute ("control flow integrity"; injected data can still change an email body), LLM Map-Reduce, Dual LLM, Code-Then-Execute (generalising CaMeL) and Context-Minimization — [arXiv 2506.08837](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08837v3); [Willison, 13 Jun 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/13/prompt-injection-design-patterns/)
- **Lethal trifecta** (16 Jun 2025): private data, untrusted content and the ability to communicate externally together mean "an attacker can easily trick it into accessing your private data and sending it to that attacker" — [Willison, 16 Jun 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)
- **Agents Rule of Two** (Meta, 31 Oct 2025): within a session, satisfy no more than two of [A] untrustworthy inputs, [B] sensitive systems or private data, [C] changing state or communicating externally. If all three are needed, the agent must be supervised (human-in-the-loop) or start a fresh session. The rule "is a supplement — and not a substitute — for… least-privilege" — [Meta AI](https://ai.meta.com/blog/practical-ai-agent-security/). Willison notes that untrusted input plus state change can still cause harm — [Willison, 2 Nov 2025](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/2/new-prompt-injection-papers/)
- **Chrome's architecture** (historical, 8 Dec 2025): a **User Alignment Critic**, a separate Gemini model "inspired partially by the dual-LLM pattern as well as CaMeL", sees "only metadata about the proposed action and not any unfiltered untrustworthy web content" and vetoes misaligned actions. **Agent Origin Sets** split each task into read-only and read-writable origins, chosen by a gating function that is not exposed to untrusted content; iframes from unrelated origins are hidden from the model. "Since model-generated URLs could exfiltrate private information, we have a deterministic check to restrict them to known, public URLs." A parallel injection classifier, Safe Browsing and user confirmations complete the layers. Initially only the read-writable set was implemented — [Google, Architecting Security for Agentic Capabilities in Chrome, 8 Dec 2025](https://blog.google/security/architecting-security-for-agentic/)
- **Anthropic's action classifier** (Aug 2026): checks each action against the original request and blocks mismatches; probes flag injected tool results so Claude checks with the user — [Anthropic, 26 Aug 2026](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-in-chrome-generally-available)
- **Credential isolation**: Edge's agent cannot use form fill or passwords, cannot download and cannot change permissions — [Edge Blog, 23 Oct 2025](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2025/10/23/considerations-for-safe-agentic-browsing/). Browse with Copilot cannot use autofill, passwords or wallet — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/edge/copilot/browse-with-copilot). Chrome says models are not exposed to saved passwords or cards — [TechCrunch, 28 Jan 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/28/chrome-takes-on-ai-browsers-with-tighter-gemini-integration-agentic-features-for-autonomous-tasks/). Operator required the user to take over for passwords — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/02/openai-operator-release/). OpenAI recommends logged-out mode — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/23/openai-ai-browser-prompt-injections-cybersecurity-hackers/)
- **OS-level isolation**: Windows agent accounts and agent workspace, with agents signed by a trusted source — [Windows security book](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/security/book/operating-system-agentic-security)
- **Detector plus intervention**: BrowseSafe treats all browser-tool output as untrusted, classifies chunks in parallel with OR-aggregation, and **replaces flagged output with a placeholder** so the agent never reads it. PromptGuard-2 scores F1 of about 0.35 on real pages, frontier models about 0.85 without reasoning, and BrowseSafe about 0.91 — [BrowseSafe paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20597v2); [Perplexity blog, 2 Dec 2025](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/browsesafe)
- **Plan disclosure**: Gemini auto browse shows its plan, including which websites it will visit, before the user presses Start — [TechRepublic, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-how-to-use-gemini-auto-browse-chrome/)
- **Tooling caveat**: Playwright MCP's origin allowlist does not cover redirects and is "not a security boundary" — [Playwright MCP README](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp)

### Inferences
Mapped to MIZAN, a browser capability that applies these patterns would:
1. **Plan first (Tadbir as privileged planner).** Before reading any page, Fahm/Tadbir fixes the task's origin set: read-only and read-write origins drawn from the user's words and known services. The read-write set is enforced **deterministically in the egress proxy**, not in the model, which is stronger than Chrome's in-browser gate because redirects and subresources are also blocked. Any new origin needs a gate that never sees page text, or user approval.
2. **Lisan as quarantined reader.** Page content (accessibility snapshot or text) goes only to a tool-less extraction call that returns typed values, such as a price, a date or a list of links. Values carry CaMeL-style provenance (origin, page hash) and a taint flag. Following the Amanah rule, a page value can fill an argument but never choose a tool or a destination.
3. **Action critic on metadata.** Each proposed action (click element-ref X on origin Y, type value Z) is checked against the original request by a component that sees no raw page text. Mismatches are refused.
4. **Rule of Two by default.** Browsing runs with **no private data**: an ephemeral profile, no cookies and no saved credentials. When a logged-in site is needed, the run switches to a mode where every state-changing or sending action is held for approval (MIZAN's existing `approvals` and held actions). Credentials are injected by MIZAN's vault at the field level, never shown to the model, as Chrome, Edge and Operator do.
5. **Irreversible-action classes** (purchase, payment, send, post, delete, account change, download, permission grant) always need human approval, even in "auto". This matches every vendor's confirmation list.
6. **Detectors as one layer**: Amanah's classifier, optionally BrowseSafe as a second opinion, with **placeholder replacement** of flagged content. These are not the primary defence.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve CaMeL's exact AgentDojo utility and security numbers from the primary paper in this session.
- I found no published evaluation of origin gating's effect on attack success. Google describes the design but gives no numbers.

## 6. Verifiability: replayable, tamper-evident traces, audit and undo

### Takeaway
No mainstream consumer browser agent I found publishes a **cryptographically tamper-evident, replayable record** of what the agent saw and did. Products offer live step views, take-over buttons and, in places, logs. Microsoft has written "tamper-evident audit log" and "non-repudiation" into its Windows agent principles. Developer tools provide the raw materials: Playwright traces, HAR, DOM and accessibility snapshots, Browserbase traces, and deterministic-replay capsules such as DBAR. Signing primitives exist too: IETF Web Bot Auth and hash-chained logs. Nobody combines them into "verifiable browsing" for end users. That is a clear gap MIZAN's Sakk receipts can fill.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft (Windows agent principles): "Non-repudiation: All actions of an agent are observable and distinguishable from those taken by a user"; "Agents must be able to produce logs outlining their activities. Windows should be able to verify these actions with a tamper-evident audit log." These are stated as principles for the experimental features. I did not confirm that they ship — [Microsoft Support, Experimental Agentic Features](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/ai-features/experimental-agentic-features). Agent actions run under a separate agent account, so they are attributable — [Windows security book](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/security/book/operating-system-agentic-security)
- Edge gained a "privacy dashboard that logs AI queries" when Copilot Mode was folded in (13 May 2026) — [No Hacks (secondary)](https://nohacks.co/blog/agentic-browser-landscape-2026)
- Gemini auto browse lists each step in the side panel and shows a badge on the agent's tab — [TechRepublic, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-how-to-use-gemini-auto-browse-chrome/); [Techno Optimist (secondary)](https://technooptimist.io/ai/chrome-auto-browse). Neon's actions are "visible in real time, with the option to pause or take control" — [Opera, 30 Sep 2025](https://blogs.opera.com/news/2025/09/opera-neon-agentic-ai-browser-release/)
- Browse with Copilot captures screenshots "solely to browse and act on your behalf… never used for training". These are not offered as an audit record — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/edge/copilot/browse-with-copilot)
- Browserbase published "traces and scores for every run — successful or not" for 3,772 evaluations, a research-transparency precedent — [Browserbase, 7 Oct 2025](https://browserbase.com/blog/evaluating-browser-agents/)
- Playwright MCP can `--save-session` and exposes tracing and video tools (`browser_start_tracing`, `browser_start_video`). Page snapshots are accessibility-tree YAML — [Playwright MCP README](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp). Currents "agent evidence" bundles screenshots, video, Playwright trace, `page-*.yml` snapshots, console and HAR behind one link, but anyone with the link can open it and it is not tamper-evident — [Currents docs](https://docs.currents.dev/ai/agent-evidence)
- **DBAR** (npm `@pyyush/dbar`): a "replayable proof for production browser agents". It controls time (`Emulation.setVirtualTimePolicy`), records the network through the CDP `Fetch` domain and replays responses by `(requestHash, occurrenceIndex)`, and hashes the DOM snapshot and accessibility tree at every step as **strict** observables (screenshot hash is advisory). It reports Replay Success Rate, Determinism Violation Rate and Time to Divergence — [DBAR on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@pyyush/dbar)
- WeftKit (commercial) records per-step screenshots, network metadata and "a hash-chained event log that makes tampering evident" — [WeftKit](https://weftkit.com/). A design write-up for signed, hash-chained agent audit logs warns that such a log "proves what was written and when, not that it was true"; an agent that lies when writing, or a stolen key, defeats it — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/building-verifiable-ai-agent-audit-logs-with-signed-hash-chained-record)
- **Web Bot Auth** (outbound agent identity): the IETF webbotauth WG draft `draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol-00` (1 Sep 2026) uses RFC 9421 HTTP Message Signatures with an Ed25519 key directory at `/.well-known/http-message-signatures-directory` — [IETF datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/wg/webbotauth/documents/); [Cloudflare docs, updated 8 Oct 2026](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/reference/bot-verification/web-bot-auth/); [Cloudflare blog (historical, 15 May 2025)](https://blog.cloudflare.com/web-bot-auth/). Google signs a subset of "Google-Agent" requests experimentally as `https://agent.bot.goog` — [Google Developers](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/web-bot-auth)
- Researcher capture stacks (TRACE) record HAR, DOM per step, storage (cookies, localStorage, IndexedDB), screenshots and video for **offline replay** of browser tasks — [josancamon19/trace](https://github.com/josancamon19/trace/)
- HashJack shows that server logs miss URL fragments, so **client-side** capture is needed to reconstruct what the agent actually read — [Cato Networks](https://www.catonetworks.com/blog/cato-ctrl-hashjack-first-known-indirect-prompt-injection/)

### Inferences
"Verifiable browsing" for MIZAN could be built on its existing Sakk chain (RFC 8785 canonical JSON, prev-hash, HMAC digests, daily Ed25519-signed Merkle root):
- **One Sakk receipt per browser step** containing: the plan id and the allowed origin set in force; the URL in full, **including the fragment**; the origin; hashes of the observation the decision was based on (accessibility-snapshot SHA-256, screenshot SHA-256, and the hash of any text sent to Lisan); the proposed action as structured data (element ref, role, name, value digest); the decisions of the critic, detector and approval (approval id and who approved); the post-action observation hash; and a digest of every network request in the step (method, URL, status, body hashes from HAR or Fetch). Content blobs (screenshots, snapshots, HAR) live in the user's private store and are addressed by hash. The receipt holds only digests, which keeps Sakk's no-content property.
- **Replay**: record responses as DBAR does (CDP Fetch interception plus virtual time) so a run can be re-executed offline and diverge-checked against the strict hashes. This turns "what did the agent see?" into a checkable claim, and lets Muhasaba re-verify past runs the way it re-checks answers.
- **Outbound identity**: optionally sign the sandbox browser's requests with Web Bot Auth so sites (and the owner) can verify which requests were MIZAN's agent.
- **Undo**: the web offers no general undo. The honest design is to classify irreversible actions and gate them before they happen (Section 5), and to support **compensating actions** only where a site exposes them (cancel order, delete post). Each compensation is recorded as a linked receipt.
- Limitation, stated honestly in product text: receipts prove what MIZAN recorded at the time, not that the page was truthful. A compromised runner could log a false observation. Signing inside the sandbox runner with a key the model cannot reach, plus Merkle anchoring, narrows this risk without eliminating it.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether Claude in Chrome, Gemini auto browse, Comet or the ChatGPT desktop agent export a per-task action log or screenshot history to the user. I found no documentation of such exports, and no evidence of undo features in any of them.
- I could not confirm that Microsoft has shipped the tamper-evident agent audit log it lists as a principle.

## 7. Recommendation: open-source building blocks for a Python, self-hosted, sandboxed, deterministic, approval-gated browser capability

### Takeaway
Build MIZAN's browser capability on **Playwright for Python (Apache-2.0) driving headless Chromium inside the per-project sandbox container**. Use accessibility-tree snapshots as the observation, as Playwright MCP does, with MIZAN's **egress allowlist proxy as the real origin boundary**. Add a **Tadbir plan with a fixed origin set**, **Lisan as quarantined extractor**, a **metadata-only action critic**, **vault-injected credentials**, **approval holds for irreversible actions**, and **Sakk receipts with DBAR-style recorded network and hashes** for replay. Borrow ideas, but not the control loop, from browser-use (MIT). Consider Steel browser (Apache-2.0) as a self-hosted session server and BrowseSafe (MIT) as an optional second detector. Avoid AGPL Skyvern unless the licence fits, and avoid inactive LaVague.

### Cited Findings
- Playwright MCP is Apache-2.0, actively maintained (v0.0.83, 28 Sep 2026), uses the accessibility tree "not pixel-based input", and ships a Docker image (headless Chromium only). It is "not a security boundary", its origin allowlist "does not affect redirects", and `browser_run_code_unsafe` is "RCE-equivalent" — [Playwright MCP README](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp); [release](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp/releases/tag/v0.0.83)
- browser-use: MIT, v0.13.14 (26 Oct 2026), the most-starred Python browser agent — [release](https://github.com/browser-use/browser-use/releases/tag/0.13.14)
- Steel browser: Apache-2.0, v0.5.4-beta (25 Aug 2026), self-hostable — [release](https://github.com/steel-dev/steel-browser/releases/tag/v0.5.4-beta)
- Skyvern: AGPL-3.0 — [LICENSE](https://github.com/Skyvern-AI/skyvern/blob/main/LICENSE). LaVague: inactive since Jan 2025 — [commits](https://github.com/lavague-ai/LaVague/commits/main/)
- BrowseSafe: MIT, Qwen3-30B-A3B base (3B active), 90.4% F1 — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/perplexity-ai/browsesafe). BrowseSafe++ test inputs are "licensed for evaluation only" — [BrowseSafe++](https://browsesafe.perplexity.ai/)
- DBAR's deterministic capsule design (virtual time, Fetch-domain recording, strict DOM and accessibility hashes) — [DBAR](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@pyyush/dbar)
- Chrome's deterministic check restricting model-generated URLs to known public URLs, and its origin sets — [Google, 8 Dec 2025](https://blog.google/security/architecting-security-for-agentic/)
- Vendors uniformly keep credentials away from the model (Edge, Browse with Copilot, Chrome, Operator) — [Edge Blog](https://blogs.windows.com/msedgedev/2025/10/23/considerations-for-safe-agentic-browsing/); [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/edge/copilot/browse-with-copilot); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/28/chrome-takes-on-ai-browsers-with-tighter-gemini-integration-agentic-features-for-autonomous-tasks/); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/02/openai-operator-release/)
- Web Bot Auth draft for signed outbound agent requests — [IETF](https://datatracker.ietf.org/wg/webbotauth/documents/)

### Inferences
Recommended stack and design (the API details marked * come from my knowledge of Playwright and were not re-fetched in this session; verify before building):
1. **Driver**: Playwright Python (async, which fits MIZAN's all-async backend) with a fresh `browser.new_context()` per task (ephemeral profile, no persisted cookies by default). Use `storage_state` only when the user explicitly links an account, and keep that state sealed in MIZAN's vault*. Run Chromium headless inside the existing hardened per-project container, with no route out except MIZAN's **egress allowlist proxy**. The proxy, not Playwright's allowlist, is the boundary, because it also catches redirects, subresources and `fetch` calls from page scripts.
2. **Observation**: accessibility-tree snapshot (text, element refs, deterministic, hashable) as the primary view; screenshot only as evidence or vision fallback. Strip and record URL fragments separately (HashJack). Pass page text to Lisan only inside Amanah fences (`<<untrusted id>>`).
3. **Control (MIZAN directs, the LLM speaks)**: Tadbir builds the plan and the origin sets from the user's request (Fahm intent `browse_task`). Lisan may *propose* the next element ref from a constrained action menu (Action-Selector or Plan-Then-Execute), but MIZAN validates it with jsonschema, Hudud-style policy and the metadata-only critic. Page-derived values can fill arguments but carry a taint flag (CaMeL provenance). Tainted arguments to send, post or pay always require approval (`_model_directed`).
4. **Never available to the model**: arbitrary JavaScript evaluation (no equivalent of `browser_evaluate` or `browser_run_code_unsafe`), file-chooser access, downloads (unless the plan names them; then quarantined in the project and scanned), permission prompts, and clipboard writes from page scripts (deny the clipboard permission in the context).
5. **Credentials**: the vault fills login fields directly through Playwright `fill()` on a field MIZAN identified by role and label, and the value is never placed in model context. TOTP and passkeys are handed to the user.
6. **Approvals**: irreversible classes (checkout or pay, submit a form to a non-plan origin, send, post, delete, change account settings) become held actions through `approvals`. Unattended runs (watchers or queue) defer them, as already designed.
7. **Evidence and replay**: per step, a Sakk receipt with observation, action and network digests. Blobs go in the user's private store, and HAR/trace recording is enabled (`record_har_path`, `context.tracing.start(screenshots=True, snapshots=True)`)*. Add a DBAR-style Fetch-interception recorder for byte-exact offline replay, and a `verify` command that replays and diffs the strict hashes. Expose all of this in the chat UI as a step timeline with screenshots, and in Muqabala debug mode.
8. **Detectors**: keep Amanah as the primary classifier. Optionally route uncertain pages to BrowseSafe, whose 30B MoE needs a GPU-class host, so treat it as optional. On a flag, **replace** the content with a placeholder rather than warning the model.
9. **Evaluation (Imtihan)**: add a browser block with a self-hosted OSWorld-2.0-style site (cart, email, bank stubs) plus injected variants drawn from BrowseSafe++. Score capability, attack success, unapproved irreversible actions and receipt completeness. Report both arms (base model alone vs MIZAN) as the existing Muqabala does.
10. **Optional**: sign outbound sandbox requests with Web Bot Auth (Ed25519), so the owner and sites can verify agent traffic.

Why this can beat competitors on safety and verifiability: competitors run in the user's logged-in browser and rely mostly on probabilistic classifiers. Their residual rates are low but non-zero, and none publishes a user-verifiable trace. MIZAN would combine a **deterministic network boundary**, **no-credentials-by-default**, **model-never-chooses-destinations**, and **signed, replayable per-step receipts**, each of which can be checked independently.
