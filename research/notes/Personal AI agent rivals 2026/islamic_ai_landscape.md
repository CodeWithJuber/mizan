# Islamic and Muslim-focused AI assistants: landscape, documented problems, data sources and unmet needs (as of 27 October 2026)

Scope note: facts are stated as of 27 Oct 2026. Items older than 2025 are marked HISTORICAL with their date. "Undated page" means the page shows no publication date and was read on 27 Oct 2026. This brief reports what scholars and institutions said; it does not issue any ruling. Where a claim comes only from a product's own marketing (LinkedIn post, press release, app-store text), it is labelled "self-reported".

## 1. Products and their capabilities, launch dates and current status

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the field has split into (a) retrieval-grounded Q&A assistants (Ansari, Usul/Qaf, Fanar, AiDeen and a wave of 2026 launches) that cite sources but still let a general LLM write the answer; (b) Quran infrastructure for other AIs (Quran Foundation's quran.ai MCP server with "grounding rules"); (c) recitation AI that runs in the cloud (Tarteel); (d) official state services (Saudi Nusuk AI, Saudi/UAE/Egyptian fatwa digitisation); and (e) Islamic finance screeners (Zoya, Musaffa) that are being built into AI trading platforms. None of the products found is self-hosted, none publishes a per-claim provenance grade, and the main prayer apps (Muslim Pro, Athan) have no on-device or verifiable AI.

### Cited Findings

**Ansari (ansari.chat → askansari.ai), open source, by M. Waleed Kadous and team**
- Became generally available on 29 Feb 2024 (HISTORICAL). It had answered more than 10,000 questions by then. In one test, GPT-4 got 97% accuracy and GPT-3.5 got 77% — [Kadous, LinkedIn, 29 Feb 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/waleedkadous_ansari-chat-activity-7169107626226638848-0hgJ); [Kadous, Medium, 29 Feb 2024](https://waleedk.medium.com/what-ive-learned-building-a-real-ai-assistant-5d520b0e6a46)
- Ansari v2 launched on 14 Oct 2024 (HISTORICAL) — [Kadous, LinkedIn, 14 Oct 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/waleedkadous_presentation-of-dr-waleed-kadous-artificial-activity-7251672943993184257-ROvo)
- Docs (undated) say Ansari 3.0 used Claude 3.7 Sonnet "because of… its ability to make citations". Ansari 3.0 scored 100% on the BATIK assessment, and "65% of answers now contain citations to authentic Islamic sources" — [Ansari FAQ](https://docs.ansari.chat/faq/); [Ansari Validation](https://docs.ansari.chat/validation/)
- The team reviewed 2,500 user interactions and found 9 answers they were unsure of, mostly fiqh such as inheritance. Scholars judged Ansari right on 8 of the 9; the wrong one was a complex inheritance question. Users can type "I want to flag this" to get a human review — [Ansari capabilities, undated](https://docs.ansari.chat/capabilities/)
- A system paper reports more than 140,000 conversations in 25+ languages since June 2023 (about 290 a day as of March 2026), with about 65% of answers citing primary sources and about 84% positive satisfaction. Ansari runs on the web, mobile apps, WhatsApp, as an MCP server and as an Agent Skill. The base model is now Google Gemini on Vertex AI. v3 added "the evidence-based tafsir encyclopedia via Usul.ai" and an approximately 18,000-page fiqh encyclopedia. The paper claims Ansari tops the public IslamicMMLU leaderboard (self-reported) — [arXiv 2608.20390, submitted 30 Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20390); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20390). Note: the arXiv identifier (2608.x) does not match the stated submission date (30 Jun 2026); treat the date as uncertain.
- Agent Skill repo (MIT, created 1 Feb 2026). Its README says "The API requires no authentication and no API key" — [ansari-project/ansari-skill](https://github.com/ansari-project/ansari-skill); "headless mode" announcement — [Kadous, LinkedIn, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/waleedkadous_integrating-ansari-with-claude-skill-tutorial-activity-7444500345566900224-E4QJ)
- In an independent pilot evaluation, GPT-4o scored highest on Islamic Accuracy (3.93) and Citation (3.38). Ansari followed (3.68, 3.32) and Fanar lagged (2.76, 1.82). Ansari led qualitative pairwise wins (116/200). The authors conclude that models "still fall short in reliably producing accurate Islamic content and citations" — [arXiv 2510.24438 (Oct 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24438v1)

### Inferences
- Nearly every product keeps the LLM as the author of the answer and adds retrieval and citations around it. Ansari's own paper concludes "grounding is necessary but not sufficient". Even the best-run assistant cites primary sources in only about 65% of answers. An architecture in which the system, not the model, quotes the text and the model only phrases it (MIZAN's design) does not appear in any product reviewed.
- Quran Foundation's MCP server is the strongest Quran-grounding infrastructure available. However, it relies on the calling LLM to obey labelling rules (the model labels itself "Grounded") and logs queries and IPs on a hosted service. A self-hosted agent could use it for retrieval and still verify the quote and keep the user's query private.
- "Answers only from the Quran/Sahihayn" is now a common marketing claim (HAZBE, Aiman & Aisha, IslamIQ, Adhan Harmony). None of these products publishes a measured error rate or lets users verify that claim.
- Official bodies (Nusuk AI, Saudi Ifta, Al-Azhar Fatwa AI, UAE) are building AI that routes questions to official fatwas or to muftis. A personal agent that defers to these sources is likely to be welcomed rather than opposed by them.

## 2. Documented problems: misquotation and fabrication, AI-fatwa controversies and scholarly statements, privacy incidents

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed and shared-task evidence (2023–2026) shows that LLMs misquote ayahs and fabricate or blend hadith, and that fixing a wrong quote after the fact is much harder than spotting it. Users cannot tell the difference: in a blind test they preferred AI answers 81% of the time, even when experts found errors. Every major institution that has spoken (Al-Azhar, Egypt's Dar al-Ifta, the OIC's Islamic Fiqh Academy, the UAE Council for Fatwa, the Saudi Minister of Islamic Affairs, the Muslim World League, Jordan's Iftaa') allows AI as a research aid for scholars but rejects AI as a mufti. The 2020–21 location-data sales by Muslim prayer apps are the defining privacy incidents. No later comparable incident was found, but cloud logging of religious queries and recitations is widespread.

### Cited Findings

**Misquotation and fabrication studies**
- HISTORICAL (2023): researchers sampled 20 responses from ChatGPT-3.5 and Bard to "What does the Quran have to say about misinformation?". "Only two out of the twenty responses… had no mistakes in attempted Quranic references." Errors included word misordering, wrong citations, irrelevant verses and "sheer confabulations" in both English and Arabic. The authors "do not find LLMs fit to adequately return references to Quranic passages" — [Bhojani & Schwarting, Theology and Science, 2 Oct 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/14746700.2023.2255944)
- IslamicEval 2025 (ArabicNLP, 8–9 Nov 2025) was the first shared task on hallucinated Quran and hadith in LLM output. Six LLMs answered Qur'an QA 2023 questions (n=251), and 13 teams took part. Findings: "detecting the textual boundaries… is substantially easier than correcting them". Models do "consistently better on Qur'anic verses than on Hadiths". The organisers recommend that "hallucination prevention should occur during generation, e.g. via RAG… instead of post-hoc correction" — [IslamicEval 2025 overview (ACL Anthology)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.67/); [PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.67.pdf)
  - The HUMAIN team scored 87.2% F1 on detection, 86.1% on validation and only 68.2% on correction — [HUMAIN at IslamicEval 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.70.pdf). BurhanAI scored 66.56% on correction — [BurhanAI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.69). The TCE team found that "LLM verifiers are occasionally deceived by similar Islamic content generated by… language models" and that removing diacritics cuts accuracy by 2–3 points — [TCE at IslamicEval 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.71.pdf)
- IslamicEval 2026 (16 teams, 4 subtasks) adds detection of verse numbers and narrators, plus whether each citation is relevant to the question — [IslamicEval 2026 overview](https://aclanthology.org/2026.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.125/). The best reported correction accuracy was 78.02% (Yaqeen) — [Yaqeen at IslamicEval 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.129/)
- Hadith recall from a reference alone: 3,000 references, 5 small open models (including Arabic-specialised Jais-7B and ALLaM-7B), 30,000 responses. "0% of 30,000 responses achieved both high lexical and semantic fidelity" (dataset card, not peer-reviewed) — [HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/dayamnadeem/hadith-hallucination-benchmark)
- A comparison of GPT, Gemini, Claude and LLaMA classified hadith errors as "fabricated hadith, incorrect attribution, hybrid hadith, and distortion of meaning", with different risk profiles per model — [Bakir & Elmunsyah, IJRSI, 30 Dec 2025](https://doi.org/10.51244/ijrsi.2025.12120025)
- IslamicMMLU (10,013 MCQs across 26 LLMs): average accuracy ranged from 39.8% to 93.8%, and the Quran track from 32.4% to 99.3%. A "madhab bias detection task" revealed "variable school-of-thought preferences across models" — [arXiv 2603.23750 (v2, 3 Apr 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23750v2)
- FiqhQA tested rulings by the four Sunni schools in Arabic and English. GPT-4o was most accurate, while "Gemini and Fanar demonstrate superior abstention behavior". "All models exhibit a performance drop in Arabic" — [Atif et al., AIES, 15 Oct 2025](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/36543)
- QIAS 2025 (Islamic inheritance and knowledge): the best inheritance system reached 97.2%, a fine-tuned Qwen3-4B. Open-source Arabic LLMs stayed "below 40%". Teams report "persistent difficulties with tasheeh" and with "ʿawl and ḥajb" — [QIAS 2025 overview PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.117.pdf)
- IslamicFaithQA (3,810 bilingual generative items) measures hallucination and abstention. "Agentic RAG yields the largest gains beyond standard RAG" — [arXiv 2601.07528, 12 Jan 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07528)
- User versus expert judgement: in blind comparisons, Muslim users preferred AI answers 81.3% of the time. User–expert agreement on which answers needed improvement was κ = 0.143 (majority of experts) or 0.061 (any expert). Every participant nonetheless said they prefer scholar answers, and many asked for "scholarly oversight" — [Alam, Abdulhai & Salehi, FAccT, 23 Jun 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3715275.3732162)
- In emotionally charged queries, "Warmth and affirmation mask substantive errors, including jurisprudential mistakes", and removing affective cues alone does not fix the quality — [CHI 2026 BiAlign workshop paper](https://bialign-workshop.github.io/2026chi-pdfs/6.pdf)

### Inferences
- The shared-task evidence directly supports MIZAN's design: quoting from the source during generation beats correcting afterwards, and correction tops out around 66–78% even for specialist systems. Replacing a quote with the stored source text (MIZAN's Sidq) avoids the hard "repair" step entirely, as long as the intended verse or hadith is identified correctly.
- Hadith is the weaker area (larger, more varied corpora). An agent that refuses to state hadith text it cannot match verbatim in a graded collection addresses the most documented failure.
- The near-unanimous institutional line is: AI may research, compile and present opinions, but must not issue the ruling, and a human mufti must decide. A product that routes ruling questions to people, and shows sources rather than verdicts, matches every statement found. The IIFA's conditions (protection of information, documentation, transparency) map to self-hosting, provenance and receipts.
- Users' inability to spot errors (κ ≈ 0.06–0.14) means visible provenance per claim has value beyond trust signalling: it is the only cue users have.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study reports a single "percentage of LLM answers that misquote the Quran" for 2025–2026 frontier models in open-ended chat. IslamicEval's overview gives task scores rather than base-rate hallucination percentages in the text read here.
- No formal Saudi Council of Senior Scholars resolution on AI fatwas was found, only the Minister's remarks (May 2026). Al-Azhar's announced AI-ethics document had not been found as published.
- No privacy incident involving an Islamic AI chatbot (2022–2026) was found. This is absence of evidence from this search, not proof that none occurred.

## 3. Authoritative data sources and APIs, with licences

### Takeaway
Quran text is well served by open, verbatim-only sources (Tanzil, CC BY 3.0, "changing it is not allowed") and a GPL morphology corpus. Quran.com content is now behind OAuth2 with approval and audit rights. Hadith is the weak spot: Sunnah.com's API is key-gated and partial. The convenient open datasets are either public-domain community compilations with multiple graders (fawazahmed0, Unlicense) or scraped copies of Sunnah.com under uncertain or non-commercial licences. Prayer times and Umm al-Qura dates have mature MIT libraries. Moon-sighting data exists, but mostly as observation reports or maps rather than open APIs.

### Cited Findings
- **Tanzil Quran text**: Creative Commons Attribution 3.0. "Permission is granted to copy and distribute verbatim copies of this text, but CHANGING IT IS NOT ALLOWED". Attribution and a link to tanzil.net are required, and the notice must appear in all copies — [Tanzil text licence](https://tanzil.net/docs/text_license)
- **Quranic Arabic Corpus** (morphology, syntax): released under the GNU GPL; the licence page carries the GPL v3 text — [corpus.quran.com/license.jsp](https://corpus.quran.com/license.jsp)
- **Quran Foundation Content API v4** (quran.com):
  - OAuth2 client credentials with `scope=content`; tokens last 3,600 s; a backend-only secret.
  - The pre-live environment holds only Al-Fatihah and Al-Baqarah; the full dataset needs approved production access.
  - Qira'at endpoints require a separate scope.
  - "Please disable any automatic browser-translation features" when displaying translations.

  Sources: [QF quickstart](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/docs/quickstart/); [Content APIs v4](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/docs/content_apis_versioned/4.0.0/content-apis/). Developer Terms (last updated 11 Oct 2026) require a public privacy policy and breach reports within 24 hours, and let QF monitor and audit — [QF Developer Terms](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/legal/developer-terms/)
- **quran.ai MCP**: a hosted remote MCP at `https://mcp.quran.ai` with grounding rules and a nonce. Logging is described above — [GitHub quran/quran-mcp](https://github.com/quran/quran-mcp); [privacy](https://mcp.quran.ai/privacy)
- **Sunnah.com API**: needs an API key requested via GitHub issue; it covers "a portion of our data", and an offline dump is "not available yet" — [sunnah.com/developers](https://sunnah.com/developers). Docs: sunnah.stoplight.io — [GitHub sunnah-com/api](https://github.com/sunnah-com/api)
- **fawazahmed0 Hadith API**: The Unlicense; "Multiple Languages and Multiple Grades"; served from the jsDelivr CDN, "No Rate limits" — [GitHub hadith-api](https://github.com/fawazahmed0/hadith-api). The HF mirror has per-grader columns (Al-Albani, Shuaib al-Arnaut, Zubair Ali Zai, Ahmad Shakir, Bashar Awad Maarouf, and others), so the graders' different verdicts are visible — [HF fawazahmed0/hadith-data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fawazahmed0/hadith-data)
- **Sunnah.com-derived datasets**:
  - freococo/sunnah_dataset: 39,318 hadiths with a `gradings` field naming the grader, under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial) — [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/freococo/sunnah_dataset)
  - meeAtif/hadith_datasets: tagged MIT — [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/meeAtif/hadith_datasets)
  - CheeseWithSauce/HadithsJSONFormat: MIT, "taken from sunnah.com" — [GitHub](https://github.com/CheeseWithSauce/HadithsJSONFormat)
- **Thaqalayn** (Shia collections): community API code under GPL-3.0, data repo CC0 — [ThaqalaynAPI](https://github.com/MohammedArab1/ThaqalaynAPI); [ThaqalaynData](https://github.com/narmafraz/ThaqalaynData)
- **Evaluation data**: IslamicEval 2025 Subtask 1B verified data — [HF IFM/IslamicEval2025-Subtask1B_verified](https://huggingface.co/datasets/IFM/IslamicEval2025-Subtask1B_verified). The QIAS 2025 dataset and baseline are public — [QIAS PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.117.pdf). IqraEval recitation data and baseline models (wav2vec2, HuBERT, WavLM base) are on HF — [IqraEval](https://huggingface.co/IqraEval)
- **Tafsir sources**: via the Quran Foundation API or MCP (Ibn Kathir, al-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, al-Sa'di and others) — [quran-mcp](https://github.com/quran/quran-mcp). Ansari uses an "evidence-based tafsir encyclopedia via Usul.ai" — [arXiv 2608.20390](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20390)
- **Prayer times**:
  - adhanpy: MIT, "An offline library calculating prayer times", a port of batoulapps/adhan-java with no runtime dependencies; methods include Moonsighting Committee — [PyPI adhanpy 1.0.5](https://pypi.org/project/adhanpy/)
  - PrayTimes.org: by Hamid Zarrabi-Zadeh — [praytimes.org](http://praytimes.org/). It is used as the on-device engine in Namazly, which offers 16 methods, Hanafi/standard Asr and high-latitude rules — [i-muslim Namazly](https://i-muslim.com/en/apps/prayer)
- **Hijri / Umm al-Qura**: hijridate (formerly hijri-converter), MIT, v2.6.0 (6 Jan 2026). It is based on archived Umm al-Qura publications and KACST data and covers 1343–1500 AH. Its limits section: "Not intended for religious purposes where lunar crescent sighting is preferred" — [PyPI hijridate](https://pypi.org/project/hijridate/)
- **Moon sighting**:
  - ICOP (International Astronomical Center, since 1998) publishes verified monthly crescent observations and "The Official Beginning of the Month in Different Countries" — [astronomycenter.net ICOP](https://astronomycenter.net/icop.html?l=en)
  - HMNAO visibility maps (Yallop, NAO TN 69) via Moon Sighting UK; its Hijri Date API "stopped working" in Nov 2024, with a paid custom version for UK residents only — [Moon Sighting UK maps](https://www.moonsighting.org.uk/moon/visibility-maps-cat.html); [API notice](https://www.moonsighting.org.uk/moon/hijri-date-api-cat/hijri-date-api-usage.html)
  - TheShia.org offers a free moon-visibility API (Yallop/Shaukat criteria, "calibrated against moonsighting.com") — [theshia.org API](https://theshia.org/en/api/moon-visibility/)
- **Fanar API**: access on request; models on HF (Fanar 1.0/2.0) — [fanar.qa](https://fanar.qa/en)

### Inferences
- MIZAN's ayah search over Arabic, transliteration and translations, plus its verbatim quoting, fits Tanzil's "verbatim only" licence well. Translations need care: many are separately copyrighted, and QF warns that re-translation corrupts vetted meaning.
- Hadith grading is not a single fact. The fawazahmed0 data carries several graders whose verdicts sometimes differ (for example Sahih vs Da'if for the same narration). An agent should show who graded what rather than one label. This also means MIZAN's use of sahih/hasan/da'if/mawdu' for *claim provenance* could be confused with *hadith authenticity* grades. Separating the two visually, for example "provenance: sahih" vs "hadith grade (al-Albani): sahih", would avoid that.
- Hijri dates in practice depend on local sighting decisions. hijridate itself says it is not for that purpose, so a "Hijri date" feature should state its basis (Umm al-Qura vs local committee) and allow a manual offset, as Namazly does.
- Sunnah.com-scraped datasets labelled MIT or CC0 may not hold the rights to the translations they redistribute. A self-hosted product should prefer the Unlicense compilation or an explicit Sunnah.com key or dump.

### Gaps
- The PrayTimes.org licence could not be confirmed from the page fetched; it was not shown.
- The Quran Foundation production terms on caching and storing content (for offline or self-hosted use) were not found in the excerpts read.
- No official machine-readable API from Saudi KACST/Umm al-Qura, Diyanet or JAKIM for month start dates was found.
- Translation copyright status per edition (Saheeh International, Jalandhry, Hindi) was not checked.

## 4. Unmet needs Muslim users and scholars describe

### Takeaway
The recurring needs are:
- answers whose quotes and citations can be checked;
- human scholarly authority kept in the loop, especially for family law, inheritance and Hajj;
- awareness of madhhab differences rather than one flattened answer;
- languages beyond English;
- recitation feedback that covers tajweed and does not harvest audio;
- worship-aware daily tools (salah, fasting, zakat, Hajj);
- halal and finance checks that state their methodology;
- privacy treated as an amanah;
- a tone that is kind but does not hide errors behind warmth.

### Cited Findings
- **Verified citations**: answers should cite "with citations attached for verification" (Ansari), yet only about 65% do — [arXiv 2608.20390](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20390). Evaluators found "models still fall short in reliably producing accurate Islamic content and citations" — [arXiv 2510.24438](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24438v1). In a study of five chatbots, none reached "Trusted Educational Output" — [Wahid 2025](https://doi.org/10.1163/27732363-20250003)
- **Scholarly oversight**: users "stressed the importance of scholarly oversight in curating or supervising AI-generated religious material" — [FAccT 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3715275.3732162). Al-Azhar's Fatwa AI keeps "the human mufti" verifying — [Al-Azhar, 13 Aug 2025](https://azhar.eg/%D8%B1%D8%A4%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B5%D8%B1-2030/ArtMID/16586/ArticleID/95089/). - **Madhhab awareness**: Dar al-Ifta warns that AI "may overlook a difference between schools" — [Dar al-Ifta](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/article/details/12325/resolving-family-issues-the-dilemma-of-ai-generated-fatwas). ### Inferences
- The strongest unmet need is not "more answers" but answers whose authority is visible and bounded: what the source says verbatim, who graded it, which school holds what, and where a human must decide.
- Tone and accuracy pull against each other in emotional queries. A design where the language model supplies warmth while the system supplies the facts, and blocks unsupported claims, is a direct response to the CHI 2026 finding.
- Privacy expectations are shaped by the 2020–21 scandals. Self-hosting is a differentiator that can be explained in one sentence to Muslim users.

### Gaps
- No large representative survey (for example by ISPU, Pew or Yaqeen Institute) of Muslim attitudes to Islamic AI in 2025–2026 was found. The user-needs evidence comes from small studies (FAccT; a student survey of n=348) and from product signals.
- There is little direct evidence of user needs for Bengali, Malay or Turkish Islamic AI beyond product launches.
- No study was found measuring how often AI halal scanners are wrong.

## 5. Concrete feature ideas no product has shipped, grounded in the evidence

### Takeaway
Of the seven candidate features, two are already partly shipped by others: prayer-time do-not-disturb (several apps) and madhhab-aware summaries (AI summaries in halal scanners; single-madhhab bots). The clearest open gaps, where no reviewed product ships anything comparable, are:
- (1) a verifiable chain of citation per claim;
- (2) automatic hand-off of ruling questions to a human scholar;
- (3) zakat and inheritance computed and checked by the agent, with each madhhab assumption stated;
- (4) recitation checking that never leaves the user's device or server;
- (5) an agent that itself respects salah and Ramadan, holding its own messages and actions.

### Cited Findings and status of each idea
- **Provable chain of citation (isnad for answers)**:
  - Closest existing: quran.ai's Grounded/Partially/Ungrounded labels, self-declared by the calling LLM — [SKILL.md](https://github.com/quran/mcp.quran.ai/blob/master/skills/quran.ai-MCP-usage/SKILL.md); Usul's "Citations attached to every question" — [Usul](https://usul.ai/vision); Ask Ghamidi's timestamped video citations — [LinkedIn, 3 Oct 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/guddushani_askghamidi-ai-islam-activity-7512143129555423232-etCK)
  - Not found: any product that grades each claim's provenance or gives a verifiable receipt that the quoted text matches a named edition.
  - Grounding: the IIFA requires "honesty, proper documentation, and transparency" — [Res. 258](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/56035.html). IslamicEval shows LLM verifiers are fooled by plausible text — [TCE](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.71.pdf)
- **Madhhab-aware answers showing the differences with sources**:
  - Partly shipped as AI summaries: Etqa gives "An AI summary of the Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali and Ja'fari positions" with a "can be wrong" label — [etqaa.org](https://etqaa.org/). HalalSnap lets users choose a madhhab — [halalsnap.org](https://halalsnap.org/). AI Usthad is single-madhhab (Shafi'i) — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/muhammed-shamil-mt_aiusthad-flutterdev-firstlaunch-activity-7442785373669208064-dMkX). Al-Azhar's Fatwa AI drafts madhhab summaries, but for muftis only — [Al-Azhar](https://azhar.eg/%D8%B1%D8%A4%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B5%D8%B1-2030/ArtMID/16586/ArticleID/95089/)
  - Not found for consumers: per-school positions quoted verbatim from named fiqh works, with the user's own school remembered.
- **Automatic referral to a scholar for rulings**:
  - Existing: Ansari's manual "I want to flag this" leads to human review — [Ansari](https://docs.ansari.chat/capabilities/). Musaffa's separate "Ask a Shariah Scholar" — [Musaffa Academy](https://academy.musaffa.com/). Saudi Ifta automates routing of questions inside the institution — [Mediacorp/SPA, 24 Oct 2026](https://berita.mediacorp.sg/dunia/arab-saudi-manfaat-ai-pendigitalan-perkhidmatan-fatwa-seiring-visi-2030-1081326)
  - Not found: a consumer assistant that recognises a ruling question (for example talaq, inheritance shares, Hajj violations) and hands off, with the user's consent, a summary and sources to a chosen mufti or institution.

- **Zakat (and inheritance) calculator verified by the agent**:
  - Calculators exist (Musaffa — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/musaffa-halal-trading/id1614624968); Islamic Foundation, noting "fatwas regarding the permissibility of using either" gold or silver — [islamicfoundation.org](https://www.islamicfoundation.org/donate/zakat-calculator)).

Ansari's one scholar-confirmed error was an inheritance question — [Ansari](https://docs.ansari.chat/capabilities/)

### Inferences
- MIZAN's existing features map to the evidence as follows:
  - **Sidq truth gate (source replaces the model's quote)**: answers IslamicEval's "prevention during generation" recommendation and the 66–78% ceiling on post-hoc correction.
  - **Per-claim provenance grades plus Sakk receipts**: are the "provable chain of citation" no reviewed product has.
  - **Local prayer times, plus holding the agent's own notices during salah**: go beyond phone-level DND apps, because they cover the agent's actions and its Telegram/push channels.
  - **Self-hosting**: answers the 2020–21 scandals and the logging practices of the hosted MCP and Tarteel.
  - **Refusing to read Quranic text with speech synthesis**: has no counterpart found in competitors. No source was found either supporting or opposing it, so it should be framed as MIZAN's own policy rather than a cited norm.
- Priority candidates for MIZAN, in order of evidence strength:
  - (a) Scholar hand-off for ruling questions (family, inheritance, Hajj), with sources attached and the user's consent. Every institution found endorses this.
  - (b) Zakat and inheritance (fara'id) engines run as verified code, stating madhhab assumptions and price sources.
  - (c) Madhhab-difference answers that quote named fiqh works verbatim, with Isnad grades.
  - (d) Hadith grading shown per named muhaddith, kept visually separate from MIZAN's provenance grades.
  - (e) A Ramadan agent mode.
  - (f) On-device or self-hosted hifz checking with the IqraEval models.
  - Prayer-time DND should be marketed only as "the agent itself respects salah", not as a first.
- Positioning risk: MIZAN's provenance grades use hadith terms (sahih/hasan/da'if/mawdu'). Scholars may object to applying muhaddith terminology to machine claims, and users may confuse them with hadith authenticity. No source was found on this; it is an inference worth testing with scholars.

### Gaps
- An absence claim ("no product ships X") rests on this search of about 40 products and pages. Smaller apps (especially Indonesian, Turkish and Malay ones) may ship some of these features unnoticed.
- No source was found on whether scholars accept hadith-grading terms for AI provenance labels, or on Islamic rulings about synthesised speech reading the Quran.
- No product was found that measures and publishes its own Quran/hadith quotation error rate. Whether any competitor runs IslamicEval-style audits in production is unknown.
