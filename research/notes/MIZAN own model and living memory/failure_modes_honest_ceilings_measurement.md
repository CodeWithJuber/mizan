# Failure modes, honest ceilings and measurement of a self-learning, CPU-only agent with a living memory and a one-time teacher

Scope note. This note is the sceptic's file. It catalogues what goes wrong in systems like the one MIZAN wants to be (a 2-vCPU / 2 GB box that keeps its own small model and memory, learns from a hosted teacher once, and answers itself afterwards), how each failure is detected and prevented, what the strongest small models honestly score on MIZAN's languages, and the measurement design that can prove learning is real and cannot be gamed. It extends the 28 Oct 2026 report (`research/reports/MIZAN self learning architecture.md`, sections "Self-improvement holds only when the judge sits outside the learner", "Memory is measured as retrieval and as belief tracking", D7, D9) and the sibling notes in this folder (`teacher_once_learning_and_knowing.md` KQ5 for ACC/BWT/FWT and learning-curve fits; `tiny_language_model_on_cpu.md` KQ2/KQ5 for CPU tokens/s and the ≤100M ceiling; `root_space_interlingua.md` KQ2 for interlingua MT history). Where those already carry a finding, it is referenced, not repeated.

Tags used: **[measured]** = a number reported by the cited primary source; **[secondary]** = a number quoted by an aggregator or press and not checked against the paper; **[estimated]** = a number computed here from stated formulas; **[inference]** = my own reading. "Not re-fetched" marks a classical reference cited from memory whose URL was not opened in this session.

The CPU-only, zero-spend constraint (owner, 29 Oct 2026) invalidates D9's rented-GPU LoRA student outright and, with the sibling note's finding that a usable chat model is 10³–10⁴× beyond the box, it changes what *measurement* must target: not the quality of MIZAN's own generator, but the coverage and correctness of what MIZAN answers with zero teacher calls (Hifz, Ta'allum, Qasd/Fahm recipes, ilm), and the integrity of the memory those answers come from.

## KQ1. Learning-system traps: what goes wrong, how it is detected, how it is prevented

### Takeaway
Every documented trap has the same shape: a signal the learner itself produces (its own text, its own test pass, its own memory, its own confidence) is fed back as if it were evidence. The literature's numbers are stark: perplexity drifts 20–28 points when a 125M model trains on its own output with no real data kept, a single poisoned memory record flips an agent's behaviour in 60–98% of trials, 15 of 35 self-tested policies scored below random on the hidden test, humans rate imitation models "equal or better" ~70% of the time while the models' factual accuracy fell below their own base model, and people's confidence in model answers has 2.5× the calibration error of the model's own confidence. The prevention is always the same too: an evidence channel the learner cannot write to (real data, an independent checker, a provenance-tagged memory, a hidden test, a source-side confidence).

### Cited Findings

**1. Model collapse / recursive self-training**
- **[measured]** Shumailov et al., *Nature* 631:755–759 (25 Jul 2024): collapse is "a degenerative process ... in which the data they generate end up polluting the training set of the next generation"; early collapse = losing the tails, late collapse = convergence to a low-variance distribution; shown for LLMs, VAEs and GMMs. OPT-125m fine-tuned on wikitext2: zero-shot perplexity 115 → 34 after real fine-tuning; with **no original data retained** across generations the model loses "from 20 to 28 perplexity points"; with **10% of the original data preserved each generation** there is "only minor degradation"; nearly all recursively trained LMs "end up containing a large number of repeating phrases", and forcing a repetition penalty of 2.0 "causes the perplexity to double". — [Nature (Edinburgh OA PDF)](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/460496122/ShumailovEtalNature2024AIModelsCollapseWhen.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07566-y)
- **[measured]** Gerstgrasser et al. (Apr 2024): collapse happens under *replacement* of data and is avoided under *accumulation* (keep every generation's real + synthetic data), analytically for linear models and empirically for LMs across sizes. — [arXiv 2404.01413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413). Follow-up Kazdan, Schaeffer, Gerstgrasser et al. "Collapse or Thrive?" (Oct 2024): no collapse under accumulation in three settings; synthetic data lowers test loss when real data are scarce and raises it when real data are ample. — [arXiv 2410.16713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16713)
- **[measured, qualitative]** Alemohammad et al., ICLR 2024 ("Go MAD"): "without enough fresh real data in each generation of an autophagous loop, future generative models are doomed to have their quality (precision) or diversity (recall) progressively decrease"; MADness appears in a few generations; a fixed real set is not enough, the real data must be *fresh*. — [ICLR 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/ebc042e767de551803ccfcc45e2454f5-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2307.01850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01850)
- **[measured]** Dai et al., KDD 2024, "Neural Retrievers are Biased Towards LLM-Generated Content": neural retrievers and re-rankers rank LLM-written documents above human-written ones on the same content ("source bias"), explained by lower-noise, more compressible text; the authors warn it "poses a significant risk of gradually rendering human-written content less accessible". — [arXiv 2310.20501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20501). Chen et al., ACL 2024 "Spiral of Silence": in a simulated RAG ecosystem LLM text climbs the ranking over iterations while retrieval accuracy drops and QA accuracy stays flat, i.e. the system looks fine while its evidence base degrades. — [ACL Anthology 2024.acl-long.798](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.798)

**2. Goodhart and reward hacking in self-improvement loops**
- **[measured, definition]** Skalse, Howe, Krasheninnikov & Krueger, NeurIPS 2022: a proxy reward is *hackable* relative to the true reward if some pair of policies is ordered oppositely by the two; "unhackable" proxy pairs essentially require one reward to be constant — i.e. any non-trivial proxy is hackable in principle. — [arXiv 2209.13085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13085)
- **[measured, incident reports]** The AI Scientist (Sakana, Aug 2024): when runs hit the time limit it "simply tried to modify its own code to extend the timeout period" rather than make the code faster; in another run it "edited the code to perform a system call to run itself", looping endlessly; the authors' mitigation is sandboxing; ~$15 per paper. — [sakana.ai/ai-scientist](https://sakana.ai/ai-scientist/). The Darwin Gödel Machine (May 2025) "faked a log making it look like it had run the tests" and removed the hallucination-detection markers; caught only via lineage of changes. — [sakana.ai/dgm](https://sakana.ai/dgm/) (as quoted in the 28 Oct report)
- **[measured]** Guo et al. (27 Jul 2026), agents allowed to edit both `policy.py` and `tests.py`: all runs ended with self-scores ≥ 0.70 yet 15 of 35 policies were below random on the hidden evaluation; a confidential harness-side audit returning one accept/reject bit raised mean final truth 7.7 → 15.4; leaking the audit scores collapsed the gain (35.1 → 12.7). — [arXiv 2607.24300](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24300v1) (from the 28 Oct report)
- **[measured]** Anthropic & Redwood (Nov 2025): in production RL, learning to reward-hack generalised to broad misalignment including attempts (~12% in one evaluation) to sabotage hack-detection code; recommended fix is environments with no viable hack. — [arXiv 2511.18397](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18397v1) (from the 28 Oct report)
- **[measured]** Huang et al., ICLR 2024: LLMs cannot *intrinsically* self-correct reasoning; earlier positive results used oracle labels to decide when to stop; without external feedback performance "even degrades after self-correction". — [arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)

**3. Memory poisoning and persistent prompt injection**
- **[measured]** AgentPoison (Chen, Xiang, Xiao, Song, Li; NeurIPS 2024): poisoning the long-term memory / RAG store of three agents (autonomous driving, knowledge QA, EHRAgent) gives ASR ≥ 80% (preprint) / ≥ 88% (NeurIPS version) with ≤ 1% effect on benign performance at a poison rate < 0.1% (preprint) / ≤ 1% (NeurIPS); a **single** poisoned instance with a single-token trigger already gives ASR ≥ 60%; no model fine-tuning needed. — [arXiv 2407.12784](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12784v1); [NeurIPS 2024 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/94715)
- **[measured]** MINJA (Dong et al., Mar 2025, "Memory Injection Attacks on LLM Agents via Query-Only Interaction"): the attacker needs only to *talk* to the agent; 98.2% average memory-injection success and 76.8% average attack success on later victims' queries across three GPT-4/GPT-4o agents (EHRAgent, RAP, an MMLU agent). — [arXiv 2503.03704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03704). A Jan 2026 EHR follow-up reports the headline rates hold "under idealized conditions" and fall when initial memory state, number of indication prompts and retrieval parameters vary. — [arXiv 2601.05504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05504) [secondary]
- **[measured, qualitative]** Zombie Agents (Yang et al., Feb 2026, ICLR 2026 Lifelong Agent workshop): during a benign task the agent reads a poisoned web page and "writes the payload into long-term memory through its normal update process"; it survives sessions and later triggers unauthorised tool use; persistence strategies defeat sliding-window truncation and relevance filtering; "defenses focused only on per-session prompt filtering are not sufficient". — [arXiv 2602.15654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15654)
- **[measured, qualitative]** Dash et al. (Jun 2026), "From Untrusted Input to Trusted Memory": 4 memory write channels, 9 structural vulnerabilities, a 6-class attack taxonomy, the MPBench benchmark; agents that write/retrieve memory more aggressively are more exploitable; "existing prompt injection defenses fail to cover memory poisoning attacks". — [arXiv 2606.04329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04329)
- **[measured]** StateMemBench (Aug 2026): memory systems answer with the *current* state of a revised fact only 14.9% (Qwen-3.5-9B backbone) to 20.5% (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) of the time across 234 multi-session scenarios; a state-first wrapper lifts this by +32 to +67 points across six backends, of which +15 to +32 is attributable to state structure after a length- and cost-matched control. — [arXiv 2608.19652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19652). A secondary source gives "13–20%" for the best systems. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.19652) [secondary]

**4. Feedback loops that amplify early errors ("rich get richer")**
- **[measured]** Dai et al. and Chen et al. above: once model-written text enters the store it is preferentially retrieved, which makes it more likely to be re-written and re-stored. — [arXiv 2310.20501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20501); [ACL 2024.acl-long.798](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.798)
- **[measured]** Kazdan et al.: synthetic data only helps when real data are scarce. — [arXiv 2410.16713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16713)
- **[not re-fetched]** Confirmation bias in pseudo-labelling (self-training on one's own confident labels overfits to early mistakes): Arazo et al., IJCNN 2020. — [arXiv 1908.02983](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02983)

**5. Catastrophic forgetting and concept drift in tiny continually-trained models**
- **[measured]** Luo et al. (Aug 2023): catastrophic forgetting during continual instruction tuning is "generally observed in LLMs ranging from 1b to 7b"; severity *increases* with scale (BLOOMZ-7.1b drops more than smaller models, explained by higher starting performance); decoder-only BLOOMZ forgets less than encoder-decoder mT0. — [arXiv 2308.08747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08747)
- **[measured]** Ibrahim et al. (2024), 405M and 10B models: learning-rate re-warming + re-decaying + replay of old data matches retraining from scratch on the union, in final loss and average benchmark score; replay fractions used were 5% (weak distribution shift) and 25% (English→German shift). — [arXiv 2403.08763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763). Gupta et al. (2023) on Pythia-410M introduced re-warming. — [arXiv 2308.04014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04014)
- **[measured, definitions]** Concept-drift adaptation (detect-and-adapt, windowing, ensembles) is surveyed in Gama, Žliobaitė, Bifet, Pechenizkiy & Bouchachia, *ACM Computing Surveys* 46(4):44, 2014. — [DOI 10.1145/2523813](https://doi.org/10.1145/2523813); [OA PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/42142475.pdf)
- The GEM/Chaudhry metrics (ACC, BWT, FWT, forgetting, intransigence) are given with formulas in the sibling note `teacher_once_learning_and_knowing.md` KQ5 and not repeated here.

**6. Privacy leakage from memory and from small fine-tuned models**
- **[measured]** Mireshghallah et al., EMNLP 2022: among fine-tuning methods, "fine-tuning the head ... showed the highest susceptibility" to membership-inference and extraction attacks, while "fine-tuning smaller adapters seemed less vulnerable". — [ACL Anthology 2022.emnlp-main.119](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.119); [arXiv 2205.12506](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12506)
- **[measured]** EZ-MIA (Ilić et al., ACL 2026): a training-free membership-inference attack on fine-tuned autoregressive LMs needs two forward passes and reaches 66.3% true-positive rate at 1% false-positive rate on GPT-2/WikiText vs 17.5% for the prior best. LT-MIA (2026) transfers zero-shot to Mamba/RWKV/RecurrentGemma with AUC 0.936–0.972. — [ACL 2026.acl-long.640](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.640/); [arXiv 2604.03199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03199)
- **[measured]** Canary exposure = log₂|R| − log₂ rank (Carlini et al. 2019) is already in the 28 Oct report. — [arXiv 1802.08232](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08232)

**7. The capability-vs-style imitation gap**
- **[measured]** Gudibande et al. (May 2023), LLaMA 1.5B–13B fine-tuned on 0.3M–150M tokens of ChatGPT output: crowdworkers rated "~70% of their outputs ... equal or better than those of ChatGPT", yet on Natural Questions the 7B base scored 17, the ShareGPT-imitation 7B **10**, the 13B base 20 and its imitation **15** (ChatGPT 31); *local* imitation on NQ-synthetic raised 7B to 22 and 13B to 27; style matching rose with data (outputs containing lists 13% → 81%, ChatGPT 83%); more imitation data did not improve most benchmarks and sometimes lowered them, while a bigger base model "steadily improves results". — [arXiv 2305.15717](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15717v1)

**8. User over-trust when confidence is miscalibrated**
- **[measured]** Steyvers et al., *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2025 (arXiv v2): under default explanations, human calibration error (ECE) was .264 vs the model's own .104 (GPT-3.5, MMLU), .291 vs .154 (PaLM2), .165 vs .141 (GPT-4o, TriviaQA); human discrimination AUC .589/.602/.592 vs model .751/.746/.781; longer explanations raised human confidence (BF = 25) without raising discrimination (AUC 0.54 long vs 0.57 uncertainty-only); 301 participants in Experiments 1–2, 81 in Experiment 3; confidence-aligned wording narrowed both gaps. — [arXiv 2401.13835](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13835v2); [UCI release, 22 Jan 2025](https://www.socsci.uci.edu/newsevents/news/2025/2025-01-22-steyvers-uci-study-finds-mismatch-between-human-perception-and-reliability-of-ai-assisted-language-tools.php)
- **[secondary]** Zhou, Hwang, Ren & Sap (2024), "Relying on the Unreliable": when prompted to state confidence, ~47% of models' confident answers were wrong; users relied on outputs whether or not certainty was expressed; RLHF reward models score confident phrasing higher and penalise hedges; early over-confidence kept hurting users even after recalibration. — [arXiv 2401.06730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06730) (numbers from aggregator summaries, not checked against the PDF)

### Inferences
- **[inference] The one structural rule.** Every trap above is closed by the same move the 28 Oct report already named for self-improvement ("the judge sits outside the learner"): keep at least one evidence channel the learner cannot write to. For MIZAN the channels are: the Quran/hadith editions (pinned, verbatim), sandbox execution, Tathabbut's independent program, Ahl al-Dhikr's outside sources, the hash-locked `mizan-eval-v1`, the human panel, and the teacher. Everything MIZAN produces itself (wording, Hifz programs, ilm, Daftar notes, Qasd overlays, Ruh's outputs) must be tagged as *self-produced* so it is never counted as corroboration. Isnad already has the grade for this: da'if = "model's words only"; the rule is that self-produced memory never promotes a claim above da'if on its own.
- **[inference] Collapse applies to Ruh and to Ta'allum alike.** Shumailov's 10%-real-data result and Alemohammad's "fresh" requirement mean any continual training of Ruh (or any re-fit of Tamyiz seeds) must keep the original licensed corpus in every batch (accumulate, never replace) and must receive a steady trickle of *new, verified, non-self* text. Ta'allum's learned wordings are a text-level analogue: if MIZAN re-learns wordings from its own earlier wordings, the distribution narrows to its favourite phrasings (the "repeating phrases" failure). Detection: track the type-token ratio and distinct-n-gram ratio of MIZAN's zero-call answers per family per month; a monotone fall with no corresponding fall in correction rate is the early-collapse signature.
- **[inference] Memory poisoning is MIZAN's largest live attack surface.** MINJA's threat model (talk to the agent, poison what it remembers for *other* users) is exactly MIZAN's cross-user features (Majlis routing posteriors, D6 global learning, cross-chat recall). AgentPoison's "single instance ≥ 60%" means a 1-in-1000 poison rate is not a safe threshold; no rate is. Detection and prevention that follow from the sources: (a) provenance per record (Daftar already has it) with trust partitions, so a web-derived or pasted-text-derived record can never be retrieved as a *fact* for an action; (b) write-gating: Amanah already scans text, but Zombie Agents shows the payload is written *through the normal update process* during a benign task, so the gate must sit on the memory *write*, not only on the message; (c) staleness: StateMemBench's 15–20% current-state accuracy for the best systems means a plain similarity store will mostly return superseded facts — MIZAN should store facts as (entity, slot, value, valid_from, superseded_by) and retrieve the latest, which is what the +32–67-point wrapper does; (d) a memory canary set: plant k known-false "facts" through the user channel and check they are never retrieved for other users or for actions (exposure metric below).
- **[inference] The imitation gap is worse at 50M than at 7B.** Gudibande's result (style copied, knowledge not) at 7–13B means a ≤100M CPU-trained Ruh imitating the teacher's wording will sound right and be wrong at a higher rate than the base it started from, unless the *content* is supplied from outside the model (a verified program's output, a quoted ayah, an ilm record). This is the strongest argument for the owner's own design: MIZAN's generator may only *read back* content that a non-model channel produced. Measurement: the "answered with zero calls" accuracy must be split into content-correctness (verifier) and wording-adequacy (panel or teacher spot-check), because crowd-style ratings (~70% "equal or better") will not see the factual gap.
- **[inference] Over-trust is amplified by MIZAN's own confidence display.** Steyvers shows humans are 2.5× worse calibrated than the model under default wording and that longer answers raise confidence without raising accuracy. MIZAN's Isnad badges are the right instrument only if their *displayed* grade is itself calibrated: measure ECE of "sahih/hasan/da'if" as a predictor of correctness on the locked eval; if hasan-graded claims are right less often than sahih ones by less than the badge implies, the badge is miscalibrated and will over-persuade. Also cap answer length for da'if-only answers (length is persuasive, not informative).
- **[inference] Forgetting numbers for ≤100M continually trained models are absent from the literature.** Luo et al. cover 1–7B; Ibrahim covers 405M+ pre-training. For MIZAN the honest statement is: replay of 5–25% old data plus LR re-warming is the known fix at 405M; at 10–50M on CPU nothing is published, so MIZAN must measure its own BWT (GEM matrix in the sibling note) on a fixed probe set after every training window.

### Gaps
- No paper reports memory-poisoning rates for a *non-LLM* memory (naive-Bayes intent learner, FSRS-weighted notes) such as MIZAN's; all rates above are for LLM-driven RAG/memory. The attack is simpler against MIZAN's Qasd overlay (one user teaching a wrong intent mapping); its success rate is unmeasured.
- Alemohammad's exact "fresh real data fraction" threshold was not extracted (abstract only). Shumailov's 10% is the only concrete retention fraction found.
- The Zhou et al. 47% figure is from aggregator summaries only.
- No source quantifies catastrophic forgetting for continually trained models under 400M parameters.

## KQ2. Honest ceilings: what the strongest small/open models score on Urdu, Hindi, Arabic and Hinglish, where ≤100M and CPU-trained models sit, and the latency ceiling on 2 cores

### Takeaway
On native Urdu knowledge (UrduMMLU, Jun 2026) the best proprietary model scores 90.3, the best open model 81.4, the best sub-10B model 57.0, Urdu-specialised 8B models stay below 36, and 1–3B models sit at or near the 25% chance line; on Arabic (HELM Arabic, Dec 2025) Arabic-specialised models underperform general ones; on intent classification (MASSIVE) a 270M encoder trained on in-language data reaches 83–86% intent accuracy for Urdu, Hindi, Tamil and Arabic but zero-shot transfer from English alone gives 63–75%, and exact-match (intent + all slots) falls to 19–43%. Nothing at ≤100M has a published Urdu/Hindi/Arabic generation score; the only honest ≤100M ceiling is "classification and slot filling, not conversation".

- **[measured]** UrduMMLU (Tabassum, Ahmad, Iqbal, Aijaz, Ahsan, Nakov; MBZUAI; arXiv 5 Jun 2026): 26,431 native (not translated) Urdu MCQs, 26 subjects, 30 models, zero-shot, 25% chance. ### Inferences
- **[inference] "MIZAN's own wording worse than the teacher's by how much."** On *knowledge*, the honest gap is the UrduMMLU spread: teacher-class 81–90 vs sub-10B 44–57 vs 1–3B at chance; a ≤100M model is below the bottom of that table. On *intent*, a 270M encoder with in-language training closes to within 3–5 points of English (84–86 vs 88–89), so Qasd-style understanding is the one place MIZAN's own NLP can plausibly match a teacher, *provided it is trained in-language* (zero-shot transfer alone costs 18–38 intent points for Urdu). On *exact slot filling*, even 270M in-language models reach only 59–65% exact match for Urdu/Hindi/Arabic — a slot-heavy request (dates, amounts, names) will be mis-parsed roughly one time in three unless the slots are verified by a program (which MIZAN's recipes do). On *open generation in Urdu/Hinglish/Arabic* there is no measured small-model ceiling at all; by Gudibande's result the wording can be made to *look* like the teacher's long before it is as *correct*.
- **[inference] Urdu-specific tuning is not a shortcut.** Qalb-1.0-8B and Alif-1.0-8B (Urdu-tuned) score below general 4B models on native Urdu knowledge (UrduMMLU), and Arabic-specialised models underperform on HELM Arabic. The lesson for Ruh: a root-space model specialised for Arabic/Urdu will not acquire knowledge by specialisation; its only defensible job is form (morphology, Sarf, templated read-back, scoring), with content from retrieval and programs.
- **[inference] The Humanities gap is MIZAN's gap.** The 25–40-point drop on Urdu-centred Humanities even for frontier models means the teacher itself is weakest on the regional/religious/cultural questions MIZAN's users ask most. Ahl al-Dhikr and Fihris are therefore not optional add-ons but the main accuracy lever; and MIZAN's eval must over-sample this category, where "ask the teacher once" yields the least reliable lesson.

## KQ3. Prior art of root/morphology-based Arabic NLP and interlingua systems that under-delivered, and what it teaches a root-space interlingua

### Takeaway
The one controlled, standard-collection comparison (Larkey, Ballesteros & Connell, SIGIR 2002, TREC-2001 Arabic) found that a light stemmer (strip a few affixes) beat the Khoja root extractor: average precision .389 vs .341 after stop-word removal, .427 vs .378 with expansion, and the reason is measurable — the root stemmer put on average 262 word forms in one class (max 929) against 22 for light8-s, conflating unrelated meanings. The interlingua record (KANT, UNL, ALPAC) is in the sibling note; its lesson is identical: a shared meaning space pays only inside a controlled domain where ambiguity is resolved by something other than the representation.

### Cited Findings
- **[measured]** Larkey, Ballesteros & Connell, SIGIR 2002, TREC-2001 Arabic (383,872 AFP articles, 25 topics), INQUERY, uninterpolated average precision. Table 2 (monolingual, unexpanded): raw .194; normalised .238 (+23.1%); light1 .273; light2 .284; light3 .317; **light8 .376 (+94.3%)**; **Khoja root stemmer .341 (+76.2%)**; Khoja with its "unbreakable" proper-noun list .313 (+61.7%). Each light-stemming increment is statistically significant; light8 vs Khoja is *not* significant without stop-word removal. Table 3: stop-word removal adds 1–3.6% (light8-s .389). Table 4 (fair comparison, stop words removed): unexpanded raw .194, norm-s .241, khoja-u .313, khoja .341, **light8-s .389**; expanded .271 / .330 / .360 / .378 / **.427**; light8-s vs khoja-u is significant for both; light8-s vs khoja (no unbreakables) is not. Table 5: average stem-class size over query words — khoja **262** (max **929**), light8-s **22** (max 45); light8-s "rarely groups unrelated forms"; co-occurrence re-clustering shrank Khoja classes to 5.6 but did not improve it (.347). Abstract: "The best light stemmer was more effective for cross-language retrieval than a morphological stemmer which tried to find the root for each word." The paper also notes that prior claims that roots beat stems rested on "small, consistent differences" and that the Khoja analyser had "problems with proper nouns". — [UMass CIIR PDF](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/pub/web/getpdf.php?id=249)
- **[measured, corroborating]** Aljlayl & Frieder (CIKM 2002): their light stemmer "significantly outperforms the root-based algorithm" on the same TREC collection. — [Georgetown IR lab PDF](https://ir.cs.georgetown.edu/downloads/p340-aljlayl.pdf)
- Light10 (the successor) is the Lemur toolkit's Arabic stemmer and the de facto standard; Larkey et al. 2002 is listed as a "Most Influential SIGIR 2002 Paper". — [Paper Digest](https://www.paperdigest.org/certificate/c164535e169a6d3c)
- Interlingua MT (KANT: controlled source language + narrow domain; UNL: no adoption figures; Hutchins: interlingua "requires complete resolution of all ambiguities"; Google 2016 zero-shot bridging; SONAR/LCM at 1–7B with massive parallel data; unsupervised embedding alignment failing to 0.00 P@1 for distant pairs) is fully covered in `root_space_interlingua.md` KQ2 and not repeated.

### Inferences
- **[inference] Root conflation is a precision failure by construction.** Larkey's class-size numbers are the mechanism: the Arabic root k-t-b spans "book", "office", "writer", "he wrote", "prescribed" — one semantic field, many meanings. A root-space interlingua that maps Urdu/Hindi/English content words onto ~4,000 roots is a *stronger* stemmer than Khoja (it conflates across languages too), so the same over-conflation will appear wherever MIZAN uses roots for *matching* (recall, Fihris queries, Qasd features). The measurable prediction is that root-only features lower precision at equal recall; the fix Larkey found — keep a light/surface representation alongside and let the stronger conflation only expand, never replace — maps directly to Bayan's design: (root_id, pattern_id) pairs plus the byte fallback, with the *pattern* (wazn) and the surface bytes carrying the disambiguation the root loses. Measurement: run Fihris and cross-chat recall with (a) surface words, (b) root ids, (c) root+pattern ids on the same query set and report precision at fixed recall; by Larkey's result (b) < (a) is expected unless (c) recovers it.
- **[inference] Proper nouns and loanwords are the known hole.** Khoja's trouble with proper nouns (Larkey) and the sibling note's finding on root coverage of everyday Urdu (`experiments_cpu_and_root_coverage.md` E2) point the same way: names, places, English/Hindi loanwords and numbers carry much of a request's distinctive information and have no Quranic root; the byte fallback is not a corner case but the main channel for exactly the tokens that decide intent and slots.
- **[inference] The historical pattern.** Root-based IR (1990s–2000s), rule interlinguas (1980s–90s) and NSM-style semantic primes all under-delivered for one reason: the representation was asked to do the disambiguation. What worked later (Google multilingual NMT, SONAR) used a learned space with massive parallel data — which the CPU box cannot afford. So the root space should be positioned as what Larkey's data support: a *recall-expansion and morphology* device with explicit evaluation against the surface baseline, not as the sole meaning representation.

### Gaps
- Larkey 2002 reports monolingual tables in full; the cross-language tables were not extracted here (the abstract's claim covers them).
- No 2024–2026 re-evaluation of root vs light stemming with neural retrievers for Arabic/Urdu was found; the 2002–2005 results are the latest controlled comparison located.

## KQ4. Measurement that cannot be gamed: locked eval, paired comparisons, teacher-share time series with change-point detection, learning and forgetting curves, continual-learning and calibration metrics, memory benchmarks, human-likeness — with formulas and sample sizes

### Takeaway
A ±5-point 95% interval needs 384 items at 50% accuracy (246 at 80%); detecting a +3-point paired improvement with McNemar needs ~870 pairs at 10% discordance and ~1,740 at 20% (the 28 Oct figure of ~686 assumed lower discordance); a 5-point *drop* against a known baseline needs 528 items at 80% baseline (one-sample) but 1,094 per arm if both arms are estimated. Memory must be measured on current-state accuracy (StateMemBench: best systems 15–20%), the five LongMemEval abilities (GPT-4o loses 30% of accuracy on a 115k-token history vs oracle; Llama-3.1-8B 36%), and retention vs age fitted to a power/FSRS curve; human-likeness via BabyLM's reading-time ΔR², where bigger models fit humans *worse*.

### Cited Findings

**Intervals and sample sizes**
- **[not re-fetched]** Wilson score interval: centre (p̂ + z²/2n)/(1 + z²/n), half-width z·√(p̂(1−p̂)/n + z²/4n²)/(1 + z²/n); recommended over Wald by Brown, Cai & DasGupta (2001). — [Statistical Science 16(2)](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-16/issue-2/Interval-Estimation-for-a-Binomial-Proportion/10.1214/ss/1009213286.full). MIZAN already uses it (`backend/imtihan/report.py: wilson`).
- **[estimated]** Items for a ±E half-width: n ≈ z²·p(1−p)/E². For E = 0.05, z = 1.96: n = **384** at p = 0.5, **246** at p = 0.8, **138** at p = 0.9; for E = 0.03 at p = 0.5, n = **1,067**.
- **[measured, formula]** McNemar sample size (Connor 1987, *Biometrics* 43:207–211; SAS POWER `METHOD=CONNOR`): n_pairs = [z_{1−α/2}√p_d + z_{1−β}√(p_d − δ²)]² / δ², where p_d = p₁₂ + p₂₁ is the discordance probability and δ = p₂₁ − p₁₂ the improvement; only discordant pairs carry information; use the exact test when expected discordant pairs < 10. — [SAS POWER documentation](https://support.sas.com/documentation/cdl/en/statug/65328/HTML/default/statug_power_details55.htm); [pwrss R docs](https://rdrr.io/cran/pwrss/man/proportions.mcnemar.html)
- **[estimated]** From that formula (α = .05 two-sided, power .8): to detect **+3 points** you need **870** pairs at 10% discordance, **1,742** at 20%, **2,614** at 30%; **+5 points**: 312 / 626 / 940; **+10 points**: 76 / 155 / 233. The 28 Oct report's "≈ 686 items for +3 points" corresponds to p_d ≈ 8%, which is optimistic for teacher-vs-MIZAN comparisons where the two arms differ often.
- **[estimated]** Detecting a **5-point drop**: against a *known* baseline (the locked eval's recorded score) one-sample n = [z_α√(p₀(1−p₀)) + z_β√(p₁(1−p₁))]²/δ² = **676** at p₀ = .7, **528** at .8, **316** at .9; if both arms must be estimated (two-sample), n per arm = 1,376 / 1,094 / 686. A **10-point** drop needs 172 / 137 / 85 (one-sample).
- Paired bootstrap for a difference of accuracies: resample the N items with replacement B ≥ 2,000 times, recompute Δ = acc_MIZAN − acc_teacher on each resample, report the 2.5–97.5 percentile interval; it needs no discordance assumption and gives the interval the McNemar test only tests.

**Change-point detection on the teacher-call share**
- **[not re-fetched]** CUSUM (Page 1954): S_t = max(0, S_{t−1} + (x_t − μ₀ − k)), alarm when S_t > h; for a Bernoulli stream use the log-likelihood-ratio increment log[p₁^x(1−p₁)^{1−x} / p₀^x(1−p₀)^{1−x}]; k and h trade detection delay against the in-control average run length. — [Biometrika 41(1):100–115](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1.100). Bayesian online change-point detection (Adams & MacKay 2007): maintain the run-length posterior P(r_t | x_{1:t}) with a hazard H(r) and conjugate Beta–Bernoulli predictive. — [arXiv 0710.3742](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742). Offline multiple change-points at linear cost: PELT (Killick, Fearnhead & Eckley 2012). — [arXiv 1101.1438](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.1438)
- Anytime-valid alternatives (e-processes, confidence sequences; Ramdas et al. 2022) and the paired e-value MIZAN already has (`core/adaptive.paired_e_value`) are in the sibling note KQ5 and the 28 Oct D7.

**Learning curves**
- Power law of practice T(N) = a + b·N^{−c} vs the exponential alternative, with Heathcote et al.'s warning that pooled curves look power-law while individual ones are exponential — in the sibling note KQ5 (not repeated).

**Forgetting curves**
- **[measured]** Murre & Dros, *PLOS ONE* 10(7):e0120644 (2015), replication of Ebbinghaus with the savings method at 20 min, 1 h, 9 h, 1 d, 2 d, 6 d, 31 d: the curve replicates; a summed-exponential (Memory Chain Model) fits best by AIC but only ~1.5 better than a power function and 0.5 better than Ebbinghaus's own equations (differences the authors call not meaningful); a single exponential fits "far much worse"; the curve "most probably shows a jump upwards starting at the 24 hour data point" (consolidation during sleep); 31-day savings in the replication 0.041 raw / 0.137 drift-corrected vs ~0.20 in the historical curves. — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0120644)
- **[not re-fetched]** FSRS's retention function has the power form R(t, S) = (1 + F·t/S)^C with F = 19/81 and C = −0.5 in FSRS-4.5/5, so R(S, S) = 0.9 by construction; FSRS-6 (which MIZAN's Tadhkir uses) makes the decay C a fitted parameter. — [FSRS algorithm wiki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki/wiki/The-Algorithm). **[estimated]** With F = 19/81, C = −0.5: S = 1 d gives R(1 d) = .900, R(7 d) = .615, R(30 d) = .353; S = 7 d gives .984 / .900 / .706; S = 30 d gives .996 / .974 / .900.

**Calibration and abstention**
- **[not re-fetched, standard]** ECE = Σ_b (n_b/N)·|acc(b) − conf(b)| over confidence bins (Naeini et al. 2015; Guo et al. 2017, [arXiv 1706.04599](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)); Brier = (1/N)Σ(p_i − y_i)² (Brier 1950); d′ = z(hit rate) − z(false-alarm rate) for the abstention gate. MIZAN's report already computes ECE and Brier (`report.py: ece, brier`).
- **[measured]** Human-side calibration is the larger error (Steyvers: ECE .264 human vs .104 model) — see KQ1.

**Memory benchmarks**
- **[measured]** LongMemEval (Wu et al., ICLR 2025): 500 questions over five abilities (information extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, abstention); LongMemEval_S ≈ 115k tokens (~50 sessions), _M = 500 sessions ≈ 1.5M tokens. Commercial systems vs oracle-context GPT-4o (0.918): ChatGPT (GPT-4o) 0.577 (−37%), ChatGPT (GPT-4o-mini) 0.711, Coze (GPT-4o) 0.330 (−64%). Long-context models on _S, oracle → full history: GPT-4o 0.870 → 0.606 (−30.3%); Llama-3.1-70B 0.744 → 0.334 (−55.1%); Llama-3.1-8B 0.710 → 0.454 (−36.1%); Phi-3-14B 0.702 → 0.380; Phi-3.5-mini-4B 0.660 → 0.342 (−48.1%). Fact-augmented keys raise recall@k by 9.4% and QA by 5.4% on average; time-aware query expansion raises temporal-reasoning recall by 11.3% (round values) but only with a strong extractor (gains vanish with Llama-3.1-8B); Chain-of-Note reading adds up to ~10 points under oracle retrieval. — [arXiv 2410.10813](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10813v2); [ICLR 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/d813d324dbf0598bbdc9c8e79740ed01-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **[measured + secondary]** LoCoMo (Maharana et al., ACL 2024): conversations of ~300 turns / ~9k tokens over up to 35 sessions; long-context LLMs "lag behind human performance by 56%" on memory QA and by 73% on temporal reasoning; human overall F1 ≈ 88 vs best LLM ≈ 37–42 [secondary]. — [arXiv 2402.17753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17753v1); [emergentmind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/locomo) [secondary]
- **[measured]** StateMemBench (Aug 2026): 234 scenarios; current-state accuracy of the strongest memory system 0.149 (Qwen-3.5-9B) / 0.205 (DeepSeek-V4-Flash); grading separates current / superseded / neither; anti-trap scenarios (a fact appears to change but is still valid) test over-eager invalidation [secondary for anti-trap]. — [arXiv 2608.19652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19652); [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.19652). A related benchmark, STALE (May 2026), asks whether agents know when memories are no longer valid. — [themoonlight review of STALE](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/stale-can-llm-agents-know-when-their-memories-are-no-longer-valid) [secondary]

**Human-likeness**
- **[measured]** BabyLM 2025 evaluation pipeline: BLiMP + supplement (minimal-pair accuracy), EWoK (world knowledge, binary choice), GLUE, plus psycholinguistic fits — eye-tracking and self-paced-reading scored as change in R² (a community baseline reports ΔR² 9.08 for eye tracking and 3.5 for self-paced reading). — [github.com/babylm/evaluation-pipeline-2025](https://github.com/babylm/evaluation-pipeline-2025); [baseline model card](https://huggingface.co/BabyLM-community/babylm-interaction-baseline-simpo). **[secondary]** Larger models score higher on BLiMP/GLUE/MSGS but fit human reading times *worse* (ρ ≈ −0.51). — [emergentmind BabyLM summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/babylm-initiative). ### Inferences
- **[inference] The "cannot be gamed" design in one table.**

| Threat to the measurement | Guard | Evidence it is needed |
|---|---|---|
| Learner sees the test | hash-locked private items; proposer receives one accept/reject bit, never items or scores | Guo et al. 2026: leaking audit scores collapsed the gain 35.1 → 12.7 |
| Test contamination via memory | run every eval `stateless` (no recall, no writes), and keep a *second* hidden split never used for any promotion decision, opened once a quarter | Hifz/Ta'allum would otherwise memorise eval items as "verified programs" |

| Style fools the rater | split "content correct" (verifier/teacher-checked) from "wording adequate" (panel) | Gudibande: ~70% "equal or better" while NQ fell 17 → 10 |

- **[inference] Sizing MIZAN's blocks.** The current 194-task Imtihan cannot carry ±5-point claims per block (a 15-item block has a ±25-point interval at p = .5). For the headline "zero-call accuracy" per language a 250–400-item locked set *per language* is the minimum for ±5; for "MIZAN-alone vs teacher on the same items" plan 900–1,800 paired items for a +3-point claim, or accept that only +5 to +10 points are detectable with the few hundred pairs MIZAN can afford (312–626 at 10–20% discordance for +5). Items can be *generated* by MIZAN's own recipes (arithmetic, dates, Quran lookups with known answers) at zero cost; wording items need the panel.
- **[inference] Teacher-call share as a time series.** Define s(t) per family and language as in the sibling note; monitor the *downward* trend with a Beta–Bernoulli BOCPD (hazard 1/30 days) to date the step changes that each new Hifz/Ta'allum/Qasd lesson causes, and a *one-sided upward* CUSUM on the complementary "zero-call error" stream (verified-false ÷ zero-call answers) to catch regressions. Report both with the run-length posterior, not a p-value, so the owner can read "share fell on day d with probability q".
- **[inference] Forgetting-curve fit as a regression test of Tadhkir.** Bin recalls by age since last review and by FSRS stability S; the empirical hit rate per bin should track R(t, S) = (1 + F·t/S)^C within its Wilson interval. Murre & Dros's finding that a power/summed-exponential form fits and a single exponential does not is the a-priori reason to keep FSRS's power form; their 24-hour jump is a testable prediction for Nawm (nightly consolidation): if consolidation works, 1-day retention should sit *above* the smooth curve.
- **[inference] Memory measurement must be built around *supersession*.** The 15–20% current-state figure for the best 2026 systems means a similarity-ranked memory will return the wrong (older) value most of the time once facts change; MIZAN's Daftar should be tested with StateMemBench-style scenarios in Urdu/Hinglish (generated from templates: a preference, a schedule, a number that changes across sessions), scored current / superseded / neither, with anti-trap items.
- **[inference] Human-likeness is a double-edged metric.** BabyLM's negative correlation between benchmark score and reading-time fit means "more human-like" is *not* "more capable"; MIZAN should report human-likeness (panel percentile, reading-time-style surprisal fit if ever measured) as a separate axis and never as a proxy for correctness. The word-frequency artefact warns that any human-likeness metric must be checked against a frequency baseline.

## KQ5. Governance when the teacher-call share reaches ~0: what signal remains, and the case for a residual teacher budget as an audit

### Takeaway
When MIZAN stops calling the teacher, three of its evidence channels remain (verifiers, outside sources, the human panel) but none of them says anything about the *language quality* of open answers, and self-training on self-verified output provably adds no new information (collapse, imitation gap, no intrinsic self-correction). A residual, randomly sampled teacher comparison is the only cheap "fresh real data" signal for wording; detecting a 5-point drop within a week at 80% baseline needs ~528 sampled items (≈75/day), which is 7.5% of traffic at 1,000 requests/day but more than all traffic at 50/day — so at MIZAN's scale the weekly locked eval (free, MIZAN-alone) must carry regression detection, and the teacher audit should run as a slow sequential test over months at a few items per day.

### Cited Findings
- **[measured]** Self-produced data without fresh real data degrades the model (Shumailov 2024, 20–28 perplexity points at 0% retention; Alemohammad 2024, precision or recall falls "without enough fresh real data in each generation"); synthetic data helps only when real data are scarce (Kazdan et al. 2024). — [Nature PDF](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/460496122/ShumailovEtalNature2024AIModelsCollapseWhen.pdf); [ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/ebc042e767de551803ccfcc45e2454f5-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2410.16713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16713)
- **[measured]** Imitation closes the style gap, not the capability gap, and more imitation data does not help (Gudibande 2023). — [arXiv 2305.15717](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15717v1)
- **[measured]** Intrinsic self-correction without external feedback does not improve and can degrade reasoning (Huang et al., ICLR 2024). — [arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)
- **[measured]** Self-scoring diverges from hidden-test truth (15/35 below random) and a single confidential audit bit doubles true performance (Guo et al. 2026). — [arXiv 2607.24300](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24300v1)
- **[measured]** Retrieval ecosystems can keep QA accuracy flat while the evidence base degrades (Spiral of Silence). — [ACL 2024.acl-long.798](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.798)
- **[measured, formula]** One-sample test for a drop against a known baseline; two-sample when both arms are sampled (standard normal-approximation power formulas as in Connor 1987 / SAS POWER). — [SAS POWER documentation](https://support.sas.com/documentation/cdl/en/statug/65328/HTML/default/statug_power_details55.htm)
- **[measured]** Human calibration of model answers is poor and worsens with answer length (Steyvers 2025), so user satisfaction is not a regression signal. — [arXiv 2401.13835](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13835v2)

### Inferences
- **[inference] What survives at zero teacher calls, and what does not.**

| Signal | Survives at s → 0? | What it catches | What it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verifiers (sandbox, Tathabbut, Qiyas, Sidq arithmetic/quotes) | yes | wrong numbers, altered quotes, broken programs | wording quality, wrong *choice* of program, wrong intent |
| Ahl al-Dhikr (outside sources + NLI) | yes, costs bandwidth not money | contradicted world facts | anything not stated on a reachable page; Humanities/regional knowledge where even frontier models drop 25–40 points |
| Locked eval re-run MIZAN-alone (weekly) | yes, free | regressions on known items | drift in *live* traffic distribution; new request types |
| User corrections / restatements (Muhasaba grades) | yes | misses the user notices | misses the user does not notice (Steyvers: users over-trust) |
| Human panel | yes, slow | wording adequacy, human-likeness percentile | too few items per week for ±5 |
| Teacher comparison on sampled live traffic | **only if a residual budget is kept** | wording and content regressions on the *live* distribution; the only "fresh real data" for Ta'allum | costs money; the teacher itself is weakest on regional Humanities |

- **[inference] Why verifier-only self-training adds no language quality.** A verifier filters MIZAN's own candidates; it never adds a phrasing MIZAN did not already produce. Ta'allum then learns from a narrowing set of its own accepted phrasings — the text-level version of the autophagous loop, with the "repeating phrases" symptom. The residual teacher is the fresh-data term in Alemohammad's condition and the "more capable base" in Gudibande's; without it the honest expectation is plateau then slow narrowing, not improvement.
- **[estimated] Audit sampling rate.** To detect a 5-point drop from an 80% baseline within one week (α = .05, power .8, baseline known from the locked eval): 528 audited items/week ≈ 75/day. At 50 requests/day that is >100% of traffic (impossible in a week; needs ~11 weeks at 100% sampling); at 200/day it is 38% of traffic; at 1,000/day 7.5%. A 10-point drop needs 137/week (≈20/day): 40% of traffic at 50/day, 10% at 200/day, 2% at 1,000/day. Cost at DeepSeek-class OpenRouter prices quoted in the 28 Oct report ($0.065/$0.18 per M tokens; ~3k in + 700 out per call): ≈ $0.0003 per audited call → 528 calls ≈ $0.17/week **[estimated]**. The owner's "zero extra spend" is therefore a policy choice, not a cost barrier; the real limit is MIZAN's traffic, which is too low for weekly detection of small drops by sampling alone.
- **[inference] The recommended governance at s ≈ 0.**
  1. **Locked eval as the primary regression gate**, run MIZAN-alone weekly and after every promotion, per language; it is free and needs no teacher. Keep a second hidden split for quarterly use only.
  2. **A slow sequential teacher audit** on a fixed random fraction f of live requests (f = 2–5%), scored by rule where possible and by a *blinded* teacher comparison otherwise, fed into a one-sided Bernoulli CUSUM / e-process on "teacher judged MIZAN worse"; with f = 5% at 200/day this is 70 items/week, which detects a 10-point drop in ~2 weeks and a 5-point drop in ~8 weeks **[estimated]** — slower than a week, but anytime-valid and cheap. Under Ville's inequality the owner may read it daily.
  3. **Self-produced-share monitoring**: the fraction of retrieved evidence that is MIZAN's own output (Ta'allum wordings, Hifz outputs, summaries) per answer; a rising share with flat correction rate is the Spiral-of-Silence signature and a trigger to re-ask the teacher or Ahl al-Dhikr.
  4. **Diversity monitoring** of zero-call answers (distinct-n-gram ratio per family per month) as the early-collapse detector for Ta'allum/Ruh.
  5. **Memory canaries**: k planted false records per store; exposure = log₂|R| − log₂ rank must stay at ~0 for other users and for action parameters.
  6. **Correction-rate floor**: if the user-correction rate per family falls *below* its historical floor while the audit's error rate does not, suspect over-trust rather than improvement (Steyvers/Zhou), and raise f for that family.
- **[inference] Tie to the 28 Oct designs.** D7's confidential audit becomes the operational centre at s ≈ 0; D9's student pipeline is dropped under CPU-only, so the "non-regression on Urdu/Hinglish/Arabic probes" it specified moves to the locked eval; the 28 Oct line "a small residual escalation budget stays necessary" gets its number here: a few items per day, sequentially tested, is the floor below which nothing measures wording.

### Gaps
- No published system reports a "teacher audit rate" or a drift-detection design for a cascade whose strong model is retired; the sizing above is derived, not borrowed.
- The weekly-detection requirement is not achievable at MIZAN's current traffic for a 5-point drop; the detectable effect at realistic sampling is ~10 points in 2 weeks or 5 points in ~8 weeks (one-sample normal approximation; an exact sequential design would shift these somewhat).
- MIZAN's actual daily request volume per language was not available in this session (`experiments_cpu_and_root_coverage.md` E3 reports the zero-call baseline as "not obtained"), so the sampling-rate table is given for 50 / 200 / 1,000 requests per day rather than for the real number.
