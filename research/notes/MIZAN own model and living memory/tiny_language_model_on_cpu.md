# What a 1M–100M-parameter model can learn and do when trained and served on 2 CPU cores / 2 GB RAM (as of 29 October 2026)

Scope note. This file covers the literature side of "MIZAN's own model" under the owner's CPU-only, zero-spend constraint. It builds on, and does not repeat, two sibling notes in this folder: `experiments_cpu_and_root_coverage.md` (E1: measured training/inference throughput of the Ruh architecture at 2 torch threads; E2: Quranic-root coverage of Urdu/Arabic text) and `audit_ruh_model_state.md` (the 46M Ruh model as it exists). It also extends the 28 Oct report `/research/reports/MIZAN self learning architecture.md`, whose §"A small student can absorb verified work" and design **D9 (Lisan-local student on rented GPUs)** assumed LoRA on a 3–8B base trained for $20–300 per adapter on rented H100s and served at Q4 in 2–8 GB. Under the new constraint D9 is **invalid as written**: no GPU may be rented, no 3B model fits beside the backend in 2 GB, and the only trainable object is a model of ≤ ~30–50M parameters. Everything below quantifies what that object can and cannot be. "Measured" = a number reported from a run; "estimated" = arithmetic from measured numbers with stated assumptions; "own inference" = my reading.

## Key question 1 — Throughput: tokens/second for training 10M / 30M / 100M-parameter transformers on 2–4 CPU cores, tokens per day, and the loss/quality that buys

### Takeaway
Measured CPU training throughput for small transformers is in the hundreds to low thousands of tokens per second (574 tok/s at 46M params and 1,034 tok/s at 15M params at 2 torch threads in the sibling experiment; ~200 tok/s for GPT-2 124M in llm.c's plain-C reference on an 8-thread laptop). On the production 2 vCPU that is roughly 10–50M tokens/day at 25% duty, enough to train a 5–30M-parameter model to a Chinchilla-style 20 tokens/parameter in days to weeks, and enough for a TinyStories-class 28M model in weeks, but 2–3 orders of magnitude short of a 100M model trained the way SmolLM2-135M was (2T tokens; ≈320 CPU-years on this box). The literature's small-model lesson is that data curation and the training objective, not scale tricks, decide quality at this size.

### Cited Findings
- **Sibling measurement (production-like 2 torch threads, batch 8×128, AdamW):** 1.03M params 3,954 tok/s; 5.43M 1,809 tok/s; 15.39M 1,034 tok/s; 45.99M (Ruh default, with MoE) 574 tok/s. Tokens/day at 25% duty: 85M / 39M / 22M / 12M. Days to 20×params tokens at 25% duty: 0.2 / 2.8 / 13.8 / 74.2. One gradient step on 1,024 tokens costs 0.26 s (1M) to 1.8 s (46M). — [experiments_cpu_and_root_coverage.md, E1](/research/notes/MIZAN%20own%20model%20and%20living%20memory/experiments_cpu_and_root_coverage.md) (measured, 29 Oct 2026)
- **llm.c CPU reference (`train_gpt2.c`):** GPT-2 124M (124,439,808 params) fine-tuned on tinyshakespeare with B=4, T=64 (256 tokens/step), `OMP_NUM_THREADS=8` on a MacBook Pro M3 Max: ~1.3–1.45 s per step (step 0 = 1452 ms, step 39 = 1324 ms), i.e. ≈ 180–200 tokens/s. Karpathy calls it "a simple reference implementation in C" and says on CPU you "won't go too far" without a powerful processor. — [karpathy/llm.c README](https://github.com/karpathy/llm.c) (measured)
- **nanoGPT:** the repository ships `bench.py` for measuring throughput; I found no published CPU tokens/s figure for nanoGPT, only community statements that the character-level Shakespeare run takes ~5 min on CPU vs ~1 min on GPU (total time, unspecified hardware). — [search summary; nanoGPT skill page](https://playbooks.com/skills/benchflow-ai/skillsbench/nanogpt-training) (unquantified)
- **TinyStories (Eldan & Li, May 2023):** GPT-Neo architecture, context 512, tokenizer cut to the top 10K tokens; models from ~1M to ~35M params (1–8 layers), largest ~80M; "each model can be trained on a single V100 GPU in at most 30 hours"; models below 10M params use embedding dim 256. GPT-4 grading (grammar/creativity/consistency, /10) on one prompt: 1M-8L 6/3/2; 2.5M-8L 5/6/3; 8.3M-8L 7/5/5; 28M-8L 9/6/9; 33M-2L 7/6/4. "Grammar is learned by relatively small models, while consistency and creativity emerge at larger sizes"; a 1M/8-layer model answers no factual prompt correctly, the 28M/8-layer model answers most and outperforms GPT2-XL (1.5B) on those story prompts; factual knowledge tracks embedding dimension, context-tracking tracks depth. — [TinyStories, arXiv 2305.07759](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.07759)
- **TinyStories dataset:** 2,141,709 rows (≈2.12M train, 22k validation), total files 7.62 GB, licence `cdla-sharing-1.0`, stories "synthetically generated by GPT-3.5 and GPT-4" (V2 = GPT-4 only). — [HF dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/roneneldan/TinyStories)
- **Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al., 29 Mar 2022):** >400 models from 70M to 16B params on 5B–500B tokens; "the model size and the number of training tokens should be scaled equally"; Chinchilla = 70B trained with Gopher's compute on 4× more data. — [arXiv 2203.15556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)
- **Data-constrained scaling (Muennighoff et al., 25 May 2023, v5 28 Jun 2025):** 400 runs up to 9B params / 900B tokens; "training with up to 4 epochs of repeated data yields negligible changes to loss compared to having unique data"; beyond that the value of extra compute "eventually decays to zero". — [arXiv 2305.16264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264)
- **MobileLLM (Meta, Feb 2024, ICML 2024):** for sub-billion models "the significance of model architecture" outweighs data and parameter count; deep-and-thin, embedding sharing, grouped-query attention, block-wise weight sharing; +2.7% (125M) and +4.3% (350M) accuracy over prior SOTA at those sizes, +0.7/0.8% more with layer sharing; 350M "close correctness to LLaMA-v2 7B on API-calling tasks". — [arXiv 2402.14905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14905)
- **MiniCPM (Apr 2024):** WSD learning-rate schedule makes the data–model scaling study "linear effort on the model axis and negligible on the data axis"; the derived compute-optimal data/model ratio is "much higher" than Chinchilla's. — [arXiv 2404.06395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06395) (the often-quoted ≈192 tokens/param figure was not confirmed in the fetched text; see Gaps)
- **BabyLM 2024 findings (Dec 2024):** 31 submissions (64 models); Strict (≤100M words) and Strict-Small (≤10M words) both won by GPT-BERT (mixed causal+masked objective; Strict BLiMP 86.1, Strict-Small BLiMP 81.2); "performance on the BabyLM evaluations also correlated strongly with training FLOPs" (regression β=2.7, p<0.01); top models trained for many epochs; the 2025 report adds "we observed in the 2024 BabyLM Challenge that larger numbers of training epochs improved model performance". — [arXiv 2412.05149](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2412.05149); [2025 findings PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- **BabyLM 2025 findings (Nov 2025):** new compute cap "at most 100M words [of exposure, counting repeated passes] for the Strict-Small track and at most 1B words for the Strict track" (≤10 epochs); baselines GPT-BERT ≈120M params (Strict, 12 layers) and ≈31M params (Strict-Small, 12 layers, 6 heads), 10 epochs; "we do not observe a complete correlation between training FLOPs and performance" (positive correlation only in the Interaction track); "some models in the Strict and Interaction tracks are comparable to a Llama 70B parameter model on the BLiMP task"; "models trained on 100M words fall well short of children's abilities". — [2025 findings PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- **bitnet.cpp paper (Oct 2024)** also gives the only published *inference* throughput I found for 125M/350M-class models at 2 threads (see KQ2). — [arXiv 2410.16144v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16144v2)

### Inferences
- **Effective compute of the production box.** Training FLOPs ≈ 6·N·D. From E1, 574 tok/s × 6 × 46M ≈ 1.6×10¹¹ FLOP/s (0.16 TFLOP/s) at 46M; 1,034 × 6 × 15.4M ≈ 0.95×10¹¹ at 15M; the 1M model reaches only 2.4×10¹⁰ (overheads dominate). So plan with **≈1–1.6×10¹¹ FLOP/s sustained** on 2 vCPU with MKL/oneDNN; plain-C llm.c on 8 fast laptop threads gave 200 × 6 × 124M ≈ 1.5×10¹¹, so 2 cloud vCPUs running llm.c-style code would be ~3–5× slower than that (estimated 40–60 tok/s at 124M).
- **Tokens per day** = r · 86,400 · duty. At 25% duty (leaving the backend responsive): 46M → 12M tok/day; ~30M (interpolating E1, ≈750 tok/s) → ~16M tok/day; 100M (extrapolated ≈250 tok/s; llm.c suggests lower) → ~5M tok/day.
- **Chinchilla budgets (20 tokens/param) at 25% duty:** 10M → 200M tokens → ~5 days; 30M → 600M tokens → ~37 days; 100M → 2B tokens → ~1 year (and see the RAM point below). Repetition up to 4 epochs being "free" (Muennighoff) means the *unique* data needed is 50M / 150M / 500M tokens respectively — relevant because clean Urdu/Hinglish text is scarce (KQ6).
- **RAM, not only compute, caps the trainable size.** AdamW in fp32 holds 16 bytes/param (weights, grads, m, v): 10M → 160 MB; 30M → 480 MB; 50M → 800 MB; 100M → 1.6 GB, which does not fit beside a FastAPI backend in 2 GB. Practical ceiling for in-process background training: **≈30–50M parameters** (own inference; E1 agrees that "the 2 GB limit is not binding for ≤50M").
- **How far from "fluent" such a model is (estimated with the Chinchilla loss form).** L(N,D) = E + A/N^α + B/D^β with the constants Hoffmann et al. report for their Approach 3 (E≈1.69, A≈406.4, B≈410.7, α≈0.34, β≈0.28 — quoted from the paper's fit, not re-fetched here; verify before citing): 10M/200M → ≈5.3 nats/token; 30M/600M → ≈4.3; 100M/2B → ≈3.5; versus an irreducible E≈1.69. These are extrapolations below the fitted range (70M) on general web text; they say a 30M model is ~2.6 nats/token above the floor on open-domain text, i.e. nowhere near general fluency, while TinyStories shows that on a *narrow, curated* distribution the same size is near-perfect in grammar.
- **TinyStories is CPU-reachable.** If the 2.1M stories are ~0.5B GPT-Neo tokens (my estimate from the 7.6 GB of files, ±50%), one epoch of a 28M model costs 6·28M·0.5B ≈ 8×10¹⁶ FLOPs ≈ 6 days at 100% duty or ~24 days at 25% on 2 vCPU. A coherent narrow-domain 10–30M generator (templated wording, slot filling, short answers in MIZAN's own house style) is therefore a realistic months-scale CPU project; the TinyStories *data* itself is GPT-3.5/4 output and is excluded by the owner's policy (KQ6), so MIZAN would need its own curated corpus of the same shape (DeepSeek-generated or human-written).
- **The "usable" tiny chat models are out of reach by ~2.5 orders of magnitude.** SmolLM2-135M used 2T tokens (KQ5): 6·135M·2T ≈ 1.6×10²¹ FLOPs ÷ 1.6×10¹¹ FLOP/s ≈ 1.0×10¹⁰ s ≈ **320 CPU-years** at 100% duty. Even the BabyLM Strict-Small baseline (31M params × 100M words × 10 epochs ≈ 1.3B token-exposures ≈ 2.4×10¹⁷ FLOPs) is ~17 days at 100% duty, ~70 days at 25%.
- **What the BabyLM record says to do with that budget:** change the objective and architecture (mixed causal+masked GPT-BERT; diffusion MLM; difficulty-based masking; small MoE), use many epochs over a small clean corpus (2024) up to the ~4–10-epoch point where repetition stops paying (Muennighoff; 2025 cap), and skip developmental curricula.

### Gaps
- No published tokens/s for nanoGPT on CPU; no CPU training numbers in TinyStories (GPU-hours only) or in BabyLM participants' compute reports (they report FLOPs, not CPU seconds). The E1 sibling measurement is the only 2-thread figure; it should be re-run on the production host itself.
- MiniCPM's exact optimal tokens/param ratio and the Chinchilla fit constants were not re-verified from the full PDFs in this session.
- I found no small-model scaling law fitted *below* 70M parameters (Chinchilla's smallest) other than TinyStories' qualitative grades; the loss estimates above are extrapolations.

## Key question 2 — Inference: tokens/second and RAM for 30M–100M models on CPU; BitNet b1.58 on CPU and ternary training from scratch at small scale; Mamba/RWKV/xLSTM vs attention for short sequences

### Takeaway
A 125M-class model decodes at ~120 tok/s (fp16, llama.cpp) to ~320 tok/s (ternary, bitnet.cpp) with only 2 threads on a laptop i7, and a 30M model proportionally faster, so serving is not the problem — weights of ≤100M params need ≤200 MB in fp16 and far less at int8. Ternary training from scratch does not pay at this size: at 6–48M params a 1.58-bit model has roughly half the effective capacity of a 16-bit one (perplexity 26.8 vs 16.6 at 48M), and its training is quantisation-aware with full-precision shadow weights, so it saves nothing in training compute. Recurrent architectures (Mamba/RWKV) win on long contexts and memory per token, not on the short turns of a chat on CPU, where a measured compressed-RWKV ran ~19% slower than comparable small transformers.

### Cited Findings
- **bitnet.cpp vs llama.cpp, 2 threads (Table 5), tokens/s:** Intel i7-13700H: 125M → llama.cpp (fp16) 119.84, bitnet.cpp 316.35 (2.64×); 350M → 41.57 vs 137.68; 700M → 18.56 vs 80.13; 1B → 13.92 vs 57.76. Apple M2: 125M → 251.95 vs 401.76; 350M → 95.18 vs 168.88. Unlimited threads (Table 4), i7-13700H: 125M → 164.04 vs 389.08; 350M → 56.67 vs 172.95. Energy (700M, unlimited threads, i7): 1.367 J/token llama.cpp vs 0.384 bitnet.cpp. **Caveat:** "The tested models are dummy setups used in a research context to demonstrate the inference performance of bitnet.cpp" — the 125M/350M weights are not trained models. — [1-bit AI Infra Part 1.1, arXiv 2410.16144v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16144v2) (measured throughput; synthetic weights)
- **Sibling measurement (torch CPU, 2 threads, no KV cache):** forward at 64-token context 7.0 ms (1M) / 16.4 ms (5.4M) / 35.8 ms (15M) / 86.3 ms (46M); one more token at ctx 64 costs 8.6 / 16.8 / 31.1 / 81.7 ms; the audit notes a 300-byte reply at full context takes ~90 s on the production 2-CPU limit without a KV cache. — [experiments_cpu_and_root_coverage.md, E1](/research/notes/MIZAN%20own%20model%20and%20living%20memory/experiments_cpu_and_root_coverage.md); [audit_ruh_model_state.md §5](/research/notes/MIZAN%20own%20model%20and%20living%20memory/audit_ruh_model_state.md) (measured)
- **ONNX Runtime vs PyTorch on CPU for GPT-2-scale:** one third-party report found ONNX CPU 21.9× faster than PyTorch on a tiny model but only 0.68× at 124M; an older engineering post found a first ONNX attempt 60% *slower* than PyTorch on GPT-2 until memory I/O was fixed, then ~3× faster. No 2-thread, int8, 124M number was found. — [Chimeraforge report](https://chimeraforge.vercel.app/reports/technical-report-118); [Lefebvre-Sarrut blog, 2022](https://ai.lefebvre-sarrut.eu/2022/02/09/what-we-learned-by-accelerating-by-5x-hugging-face-generative-language-models) (secondary; unverified methodology)
- **BitNet b1.58 Reloaded (Nielsen & Schneider-Kamp, Jul 2024):** 4-layer Mistral-like LMs with hidden 32/64/128/256 = 6M/12M/24M/48M params, BPE vocab 8,000, 135M-token corpus × 10 epochs. Best perplexity 16-bit vs 1.58-bit (mean / median quantisation): 6M 77.8 vs 130.1 / 116.6; 12M 36.7 vs 67.4 / 60.0; 24M 21.4 vs 36.3 / 37.5; 48M 16.6 vs 27.1 / 26.8. Authors estimate 1.58-bit models have "roughly half the effective capacity" and need hidden layers "about double the size" to match; training is quantisation-aware with 16-bit shadow weights (no training-cost saving claimed; efficiency claims are for inference via multiplication-free kernels). — [arXiv 2407.09527](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.09527) (measured)
- **Original BitNet b1.58 (Feb 2024):** from-scratch 1.58-bit models match 16-bit "from above 3B parameters". — [HF paper page 2402.17764](https://huggingface.co/papers/2402.17764)
- **Microsoft BitNet b1.58 2B4T (Apr 2025):** a real 2.4B ternary model with a GGUF for bitnet.cpp; a secondary blog reports ≈29 ms/token (~34 tok/s) on an i7-13800H with 8 threads (unverified). — [BitNet README mirror](https://gitee.com/natkang/BitNet); [wavect.io](https://wavect.io/blog/bitnet-cpu-llm-ram-requirements.md)
- **Mamba (Gu & Dao, Dec 2023):** "fast inference (5× higher throughput than Transformers)", linear scaling in sequence length; Mamba-3B matches Transformers twice its size — these are GPU results. — [arXiv 2312.00752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)
- **RWKV-edge (Dec 2024):** on a Raspberry Pi 5 the compressed RWKV models averaged ~19% *lower* tokens/s than comparable small/medium transformers (a few cases ~7% faster; the tiny model ~28% slower), attributed to extra multiplications in added layers; the authors argue accuracy and memory savings outweigh it. — [arXiv 2412.10856](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10856) (measured, modified RWKV)
- **BabyLM 2024:** an HGRN2 (RNN) submission "indicate[s] the viability of RNN-based models" at the 10M/100M-word scale. — [2024 CoNLL BabyLM proceedings](https://aclanthology.org/2024.conll-babylm)
- **MambaByte:** adds speculative decoding with tokenised drafting and byte verification for a 2.6× speed-up over naive byte decoding — the one place speculative decoding matters at small scale (byte-level models), otherwise irrelevant to a single tiny model. — [arXiv 2401.13660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13660)

### Inferences
- **RAM for serving:** fp16 weights = 2 B/param (30M → 60 MB; 100M → 200 MB); int8 = 1 B/param; ternary ≈ 0.2 B/param. A KV cache for a 30M model (e.g. 8 layers × d=384) costs 2·layers·d·2 B ≈ 12 KB/token, i.e. 12 MB for a 1,024-token context. None of this threatens the 2 GB budget; **RAM is binding for training (Adam), not serving.**
- **Latency targets:** scaling the i7-13700H 2-thread figure (120 tok/s fp16 at 125M) by core speed of a cloud vCPU (roughly ½–⅓ of a 13700H performance core) gives an estimated **40–60 tok/s at 125M and >150 tok/s at ≤30M** with a llama.cpp/ggml-style runtime and KV cache, versus the measured 12 tok/s (46M, torch, *no* KV cache) in E1. The engineering step that matters is exporting Ruh to a ggml/ONNX runtime with a KV cache, not shrinking it further.
- **Ternary from scratch:** at MIZAN's scale (≤50M) BitNet-Reloaded's numbers mean a ternary model needs ~2× the hidden size to match fp16 perplexity, which doubles training FLOPs (6·N·D with 4× the matmul width per layer, partially offset) while training still runs in fp32 shadow weights. Since neither RAM nor inference speed is binding at ≤100M, **ternary buys nothing here**; it would matter only if MIZAN ever served a ≥1B model on this box (not possible under the training constraint anyway).
- **Attention vs recurrence on CPU for short turns:** a chat turn of ≤512 tokens through an 8-layer d=384 model spends most FLOPs in the MLP/projections, so attention's O(T²) is a minor term; Mamba's GPU throughput claim and RWKV's constant memory per token do not translate into a CPU latency win at this length (RWKV-edge measured the opposite). Recurrence becomes attractive only for a *long-running state* (a model that reads the whole chat history or a long document once); that is a design choice about memory, covered in the neuroscience/memory note, not a speed choice.

### Gaps
- No measured tokens/s for a *trained* 30–100M model on a 2-vCPU cloud VM with llama.cpp/ggml or ONNX Runtime int8; the bitnet.cpp table uses dummy weights on a laptop CPU. This must be measured on the production host.
- No Mamba-vs-transformer CPU tokens/s comparison for small models was found; no xLSTM CPU numbers at all.
- The 2.4B BitNet 2B4T speed figure is from a secondary blog and unverified.

## Key question 3 — Tokenisation for a root-space + bytes design: byte-level and tokenizer-free models, morphology-aware tokenisation for Arabic/Urdu/Turkish, Hinglish/code-switching, and how Bayan's (root_id, pattern_id) factorisation compares

### Takeaway
Byte-level models now match subword models at scale only with a patching/chunking layer (BLT's entropy patches, SpaceByte's word-boundary blocks, H-Net's learned chunks, MambaByte's state space), and the evidence at tiny scale is thin or negative (H-Net pilots at 22.5M; a neural morphological tokenizer for Turkish scoring *worse* bits/char than byte-BPE). Morphology-aware BPE (MorphBPE, 300M/1B, Arabic among four languages) consistently lowers cross-entropy, but a March 2026 LREC study of Arabic root–pattern morphology finds "tokenizer morphological alignment is not necessary nor sufficient for morphological generation". Bayan's (root, pattern) factorisation is best understood as a hand-designed patcher plus a factored embedding that compresses parameters (measured 5.7× in the sibling audit); whether it lowers loss at matched compute is not established anywhere in the literature and must be measured in bits-per-byte against byte-BPE on the same CPU budget.

### Cited Findings
- **Byte Latent Transformer (Meta, 13 Dec 2024):** patches "segmented based on the entropy of the next byte"; first FLOP-controlled scaling study of byte-level models up to 8B params and 4T training bytes; "for fixed inference costs, BLT shows significantly better scaling than tokenization-based models, by simultaneously growing both patch and model size"; qualitative gains on reasoning and long-tail generalisation. — [arXiv 2412.09871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09871)
- **BLT weakness:** a 2025 follow-up (ATDC) reports entropy patching "can drift on highly repetitive text" such as multiple-choice prompts, and notes H-Net's evaluation "on limited data (1.4B tokens) and smaller model scales prevents a comprehensive performance comparison". — [arXiv 2508.05628v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.05628v1)
- **H-Net dynamic chunking, small-scale pilots:** 22.5M-parameter byte-level H-Net checkpoints; "the chunk-level main network is worth only ~0.07 BPB at this scale"; killing the whole chunk pathway costs +0.34 nats/byte; aligned-minus-misaligned activation patching is at the construction rate (i.e. learned chunks not functionally localised). — [HF project page](https://huggingface.co/AdaptiveChunking/hnet-chunking-pilots) (not peer-reviewed)
- **SpaceByte (Apr 2024):** byte-level decoder with larger blocks applied only after certain bytes "such as spaces that mark word boundaries"; at fixed training and inference compute it outperforms other byte-level architectures and "roughly matches the performance of tokenized Transformer architectures". — [arXiv 2404.14408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14408)
- **MambaByte (Jan 2024, COLM 2024):** token-free SSM "competitive with, and even to outperform, state-of-the-art subword Transformers"; keeps "robustness to noise"; 2.6× decoding speed-up via speculative decoding. — [arXiv 2401.13660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13660)
- **MorphBPE (2 Feb 2025; ACL 2026 Findings):** morphology-aware BPE that constrains merges with morpheme boundaries; English, Russian, Hungarian, Arabic; 300M and 1B LLMs; "consistently reduces cross-entropy loss" vs BPE and "accelerates convergence"; new metrics Morphological Consistency F1 and Morphological Edit Distance; integrated into the Arabic-centric Fanar LLM. — [arXiv 2502.00894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00894); [ACL 2026 Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2068.pdf)
- **Morphemes Without Borders (16 Mar 2026, LREC 2026):** seven Arabic-centric and multilingual LLMs and tokenizers; measures tokenizer morphological fidelity against gold segmentation and LLM performance on productive root–pattern generation; finds "tokenizer morphological alignment is not necessary nor sufficient for morphological generation" and questions the role of morphological tokenisation in downstream performance; a secondary summary adds that GPT-4 has the highest fertility and low morphological alignment on Arabic. — [arXiv 2603.15773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15773)
- **Morpheus (Turkish, Jun 2026):** neural, reversible morphology-aware tokenizer; emits 30% more tokens than byte-level BPE and in a controlled LM probe reaches 1.451 bits/char vs 1.411 for byte-BPE (worse). — [arXiv 2606.18717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18717) (single-author preprint)
- **NedoTokenizer (Turkish, NeurIPS 2026):** a runtime morphology *constraint* "improves BPB in every matched comparison", while morphology-informed vocabulary induction is budget-dependent. — [NeurIPS 2026 entry](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/164799)
- **BabyLM 2024:** "Grapheme and phoneme-aware tokenization schemes did not seem to help language models on the base BabyLM evaluation tasks." — [arXiv 2412.05149](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2412.05149)
- **Bayan/ISM as built:** the sibling audit measured the factored ISM embedding at 361,216 params against a flat 2,048,000, a **5.7× compression (not the 7.8× the README claims)**; the Quranic Arabic Corpus morphology pinned by Sarf has 1,651 roots and 14,835 surface forms; only 43.0% of everyday modern-Arabic tokens and 10.5% of Urdu tokens are exact Quranic surface forms, and 36.6% of Urdu tokens contain a letter Arabic lacks. — [audit_ruh_model_state.md](/research/notes/MIZAN%20own%20model%20and%20living%20memory/audit_ruh_model_state.md); [experiments_cpu_and_root_coverage.md, E2](/research/notes/MIZAN%20own%20model%20and%20living%20memory/experiments_cpu_and_root_coverage.md) (measured)
- **Code-switching resources (sizes in KQ6):** LinCE combines ten corpora over four language pairs, covering Hindi–English for LID, POS and NER (not sentiment) — [LinCE, LREC 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.04322.pdf); Hinglish-TOP has ~10K human-annotated code-switched semantic-parsing utterances plus 170K CST5-generated ones — [Hinglish-TOP](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/Hinglish-TOP-Dataset); HinGE pairs human-written Hinglish with two rule-based generators over parallel Hindi–English — [HinGE, Eval4NLP 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03760).

### Inferences
- **Bayan is a patcher.** A word that Bayan recognises becomes one (root_id, pattern_id) position; everything else becomes UTF-8 bytes (2 bytes per Arabic/Urdu character). That is structurally SpaceByte's idea (one "global" step per word, cheap local steps otherwise) with a linguistic rather than learned boundary rule. The literature therefore predicts the *mechanism* is sound for compute (fewer positions per word than bytes), but says nothing about MIZAN's specific vocabulary: with ~1,650 Quranic roots covering ≤43% of Arabic and ≤10% of Urdu tokens exactly (E2), most Urdu/Hinglish text will still travel as bytes, so sequence length — and CPU cost — will be dominated by the byte path unless the root inventory is widened to classical/modern Arabic roots (the ~4,000 roots the owner mentions) and Urdu/Hindi/English get their own word-level patches.
- **Factored embeddings trade capacity for sharing.** A (root, pattern) embedding E(w) = f(e_root, e_pattern) shares statistics across all words of a root (good for rare derived forms) but cannot represent lexicalised meanings that have drifted from the root (the classic objection to factored/morphological embeddings); MorphBPE's gains at 300M–1B and the Turkish constraint results suggest *soft* morphology (constraining merges or adding morpheme features) helps, while the LREC 2026 result says alignment alone does not deliver generation quality. For Ruh this argues for **(root, pattern) as additional input features alongside a byte/BPE channel** rather than as the only token identity — exactly what the "interlingua as learned concepts over all languages, roots as one feature channel" conclusion in E2 says.
- **The right yardstick is bits per byte (BPB).** Only BPB (or nats per character) is comparable across tokenisations; perplexity per token is not. Any claim that Bayan beats byte-BPE must be a matched-compute BPB comparison on the same Urdu/Arabic/English held-out text on the production CPU — the small-scale H-Net and Morpheus results show such comparisons can come out negative.
- **Hinglish/Roman Urdu is the hardest case for a root tokenizer:** Latin-script spelling is unnormalised (one Urdu word, many romanisations), so a root lookup needs transliteration first (Dakshina/Roman-Urdu-Parl-style data, KQ6); until then bytes carry it. MIZAN's Lughah already identifies the language; the missing piece is a Roman→Urdu-script normaliser whose output Bayan can root-analyse.

### Gaps
- No paper evaluates factored (root, pattern) *input* embeddings for Semitic languages in a modern transformer at matched compute; the classic factored-LM literature (2000s) was not fetched in this session and is not cited here.
- No BLT/SpaceByte/MambaByte results at ≤100M params on Arabic, Urdu or Hindi were found; BLT's multilingual (FLORES) numbers were not in the fetched abstract.
- I found no Urdu-specific morphology-aware tokenisation study; the Arabic and Turkish results are the nearest evidence.
- The Morpheus and NedoTokenizer results are preprints/single-venue entries and have not been replicated.

## Key question 4 — Training recipes that matter at tiny scale: curation vs size, curriculum, distillation without logits, sequence-level distillation from allowed teachers, continual/online learning, kNN-LM/retrieval as the way a tiny model gets knowledge

### Takeaway
At ≤100M parameters the recipes with evidence are: a small, clean, in-distribution corpus (TinyStories), many epochs over it (BabyLM 2024; ≤4 epochs are free per Muennighoff), a better objective (GPT-BERT's mixed causal/masked, diffusion MLM, difficulty-based masking), and distillation — logit distillation from an ensemble of teachers beat the teachers themselves at 58M on 10M words (BabyLlama), and the 2025 BabyLM Interaction track was won by a student trained on a Llama teacher's *corrections* of its own outputs plus preference optimisation, which is the sequence-level form MIZAN can use with a DeepSeek-class teacher. Curriculum learning was "largely unsuccessful" (2023), had a non-significant negative effect (2024, β=−3.6) and again did not top any track (2025). For knowledge, retrieval beats parameters: kNN-LM on a 247M model cut Wikitext-103 perplexity 18.65→16.12 with an in-domain datastore, and a model trained on 100M tokens plus a 3B-token datastore (13.73) beat a model trained on all 3B tokens (15.17) — the quantitative basis for keeping MIZAN's knowledge in verified memory rather than weights.

### Cited Findings
- **Curriculum learning — three negative-to-null results:** 2023: "Curriculum learning attempts, which accounted for a large number of submissions, were largely unsuccessful," though some showed modest improvements — [arXiv 2504.08165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08165); 2024: most common approach, "did not lead to high scores, on average", estimated effect β=−3.6 (p=0.055); "training objective curricula" (e.g. masking parts of speech over training) flagged as promising — [arXiv 2412.05149](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2412.05149); 2025: "While curriculum learning remains popular, the best-performing approaches were again based on modifications to the pretraining objective or the model architecture." — [2025 findings PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- **Objectives that won:** GPT-BERT (causal:masked ≈ 1:7) won both 2024 text tracks; "combining causal and masked language modeling objectives clearly improves performance over single objective baselines"; AntLM alternated 6 causal / 60 masked / 6 causal epochs — [arXiv 2412.05149](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2412.05149). 2025 NLP-task winners: Simple Diffusion (diffusion masked LM, Strict) and AMLM-Hard-Decay (dynamically masking the tokens the model finds hardest, Strict-Small); human-likeness winners CLASS-IT (fine-tuning small LMs on a general instruction-following dataset, Strict) and MoEP (modular mixture of experts, Strict-Small). — [2025 findings PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- **Logit distillation at 58M:** BabyLlama, a 58M LLaMA student distilled from an ensemble (GPT-2 and small LLaMA teachers) on the 10M-word BabyLM corpus, "outperforms both of its teachers" and a same-size model trained without distillation. — [arXiv 2308.02019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02019). BabyLlama-2 (345M, two teachers, 8 epochs, temperature 1, α=0.5) reached a Strict-Small text average of 64.2 vs 61.6 for BabyLlama; the 2024 findings state BabyLlama's two-step approach "definitively outperforms simply training one causal language model". — [BabyLlama-2, CoNLL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.conll-babylm.26.pdf); [arXiv 2412.05149](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2412.05149). "Teaching Tiny Minds" reports effective distillation "with significantly smaller teacher models". — [CoNLL 2024 p. 302](https://aclanthology.org/2024.conll-babylm.27.pdf)
- **Sequence-level teaching without logits (Interaction track, 2025):** baseline and winner (BLM, Martins et al.) use a Llama teacher: the student "generates a completion to a story, and the teacher" corrects it; the student is trained with the LM loss on "full teacher-corrected datapoints" (7 epochs) then 2 epochs of SimPO preference optimisation with teacher vs student completions as winning/losing pairs; "interaction with teacher models can yield high-quality language models"; the student could generate at most 100M words of its own, and teachers were limited to models below 1B parameters. — [2025 findings PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- **TinyStories-Instruct** is itself sequence-level distillation from GPT-3.5/4-generated text: two layers suffice for some instruction following; 33M/4-layer scores grammar 8, creativity 7, plot 8, consistency 9 (/10). — [arXiv 2305.07759](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.07759)
- **Repetition:** ≤4 epochs "negligible changes to loss"; returns then decay — [arXiv 2305.16264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264). BabyLM 2024 top models trained many epochs; 2025 capped exposure at 10 epochs for reproducibility. — [arXiv 2412.05149](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2412.05149); [2025 findings PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- **kNN-LM (Khandelwal et al., ICLR 2020):** 247M-param, 16-layer LM; p(y|x) = λ·p_kNN(y|x) + (1−λ)·p_LM(y|x), λ=0.25 in-domain, 0.65 for domain adaptation; Wikitext-103 test perplexity 18.65 → 16.12 with the 103M-token training set as datastore, 15.79 with a continuous cache added; one 1024-d key per training token, quantised to 64 bytes; FAISS IVF with 4096 centroids, 32 probed, k=1024; Books adaptation: Wiki-3B model 34.84 → 20.47 with a Books datastore (Books-trained LM 11.89); "retrieving nearest neighbors from the corpus outperforms training on it": model trained on 100M tokens + 3B-token datastore 13.73 vs 15.17 for training on all 3B tokens. — [arXiv 1911.00172](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.00172)
- **Parametric imitations of retrieval (2025):** MLP Memory pretrains an MLP to mimic kNN retrieval over pretraining data, reporting ~2.5× faster inference than RAG and 5.6× faster than kNN-LM with a 5B-token corpus and gains over CPT/LoRA/RAG — [arXiv 2508.01832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01832); Memory Decoder trains a small decoder to imitate a non-parametric retriever as a plug-in for any model with the same tokenizer — [arXiv 2508.09874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09874). A secondary note states a Wikitext-103 kNN datastore for GPT-2-small needs nearly 500 GB unquantised. — [search summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/1911.00172.md)
- **Continual learning at 125M:** a 2025 preprint on continued pre-training of 125M models to educational content reports that replaying a small share of the original data, "roughly 1–5%", substantially reduces forgetting, and that no method reviewed fully solves catastrophic forgetting — [arXiv 2504.09687](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09687); CALM compares EWC, learning-rate control and experience replay for continued LM adaptation (results not in fetched text) — [arXiv 2004.03794](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.03794); an EACL 2026 paper uses a replay-adapter strategy with POS-based code-switching to limit forgetting when distilling small models for low-resource languages — [EACL 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2026.eacl-long.293). The 28 Oct report already cites Ibrahim et al. (replay matching retraining) and Shumailov/Gerstgrasser on collapse vs accumulation.
- **Style vs capability:** imitation students "copy its style" but gain little factuality (Gudibande et al.) — already cited in the 28 Oct report §"Verified SFT first".

### Inferences
- **A recipe for a CPU-trained MIZAN model, in the order the evidence supports:** (1) a small, clean, in-domain corpus of MIZAN's own shape — verified answers, templated wordings, slot-filled replies, Hifz program descriptions, DeepSeek-generated paraphrases — repeated 4–10 epochs; (2) a mixed causal+masked objective (GPT-BERT) so the same ≤30M model serves both as a scorer/encoder for Qasd-like tasks and as a generator; (3) teacher-correction + preference pairs exactly as the 2025 Interaction baseline (LM loss on teacher-corrected outputs, then SimPO/KTO on (teacher, student) pairs), using DeepSeek (allowed) as the corrector and MIZAN's verifiers (Tathabbut, Sidq, Hudud) as the judge; (4) no developmental curriculum. Logit distillation (BabyLlama) is unavailable because the teacher is an API without logits — unless a sub-1B open-weight teacher runs on the CPU itself, which the budget forbids for anything useful.
- **Knowledge lives in the datastore.** kNN-LM's "100M tokens of training + 3B tokens retrieved beats 3B tokens trained" is the quantitative justification for MIZAN's architecture: a ≤30M model needs only to be a fluent *reader* of retrieved, verified text (Daftar notes, Hifz programs, Ahl al-Dhikr sentences), not a store of facts. Datastore cost on this box: ≈64 B/key + 4 B/value ≈ 70 B per stored token → 1M tokens ≈ 70 MB, 5M ≈ 350 MB (fits), 10M+ should be on disk with a memory-mapped IVF index. For a 30M model with d=384 the keys are 384-d, so ~¼ of kNN-LM's size per token before quantisation.
- **Online weight updates are cheap but must be guarded.** E1 shows one AdamW step on 1,024 tokens costs 0.3–1.8 s at 1–46M params; the forgetting literature says every such step needs a replay slice (1–5% of original data in the 125M study; the report's earlier recommendation was to retrain adapters from a frozen base on cumulative data). For a tiny model the cheapest safe scheme is: accumulate verified examples during the day, train in the nightly window (Nawm) on new + replay data with a fixed epoch cap, promote only if BPB on a frozen held-out set does not worsen (Tajdid's e-value gate applies), keep the previous checkpoint for rollback.
- **Expected gain from sequence-level distillation at 30M:** none of the fetched papers reports a number at 30M; BabyLlama-2's +2.6 text-average points at 345M and TinyStories' instruction-following at 33M bound the expectation: large gains in *format and house style*, little in factuality — consistent with Gudibande et al.

### Gaps
- No paper quantifies sequence-level (output-only) distillation gains at ≤100M params with a strong API teacher in Urdu/Hindi/Arabic; the BabyLM Interaction track used sub-1B teachers and English only.
- Continual-learning numbers for tiny LMs (EWC vs replay accuracy/perplexity tables) were not obtained; the 125M replay result is the nearest.
- The kNN-LM numbers are 2020 and English; no retrieval-augmented tiny-model results for Arabic-script or code-switched text were found.

## Key question 5 — Honest ceiling: what a 30–100M CPU-trained model can do well, what it cannot, where the "usable chat" threshold sits in 2026, and whether a CPU can reach it

### Takeaway
A CPU-trained 10–50M model can be excellent at narrow, in-distribution jobs — grammar and templated multi-paragraph text in a curated domain (TinyStories 28M: grammar 9/10, consistency 9/10), classification, slot filling, scoring and rewriting MIZAN's own wordings, morphology — and cannot hold open conversation, reason, or know facts: even the GPU-trained SmolLM2-135M (2T tokens, 64 H100s) scores 31.5 on MMLU-cloze (chance 25), 4.1 on TriviaQA and 1.4 on GSM8K, and its instruct version 29.9 on IFEval. The smallest models that behave like a chat assistant in 2026 are ~0.3–0.5B parameters trained on trillions of tokens (SmolLM2-360M-Instruct IFEval 41.0, MT-Bench 3.66/10; Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct IFEval ~31.6, MT-Bench 4.16; Gemma 3 270M IFEval 51.2 but "not designed for complex conversational use cases"); reaching that on 2 vCPU would take centuries of compute, so the answer is **no**, and MIZAN's architecture (recipes + memory + a teacher that teaches once) is the only route to falling API calls.

### Cited Findings
- **SmolLM2-135M (base):** trained on 2T tokens (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM, The Stack, plus filtered sets) on 64 H100s; "primarily understand and generate content in English"; HellaSwag 42.1, ARC-avg 43.9, PIQA 68.4, MMLU-cloze 31.5, CommonsenseQA 33.9, TriviaQA 4.1, Winogrande 51.3, OpenBookQA 34.6, GSM8K (5-shot) 1.4; Apache 2.0; "should be used as assistive tools rather than definitive sources of information." — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M). Paper (4 Feb 2025): the 1.7B was trained on ~11T tokens. — [arXiv 2502.02737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02737)
- **SmolLM2-135M-Instruct:** IFEval (prompt/instruction avg) 29.9 vs 17.2 for SmolLM-135M-Instruct; HellaSwag 40.9. — [HF card via search](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M)
- **SmolLM2-360M vs Qwen2.5-0.5B:** base: HellaSwag 54.5 vs 51.2, ARC-avg 53.0 vs 45.4, MMLU 35.8 vs 33.7, GSM8K 3.2 vs 33.4; instruct: IFEval 41.0 vs 31.6, MT-Bench 3.66 vs 4.16, GSM8K 7.43 vs 26.8. — [SmolLM2-360M card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-360M); [SmolLM2-360M-Instruct card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-360M-Instruct)
- **Qwen2.5-0.5B:** the technical report says it is "on par with or even surpassing the Qwen2-1.5B model" on in-house evaluations; its multilingual IFEval tables begin at 7B, so no 0.5B multilingual chat number is published. — [Qwen2.5 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.15115). Predecessor Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct: IFEval prompt-strict 20.0 (Qwen1.5-0.5B-Chat 14.6). — [Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct). M-IFEval (Feb 2025) notes IFEval is English-only and Qwen translated 100 items per language. — [arXiv 2502.04688](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04688)
- **Gemma 3 270M (Google, 14 Aug 2025):** 170M embedding parameters (256k vocabulary) + 100M transformer parameters; intended for task-specific fine-tuning; the instruction-tuned variant follows general instructions "out of the box" but is "not designed for complex conversational use cases"; INT4 on a Pixel 9 Pro used 0.75% battery for 25 conversations; "You wouldn't use a sledgehammer to hang a picture frame." — [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-gemma-3-270m/). IFEval 51.2% is reported by press, not in Google's post. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/google-unveils-ultra-small-and-efficient-open-source-ai-model-gemma-3-270m-that-can-run-on-smartphones) (secondary)
- **MobileLLM-350M** shows "close correctness to LLaMA-v2 7B on API-calling tasks" — a narrow structured task where a sub-billion model is adequate. — [arXiv 2402.14905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14905)
- **TinyStories 28M/8-layer:** grammar 9, creativity 6, consistency 9 (/10) on a story prompt and "outperforms GPT2-XL (1.5B)" on the paper's factual/reasoning/context prompts *within the story domain*; the largest (~80M) is near-perfect on grammar and consistency "but scores well below GPT-4 on creativity". — [arXiv 2305.07759](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.07759)
- **BabyLM 2025:** 100M-word models reach Llama-70B-level BLiMP (grammatical acceptability) yet "fall well short of children's abilities" on human-likeness; the Multimodal track has had no submission beat its baseline in two years. — [2025 findings PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28.pdf)
- **Quantisation hurts non-Latin scripts most** (Marchisio et al. 2024) — already in the 28 Oct report; keep Urdu/Arabic-script generation at ≥Q5 or fp16 (trivial at ≤100M).

### Inferences
- **Compute gap to the threshold.** "Usable chat" ≈ 0.36–0.5B params × 2–4T tokens ≈ 4–12×10²¹ FLOPs. The production box sustains ~1.6×10¹¹ FLOP/s → **800–2,400 CPU-years at 100% duty**. Even SmolLM2-135M (not usable as chat, IFEval 29.9) is ~320 CPU-years. Nothing in the 2024–2026 literature (BLT, MobileLLM, MiniCPM, BitNet, diffusion LMs) changes this by more than a small constant; the gap is 10³–10⁴×.
- **What the ≤30–50M model should be asked to do (well-evidenced):** (a) classification and slot filling — MASSIVE-style intent/slot tasks are solvable by far smaller models than this (Tamyiz already does intent with naive Bayes; a 10M encoder trained with a masked objective on MIZAN's seeds + MASSIVE can add slots and facets); (b) scoring/reranking candidate wordings and detecting form violations (Nazm) — a GPT-BERT-style model gives pseudo-log-likelihoods for free; (c) templated or near-templated generation in MIZAN's house style over retrieved facts (TinyStories-class fluency in a narrow domain); (d) morphology — root/pattern prediction for unseen Arabic/Urdu words, where Ruh already specialises; (e) next-word prediction for Sama' decoding context and for Hifz/Ta'allum "same words again" matching.
- **What it must never be asked to do:** answer factual questions from weights, quote scripture, reason multi-step, or hold open conversation in Hinglish/Urdu/Arabic. Those remain the teacher's job until the teacher's verified output has been turned into a recipe, a program, or a stored wording — then the small model's role is to *read it back fluently*, with Sidq/Isnad gates unchanged.
- **The "falling API calls" curve is therefore driven by Hifz/Ta'allum coverage and retrieval, not by the model's size.** A measurable proxy is already proposed in E3 (share of answers with zero hosted-model calls); the tiny model raises that share only through tasks (a)–(e).

### Gaps
- No multilingual (Urdu/Hindi/Arabic/Hinglish) chat or instruction-following evaluation of any ≤500M model was found; HinglishEval-style results for small models were not located.
- MT-Bench and IFEval figures for the 135M–500M class come from model cards, not independent replications; the Gemma 3 270M IFEval number is press-reported.
- The position of the "usable" threshold is a judgement from these benchmarks; no paper defines it.

### Inferences
- **Budget-matched corpus.** A 30M model at 20 tokens/param needs 600M tokens, or 150M unique tokens at 4 epochs. A balanced mix is available under permissive terms: ~40M each of English (FineWeb-Edu), Arabic and Hindi (FineWeb2), ~20M Urdu-script (FineWeb2/IndicCorp v2), plus the whole of MASSIVE's five locales (~58k utterances), Dakshina lexicons and FLORES — all ODC-By/CC0/CC BY/CC BY-SA, which only require attribution/share-alike on *redistributed data*, not on a trained model's outputs (own reading; confirm share-alike obligations for any redistributed Ruh training set).
- **Roman Urdu / Hinglish must be manufactured.** With ≤10k human romanised strings (Dakshina) and 10k Hinglish utterances (Hinglish-TOP), the only scalable route is (1) a transliteration model trained on Dakshina + Roman-Urdu-Parl (if its licence clears) that romanises Urdu-script text with spelling variation, and (2) DeepSeek-generated Hinglish paraphrases of MIZAN's own seeds (allowed teacher). FineWeb2 Urdu is Arabic-script only, so it does not help the Latin-script case directly.
- **Policy exclusions.** TinyStories (GPT-3.5/4 output) is out under the owner's rule even though its licence is permissive; any Samanantar use must wait for the licence conflict to be resolved (treat as NC until AI4Bharat's CC0 statement is confirmed on the data files themselves); Tashkeela's GPLv2 is a software licence applied to data — its copyleft reach over a model trained on it is legally unclear, so it is better used as a *source to quote/evaluate diacritisation* than as training text.
- **Quran and hadith** remain sources to quote verbatim (Fihris/hadith_index already hold them) and never training targets; the held-out evaluation set should include scripture quotes precisely to verify the model *does not* reproduce them from weights.

### Gaps
- Per-language token counts for IndicCorp v2 (Hindi/Urdu) were not found; only viewer row counts. FineWeb2's first count column was unlabelled in the fetched text (words vs tokens).
- Licences not found: LinCE, HinGE, OSCAR (Arabic), original Roman-Urdu-Parl, ERUPD size/licence; OPUS (per-corpus licences vary and were not enumerated); NLLB-Seed not checked; UrduHack corpora not located at all.
- No open Hinglish *monolingual* pretraining corpus of meaningful size (≥100M tokens) with a clear licence was found; this is the single largest data gap for the owner's language list.
