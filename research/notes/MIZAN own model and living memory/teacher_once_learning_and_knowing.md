## KQ1. Memory-based learning: lazy learners, the CBR cycle, kNN-LM and instance-based continual learning

### Takeaway
Lazy, instance-storing learners are the right formal shape for Hifz/Ta'allum: on language tasks keeping every example beats eager abstraction by 0.4–5 accuracy points, and a tree-compressed variant (IGTree) classifies two orders of magnitude faster at a cost of 0.2–5 points. The modern "semi-parametric" form (kNN-LM, RETRO) proves the principle at scale but its datastore (200 GB, 14× slowdown) is out of reach on 2 vCPU; the buildable modern form is case-based reasoning over MIZAN's own verified cases (Memento, Agent Workflow Memory), where 4 retrieved cases already saturate the gain.

### Cited Findings
- **[established] Memory-Based Language Processing results (Daelemans, van den Bosch, Zavrel, Machine Learning 1999).** 10-fold CV generalisation accuracy, IB1-IG / IGTree / C5.0: grapheme-phoneme (GS) 93.45 / 93.09 / 92.48; POS tagging 97.94 / 97.75 / 97.97; PP attachment 83.48 / 78.28 / 80.89; NP chunking 98.07 / 97.28 / (C5.0 ran out of memory). Dataset sizes 675,745 (GS), 1,046,152 (POS), 23,898 (PP), 251,124 (NP) instances. Editing even 1% of "exceptional" instances hurt GS accuracy. Conclusion quoted: "keeping full memory of all training instances is at all times a good idea in language learning." — [arXiv cs/9812021](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/9812021)
- **[established] CPU cost of lazy vs compressed lazy learning (same paper, Table 8, 1998 hardware, one partition, seconds train/test):** GS: IGTree 79/9 vs IB1-IG 83/2391; POS: 43/18 vs 211/6416; PP: 6/1 vs 7/10; NP: 152/8 vs 98/19474. Testing with IGTree is "some 2 orders of magnitude faster than IB1-IG". — [arXiv cs/9812021](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/9812021)
- **[established] TiMBL today:** version 7.0 (1998–2026), GPL v3, implements IB1-IG ("k-nearest neighbor classification with feature weighting suitable for symbolic feature spaces") and IGTree ("a decision-tree approximation of IB1-IG"); "in many cases far more efficient in classification" than plain kNN because of its tree-based implementation. — [GitHub LanguageMachines/timbl](https://github.com/LanguageMachines/timbl)
- **[established] The CBR cycle.** Aamodt & Plaza, *AI Communications* 7(1):39–59, 1994: retrieve the most similar case, reuse its information, revise the proposed solution against the world, retain the parts likely to help later. — [DOI 10.3233/AIC-1994-7104](https://www.doi.org/10.3233/AIC-1994-7104); 2005 review of each step by López de Mántaras et al. — [KER review](https://www.maxapress.com/data/article/ker/preview/pdf/S0269888906000646.pdf)
- **[established] kNN-LM (Khandelwal et al., ICLR 2020).** WikiText-103 perplexity 18.65 (base) → 16.12 with the datastore, no retraining; 15.79 with a continuous cache; a model trained on 100M tokens retrieving from a 3B-token datastore reached 13.73 vs 15.17 for a model trained on 3B tokens; swapping in a Books datastore moved Books perplexity 34.84 → 20.47 without retraining. — [arXiv 1911.00172](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00172)
- **[established] kNN-LM cost.** The WikiText-103 datastore "has 103M records and takes 200G space"; base LM 3,847 tokens/s vs kNN-LM 277 tokens/s (13.9× slowdown); adaptive retrieval + pruning + PCA to d=512 recover 6.6× (1,835 tokens/s) at perplexity 16.67. — [He, Neubig, Berg-Kirkpatrick 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04212)
- **[established] Critique:** interpolating with retrieval worsens the prediction of the majority of WikiText-103 tokens; aggregate perplexity improves because a small subset improves a lot. — [Wang et al. 2023, arXiv 2305.14625](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14625.pdf)
- **[established] RETRO (Borgeaud et al., ICML 2022):** a 2-trillion-token retrieval database lets a model match GPT-3 and Jurassic-1 on the Pile with 25× fewer parameters; can be retrofitted onto a pretrained transformer. — [PMLR 162](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/borgeaud22a.html)
- **[established] Learned metrics for kNN:** LMNN learns a Mahalanobis metric (equivalently a global linear map followed by Euclidean kNN) by a semidefinite program so that each example's k neighbours share its label with a margin. — [Weinberger & Saul, JMLR 10 (2009) 207–244](https://jmlr.org/papers/v10/weinberger09a.html)
- **[established] Instance-based continual learning.** GEM (NeurIPS 2017) keeps a small episodic memory and projects gradients; with T=20 tasks it reaches ACC 0.8260 (MNIST permutations; single-model 0.6018, EWC 0.6185), 0.8607 (rotations), 0.6783 (CIFAR-100; iCaRL 0.5462). — [arXiv 1706.08840](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840). Chaudhry et al. 2019: experience replay with reservoir sampling beats purpose-built CL methods once the buffer is "reasonably large"; repeated training on a tiny memory "does not harm generalization, on the contrary, it improves it", with gains of 7–17% from a single example per class. — [arXiv 1902.10486](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)
- **[promising] CBR for LLM agents without weight updates — Memento (Aug 2025).** Memory-based MDP ⟨S, A, P, R, γ, M⟩ with cases c=(s,a,r); policy π(a|s,M) = Σ_c μ(c|s,M)·p_LLM(a|s,c); non-parametric Write M_{t+1} = M_t ∪ {(s_t,a_t,r_t)}, Read = top-K by cosine similarity of a frozen state encoder; parametric variant learns Q(s,c;θ) with a binary cross-entropy loss and reads top-K by Q. Gains plateau at K=4 (DeepResearcher F1 59.9 → 64.5 at K=4, 63.9 at K=32). Ablations with/without case memory: HLE 22.2 → 26.7 F1, SimpleQA 81.0 → 84.7, DeepResearcher 59.9 → 66.6; +4.7 to +9.6 absolute on out-of-distribution sets; GAIA 87.88% Pass@3 validation, 79.40% test (authors' own evaluation). — [arXiv 2508.16153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16153)
- **[established, ICML 2025] Agent Workflow Memory (Wang, Mao, Fried, Neubig):** induces reusable workflows from past (self-judged correct) trajectories and adds them to memory; +24.6% (Mind2Web) and +51.1% (WebArena) *relative* success; online induction beats baselines by 8.9–14.0 absolute points as train–test gaps widen. — [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07429), [PMLR 267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wang25bx.html)
- **[established, EMNLP 2024] Knowledge belongs in retrieval, not weights:** Ovadia et al.: RAG "consistently and significantly" beat unsupervised fine-tuning for knowledge injection; current-events accuracy 0.875 (RAG) vs 0.504 (fine-tuned Mistral-7B); MMLU-Anatomy 0.556 → 0.570 by fine-tuning vs 0.681 by RAG; models "struggle to learn new factual information through unsupervised fine-tuning". — [arXiv 2312.05934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934) (figures as reported by the paper's abstract and secondary summaries; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- **[inference] Hifz/Ta'allum are already a CBR system; name the four steps and add the missing one.** Retrieve = Tamyiz posterior + Hifz/Ta'allum/ilm lookup; Reuse = replay the program or wording; Revise = Tathabbut/Sidq verification, and *only on failure* ask the teacher (this is the teacher-once step); Retain = store with provenance and an ijaza grade (KQ6). Today the "revise → ask teacher → retain" edge exists only for Hifz programs and Ta'allum wording; the audit's gap A3 (no store of teacher-given facts/mappings with provenance) is exactly the missing Retain.
- **[inference] Keep every verified case; compress only for speed.** The MBLP evidence says abstraction (pruning, trees) *loses* accuracy on language data while a tree index merely speeds lookup. For MIZAN: the authoritative store is the full case bank (Hifz rows, Ta'allum rows, Qasd overlay, ilm), and any compressed artefact (an IGTree-like index over hashed n-grams, a slot template from KQ2) is a cache that must reproduce the cases it summarises.
- **[inference] CPU budget.** IGTree classified 67k GS test instances in 9 s on 1998 hardware (≈0.13 ms each); a 2026 core is ≥30× faster, so ≪10 µs per lookup. Brute-force weighted-overlap kNN over MIZAN's own verified cases (expected thousands to tens of thousands per user, feature vectors of hashed character n-grams + words as Tamyiz already builds) is O(N·d) ≈ 10⁴ × 10³ = 10⁷ operations ≈ a few ms in NumPy on one core, so no index is needed until N ≈ 10⁵. kNN-LM-style token datastores (200 GB, 14× slowdown) are ruled out on 2 GB RAM; RETRO needs a trained model. The "learned metric" MIZAN can afford is the information-gain weighting that IB1-IG already uses (Tamyiz's fitted weights are the same idea); LMNN's semidefinite program is unnecessary.
- **[inference] Memento's K=4 plateau and AWM's self-judged induction map onto Lisan prompts.** When MIZAN must still ask Lisan (unverifiable wording), attach the 4 most similar *verified* cases (request → recipe/slots/wording) as Memento does; Memento's +3.7 to +8.2 F1 and AWM's +24–51% relative were obtained with frozen LLMs, so this is the one place where stored cases raise the teacher's own accuracy without training anything.
- **[inference] Continual-learning reading of MIZAN's stores.** Replay with "tiny memories" works because the stored exemplars are real; MIZAN's per-user Qasd overlay plus the global seeds is a reservoir buffer. The GEM metrics (KQ5) apply directly if "tasks" are request families and "training steps" are weeks.

### Gaps
- No modern (2020s) TiMBL/IGTree timing on current CPUs was found; the only numbers are from 1998 hardware.
- The Ovadia et al. figures (0.875 vs 0.504 etc.) come from the abstract and secondary summaries; the exact tables were not fetched.
- No paper was found that reports kNN-LM or Memento-style case memory on Urdu/Hinglish/Hindi; all results are English (or Minecraft/web tasks).

## KQ2. Program and skill learning with verification: wake–sleep library learning, library compression, skill libraries, one-shot program induction, and safe generalisation of a verified program into a slot template

### Takeaway
DreamCoder's sleep phase is literally a nightly consolidation that compresses verified programs into reusable abstractions under an MDL/Bayesian objective, but its cost (≈ a day on 20–100 CPUs) is unaffordable; Stitch/babble reproduce or beat its compression 3–4 orders of magnitude faster, which puts a nightly "Nawm" compression of the Hifz corpus within reach of 2 cores. Generalising one verified program into a slot template is anti-unification (Plotkin's least general generalisation, unique for first-order terms) *plus* re-verification of each instance — the test-guided step is what keeps it safe.

### Cited Findings
- **[established] DreamCoder (Ellis et al., PLDI 2021).** Objective: J(D,θ) ≜ P[D,θ] Π_{x∈X} Σ_{ρ∈B_x} P[x|ρ] P[ρ|D,θ] (Eq. 1); the abstraction-sleep objective (Eq. 4) is log P[D] + max_θ Σ_x log Σ_{ρ∈B_x} P[x|ρ]·max_{ρ'→β ρ} P[ρ'|D,θ] + log P[θ|D] − |θ|₀, with P[D] ∝ exp(−λ Σ_{ρ∈D} size(ρ)), beams |B_x| = 5, and refactorings enumerated in a version-space / e-graph structure (10¹⁴ refactorings represented). Results: solves at least as many held-out problems as every alternative in all eight domains; mean solve time 54.1 s, median 15.0 s; in LOGO graphics and towers neither ablation (no library / no recognition model) solves more than 60% of held-out tasks while DreamCoder solves "nearly 100%"; physics laws 93.3% (best of five runs, mean 84.3%) after 8 wake/sleep cycles vs EC2 86.6%; list/text converge in 10 cycles with a 12-minute synthesis timeout on 64 CPUs (6× less compute than EC2; 15× on symbolic regression); "typically takes around a day using moderate compute resources (20–100 CPUs)"; learning functional-programming idioms from fewer than two dozen problems took "roughly five days on 64 CPUs". — [DOI 10.1145/3453483.3454080](https://doi.org/10.1145/3453483.3454080) (text extracted from the MIT CSAIL copy)
- **[established, POPL 2023] Stitch:** top-down library learning that is "3–4 orders of magnitude faster" than DreamCoder's deductive library learning, uses "about 2 orders of magnitude less memory", with "comparable or better compressivity", scales to corpora of hundreds of complex programs, and is robust to early stopping. — [arXiv 2211.16605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16605)
- **[established, POPL 2023] babble:** "library learning modulo theory" — e-graphs plus anti-unification with an equational theory as input — achieves better compression than DreamCoder on DreamCoder's benchmarks, "orders of magnitude faster". — [arXiv 2212.04596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04596)
- **[established, ICLR 2024] LILO:** Stitch gave ≈1.5–3.5× corpus compression across three domains; feeding raw abstractions to the LLM *hurt* (−30.60 points on REGEX) until the abstractions were documented in natural language (AutoDoc); GPT-4 used as the compressor "did not produce meaningfully compressive abstractions". — [arXiv 2310.19791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19791)
- **[established] Voyager (2023):** skill library of verified action programs indexed by the embedding of their description; 3.3× more unique items, 2.3× longer distances, tech-tree milestones up to 15.3× faster than prior SOTA; the library transfers to a new world. — [arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291)
- **[established] Bayesian Program Learning (Lake, Salakhutdinov, Tenenbaum, Science 2015):** one-shot 20-way Omniglot classification error 3.3% (humans 4.5%, ConvNet 13.5%, deep Siamese 8.0%). — [Omniglot progress report, arXiv 1902.03477](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03477)
- **[established] Anti-unification / least general generalisation.** For first-order terms the LGG is unique up to variable renaming and Plotkin's algorithm computes it (complete, minimal singleton generalisation set); anti-unification is used to build templates in programming-by-example and in babble. — [Cerna & Kutsia, IJCAI 2023 survey](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0736.pdf), [arXiv 2302.00277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00277)

### Inferences
- **[inference] Nawm's "generalise skills" step, made concrete.** Input: the user's Hifz corpus {(request_i, program_i, outputs_i, checked=true)}. Step 1 — cluster by recipe family and normalised AST shape (constants and string literals masked). Step 2 — for each pair/cluster compute the first-order LGG over the AST (Plotkin): positions that differ become typed slots v_1..v_m with a substitution σ_i per original program. Step 3 — *test-guided acceptance*: the template T is kept only if (a) T·σ_i re-executed in the sandbox reproduces outputs_i for every i (Tathabbut-style equality on printed numbers), (b) each slot's fillers share one type (int/decimal/date/string) and the slot is bound to a request span (so Fahm can fill it from the user's own words, never from model text), (c) the Hudud hook passes for every instance. Step 4 — store T with its instances as evidence (Beta posterior over verified reuses, KQ6). Cost: LGG is linear in AST size; re-execution is the dominant cost and already happens in Hifz reuse.
- **[inference] A Stitch-style MDL objective for the whole corpus is affordable nightly.** Minimise Σ_i size(program_i | library) + λ·Σ_{T∈library} size(T), the same P[D] ∝ exp(−λ Σ size) prior as DreamCoder Eq. 4 with the beams replaced by MIZAN's verified programs (one per request). Stitch ran corpora of hundreds of programs where DreamCoder took CPU-days; with 3–4 orders of magnitude speed-up a few hundred Hifz programs should compress in seconds-to-minutes on one core. This should be stated as a target to measure, not a reported number (no CPU-second figure for Stitch was found).
- **[inference] LILO's AutoDoc lesson.** A learned template needs a one-line natural-language description (what it computes, what the slots mean) or it will *hurt* when shown to Lisan; the description is the retrieval key (Voyager indexes skills by description embedding; MIZAN can index by Tamyiz features of the originating requests instead of an embedding).
- **[inference] One-shot induction, DreamCoder-free.** MIZAN's Qiyas already does Lake-style "program from one demonstration + verification on every example". The missing piece is the library: a verified Qiyas transform that re-solves a later puzzle should be retained as a primitive (Voyager pattern), with the same Beta-posterior evidence.
- **[inference] What not to build:** a DreamCoder-style neural recognition model (its "dreaming" phase) — it needs training compute and is the part Stitch/babble show is unnecessary for compression.

### Gaps
- No CPU-seconds figure for Stitch or babble on a 2-core machine; only relative speed-ups vs DreamCoder.
- No published result on anti-unification-based template induction applied to LLM-written Python programs with test-guided acceptance; the closest are PBE systems (FlashFill) and babble on λ-calculus corpora.
- Voyager's skill-library ablation numbers (an aggregator reported −8.3 points without the library) could not be verified against the paper.

## KQ3. Distillation without teacher logits: sequence-level KD, verified-only self-training, what should be distilled, collapse risks, and what Anthropic/OpenAI terms allow

### Takeaway
Every API-teacher distillation method that works is a form of "train on the teacher's verified outputs": Seq-KD recovers almost the whole teacher with 2–4× fewer parameters, STaR/ReST-EM double or triple small-model accuracy by fine-tuning only on self-generated answers that passed a check, and distilling step-by-step beats a 540B model with a 770M one. But imitation copies form, not knowledge, fine-tuning is a poor way to inject facts, and synthetic data must accumulate beside real data to avoid collapse — so MIZAN should distil formats, text→recipe/slot mappings, lexicon entries, templates and provenance-tagged facts, never open-domain knowledge. Anthropic's and OpenAI's terms let MIZAN own, store and reuse outputs inside its own product for the same customer, but forbid using them as training targets for a competing model; both now say explicitly that API data is not used to train the provider's own models.

### Cited Findings
- **[established] Sequence-level KD (Kim & Rush, EMNLP 2016).** Seq-KD trains the student on "the result of running beam search and taking the highest-scoring sequence with the teacher model" (the teacher's mode); Seq-Inter on the beam sequence with the highest BLEU against gold. WMT14 En→De BLEU (greedy/beam): teacher 4×1000 (221M) 17.7/19.5; student 2×500 (84M) baseline 14.7/17.6 → Seq-KD 18.9/19.0 → Seq-KD+Seq-Inter 18.9/19.3; student 2×300 (49M) baseline 14.1/16.9 → Seq-KD 18.1/18.1. Greedy student decodes at 1,051 vs teacher-beam 102 words/s (≈10×); with pruning, 13× fewer parameters for −0.4 BLEU. — [arXiv 1606.07947](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07947)
- **[established] STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022).** Generate rationales, keep only those whose answer is correct ("we filter the generated rationales to include only the ones which result in the correct answer"), fine-tune "from the original pre-trained model M instead of continually training one model to avoid overfitting", iterate. GPT-J 6B on GSM8K: few-shot CoT 3.1% → direct fine-tune 5.8% → STaR 10.1% → STaR+rationalization 10.7%; CommonsenseQA: direct fine-tune 60.0% → STaR 72.5% vs 73.0% for fine-tuned GPT-3 ("30× larger"); arithmetic 89.5% after 16 iterations vs 76.3% baseline. — [arXiv 2203.14465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14465)
- **[established, TMLR 2024] ReST-EM:** sample, filter by binary correctness, fine-tune, repeat; on MATH and APPS with PaLM-2 it "significantly surpasses fine-tuning only on human data" and scales with model size. — [arXiv 2312.06585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06585)
- **[established, ACL Findings 2023] Distilling step-by-step:** a 770M T5 beats few-shot 540B PaLM using 80% of the benchmark data; standard fine-tuning of the same T5 fails to match PaLM with 100%. — [arXiv 2305.02301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02301)
- **[established] Imitation copies style, not knowledge:** students of proprietary LMs "copy its style" but gain little factuality. — [Gudibande et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15717)
- **[established] Collapse vs accumulation:** training on model output that *replaces* real data degrades models over generations ([Shumailov et al., Nature 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y)); *accumulating* synthetic data alongside real data avoids it ([Gerstgrasser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413)).
- **[established, EMNLP 2024] Facts are better injected by retrieval than by fine-tuning** (Ovadia et al.; numbers in KQ1). — [arXiv 2312.05934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934)
- **[established] Anthropic Commercial Terms (effective 17 Jun 2025).** Section B: Customer "owns its Outputs"; "Anthropic disclaims any rights it receives to the Customer Content under these Terms"; "Anthropic may not train models on Customer Content from Services." Section D.4 forbids using the Services "to build a competing product or service, including to train competing AI models or resell the Services except as expressly approved by Anthropic." No provision addresses caching or the customer's retention/reuse of Outputs. — [anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
- **[established] Anthropic support article "Can I use my outputs to train an AI model" (dated 16 Mar 2026):** "We prohibit customers from using our services to train or develop AI models without our written permission"; "You can use Claude's Outputs to train models that don't compete with Anthropic's own models" (examples: sentiment analysis, summarization, anomaly detection); prohibited examples: general-purpose chatbots, models for open-ended text generation, "using Outputs as training targets", reverse-engineering training methods; permitted: Outputs "can also be integrated into your applications to power features within your products" and to "improve internal workflows and productivity". Storing outputs is not addressed. — [support.claude.com article 12326764](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12326764-can-i-use-my-outputs-to-train-an-ai-model)
- **[established] Anthropic enforces the clause:** Feb 2026 action against distillation campaigns (~16M exchanges via ~24,000 fraudulent accounts). — [Anthropic, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks)
- **[established, with caveat] OpenAI Business Terms.** Ownership: "Customer retains all ownership rights in Input and owns all Output. OpenAI hereby assigns to Customer all OpenAI's right, title, and interest, if any, in and to Output." Restriction (document dated 1 Jan 2026, captured 19 Aug 2026 by a compliance tracker): Customer may not, "except for a Permitted Exception, use Output to develop artificial intelligence models that compete with OpenAI's products and services." The August 2023 version's exception allowed outputs for models "primarily intended to categorize, classify, or organize data (e.g., embeddings or classifiers)" not distributed or sold commercially. — [openai.com/policies/business-terms](https://openai.com/policies/business-terms) (live page returned 403 to my fetcher; wording from search capture), [ConductAtlas capture](https://conductatlas.com/platform/openai/openai-business-terms/provision/CA-P-059973/prohibition-on-using-output-to-develop-competing-ai-models/), [Aug 2023 version](https://openai.com/policies/aug-2023-business-terms/)
- **[established] OpenAI API data use:** "data sent to the OpenAI API is not used to train or improve OpenAI models" unless the customer opts in; abuse-monitoring logs kept up to 30 days; Responses API keeps application state 30 days by default unless `store=false` or Zero Data Retention. No statement on customers storing/reusing outputs. — [developers.openai.com — Your data](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)
- **[established] Permissive teachers:** DeepSeek's Terms of Use §4.2(3) allow using outputs for "training other models (such as model distillation)"; OpenRouter adds no training restriction and defers to each model's terms. — [DeepSeek ToU](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-terms-of-use.html), [OpenRouter terms](https://openrouter.ai/terms)

### Inferences
- **[inference] What a "teacher-once" distillation target list looks like, by evidence.** (1) *Formats* and calling conventions — the one thing imitation reliably transfers (Gudibande) and Seq-KD's forte. (2) *Mappings* text → recipe/intent/slots — labels for Qasd/Lughah; the teacher only supplies the label, the user's own words are the input, and the label is kept only after MIZAN's verification succeeded (STaR's filter). (3) *Lexicon entries* (word → root/concept id, KQ6 interlingua) with provenance. (4) *Slot templates* (KQ2). (5) *Verified facts with provenance* in `ilm`/Daftar — retrieval, not weights (Ovadia). (6) Never open-domain knowledge into Ruh's weights, and never Quran/hadith text.
- **[inference] STaR's two rules transfer directly to Tajdid/Ta'lim:** keep only outcomes that passed a check, and refit from the fixed seed corpus each round rather than stacking (the earlier report's "retrain adapters from the base" rule survives as "refit Tamyiz from seeds + accumulated verified overlay"). Gerstgrasser's accumulation rule means the human-written seeds (`tadbir/data/{lughah,qasd}`, MASSIVE batches) must never be dropped as learned rows accrue.
- **[inference] The legal shape for MIZAN, stated precisely (not legal advice).** Storing an Anthropic/OpenAI output and re-showing it to the *same customer's* users through MIZAN's product is "integrating Outputs into your applications to power features" and the customer "owns its Outputs" — permitted by the text of both providers; neither document restricts retention or caching. Using those outputs as training targets for Ruh (an open-ended text generator, i.e. a model in the competing class) is prohibited under Anthropic's article ("using Outputs as training targets", "general-purpose chatbots") and under OpenAI's competing-model clause. A narrow classifier (Qasd intent labels) is in the class Anthropic's article calls permitted and OpenAI's 2023 exception covered, but the owner's own policy already excludes Anthropic/OpenAI outputs from training, so the safe implementation is: tag every Ta'allum/Hifz/ilm row with `provider`/`model` (ruh_learner already records them), allow *reuse at inference* for any provider, and allow *training export* only for rows whose provenance is MIZAN's own computation, a quoted source, the user's own words, or a licence-permitting model (DeepSeek, Apache/MIT weights).
- **[inference] A teacher call must leave a retained artefact.** Define yield y = (verified rows retained) / (teacher calls); a call with y = 0 is pure consumption. Seq-KD's lesson that the teacher's *mode* (one verified sequence) is enough means one teacher call per request family is the design target, not k samples.

## KQ4. Knowing when you know: selective prediction, conformal abstention, semantic entropy, consistency, evidential outputs, value of information, and cascades

### Takeaway
The mathematics for "answer alone vs ask" is settled at three levels: (1) selective prediction defines the trade-off (coverage vs selective risk); (2) split conformal prediction / conformal risk control turns any confidence score — including Tamyiz's posterior or a match-count across samples — into a guaranteed error bound with ~100 calibration items for ±5% slack and ~1,000 for ±2%; (3) value of information gives the decision rule "ask iff (q_T − p)(U + C_err) > C_ask", of which the earlier report's D3 rule is the special case U = 0. Cascades (FrugalGPT, RouteLLM) report 50–98% cost cuts at matched quality; their routing rule is a threshold on a learned win/score probability, which MIZAN already has in Wazn and Tamyiz.

### Cited Findings
- **[established] Selective prediction (Geifman & El-Yaniv, ICML 2019).** A selective model is a pair (classifier f, rejection function g); coverage φ(g) = P(g(x)=1) (the probability an input is not rejected); selective risk R(f,g) = E[ℓ(f(x),y)·g(x)] / φ(g); the whole profile is the risk–coverage curve; SelectiveNet trains f and g jointly and improves the trade-off over threshold-on-softmax baselines. — [arXiv 1901.09192](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09192), [PMLR 97](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/geifman19a.html)
- **[established] Split conformal prediction (Angelopoulos & Bates tutorial, 2021/22).** Compute calibration scores s_i = s(X_i,Y_i); set q̂ = the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉/n quantile; C(X_test) = {y : s(X_test,y) ≤ q̂}. Theorem (Vovk et al.): if calibration and test pairs are i.i.d./exchangeable, 1−α ≤ P(Y_test ∈ C(X_test)) ≤ 1−α + 1/(n+1), "no matter what (possibly incorrect) model is used." Conditional on calibration data, coverage ~ Beta(n+1−l, l) with l = ⌊(n+1)α⌋; with n = 1,000 and α = 0.1 coverage typically falls in 0.88–0.92; Table 1 (δ = 0.1, α = 0.1): n = 22 for slack ε = 0.10, 102 for 0.05, 2,491 for 0.01, 9,812 for 0.005. Conformal Risk Control: for a bounded loss monotone in λ, E[ℓ(C_λ̂(X),Y)] ≤ α with λ̂ the smallest λ whose empirical risk ≤ α − (B−α)/n; Learn-then-Test for non-monotone risks. — [arXiv 2107.07511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)
- **[established] Conformal abstention for LLMs (Yadkori, Kuzborskij, Szepesvári et al., Apr 2024).** Score g(X) = (expected) match count of the greedy answer among sampled responses (self-evaluated similarity), or its log-probability; abstain when g(X) < λ; loss ℓ = (1−a_λ(X))(1−m(X;f(X),Y)); choose the smallest abstention rate with risk ≤ α by CRC: λ̂_n = inf{λ : (n/(n+1))·L_n(λ) + 1/(n+1) ≤ α}, guaranteeing E[R(λ̂_n)] ≤ α; RCPS variants give high-probability bounds. Gemini Pro, Temporal Sequences, calibration n = 3,200: at α = 0.1 test loss 0.082 ± 0.001 with abstention 0.117 ± 0.006 (match count) vs 0.219 ± 0.005 (log-prob); at α = 0.05, loss 0.035, abstention 0.331. The uncalibrated baseline "sometimes violates the risk condition at small calibration sizes". — [arXiv 2405.01563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01563)
- **[established] KnowNo — conformal "ask for help" for LLM planners (Ren et al., CoRL 2023).** Multiple-choice next steps scored by the LLM's label probabilities; nonconformity s = 1 − f̂(x)_y; C(x) = {y : f̂(x)_y ≥ 1 − q̂}; act iff |C(x)| = 1, else ask; calibration N = 400, δ = 0.01. Hardware tabletop at 1−ε = 0.75: plan success 0.76, help-per-step 0.58 vs Simple Set 0.72 (No Help: 0.41 success); mobile manipulation at 0.85: help 0.67 vs 0.81; simulation: up to 24% less help at the same success level; an ensemble baseline needs ≈20× inference. — [arXiv 2307.01928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928)
- **[established, ACL 2025] SConU:** two conformal p-values test whether a test sample's uncertainty deviates from the calibration distribution, to catch exchangeability violations that otherwise give "unbounded miscoverage rates". — [arXiv 2504.14154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14154)
- **[established] Semantic entropy (Farquhar et al., Nature, 19 Jun 2024):** cluster 5–10 samples by bidirectional entailment, entropy over meanings; AUROC 0.790 averaged over 30 task–model pairs vs 0.691 (naive entropy) and 0.698 (P(True)); a DeBERTa NLI judge was ≈0.05 AUROC behind GPT-3.5 as judge. — [Nature s41586-024-07421-0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07421-0)
- **[established] Self-consistency (Wang et al., ICLR 2023):** majority vote over sampled reasoning paths improves CoT by +17.9% GSM8K, +11.0% SVAMP, +12.2% AQuA, +6.4% StrategyQA, +3.9% ARC-challenge. — [arXiv 2203.11171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171)
- **[established] Evidential deep learning (Sensoy, Kaplan, Kandemir, NeurIPS 2018):** evidence e_k ≥ 0 → Dirichlet α_k = e_k + 1, S = Σ_k α_k, belief b_k = e_k/S, uncertainty u = K/S ("inversely proportional to the total evidence"), p̂_k = α_k/S; loss Σ_j (y_j − p̂_j)² + p̂_j(1−p̂_j)/(S+1) + λ_t·KL[Dir(α̃)‖Dir(1)], λ_t = min(1, t/10). — [arXiv 1806.01768](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01768)
- **[established, ACL 2026] Value of information for asking (Dong et al., Jan 2026).** V(b) = max_a Σ_θ b(θ) U(θ,a); V_post(b,q) = Σ_y p(y|q,b)·V(b_y); VoI(q) = V_post(b,q) − V(b); NetVoI = VoI − c; ask q* = argmax NetVoI iff NetVoI > 0, else commit. Beliefs b(θ) come from the LLM, answers simulated per hypothesis. Matches or beats the best hand-tuned threshold in 18 of 20 conditions, up to +1.36 utility in high-cost settings (20Q at c = 0.05: 5.01 vs 3.65); loses only when the cost is so high that asking nothing is optimal. — [arXiv 2601.06407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06407)
- **[established] FrugalGPT (Chen, Zaharia, Zou, May 2023).** Cost c_i(p) = c̃_{i,2}‖f_i(p)‖ + c̃_{i,1}‖p‖ + c̃_{i,0}; choose a list L = (L_1..L_m) and thresholds τ_i to maximise E[r(a, f_{L_z}(q))] subject to E[Σ_{i≤z} cost] ≤ b, where z is the first i with scorer g(q, f_{L_i}(q)) ≥ τ_i; g is a DistilBERT regressor predicting correctness. Matched-accuracy savings: HEADLINES 33.1 → 0.6 (98.3%), OVERRULING 9.7 → 2.6 (73.3%), COQA 72.5 → 29.6 (59.2%, best single model GPT-3); +4% accuracy over GPT-4 at equal cost (figure caption says "up to 5%"). — [arXiv 2305.05176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)
- **[established] RouteLLM (Ong et al., 2024/25).** Route to the weak model iff P(win_strong | q) < α; PGR = (r_router − r_weak)/(r_strong − r_weak); APGR = area under PGR vs strong-call fraction; CPT(x%) = minimum strong-call fraction for PGR x. Trained on 65k Chatbot Arena pairwise comparisons (+ ~1.5k MMLU gold, 120k GPT-4-judged pairs costing ≈ $700). Cost-saving ratios vs GPT-4 at CPT(50%): MT Bench 3.66× (95% of GPT-4 quality), MMLU 1.41× (92%), GSM8K 1.49× (87%); "up to 40% fewer calls" than Martian/Unify at equal quality. — [arXiv 2406.18665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665)
- **[established, ICLR 2024] Token-level cascades (Gupta et al.):** sequence-level confidence suffers length bias; quantiles of per-token uncertainty with a learned post-hoc deferral rule "significantly outperform" simple aggregation. — [ICLR 2024 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/11f5520daf9132775e8604e89f53925a-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **[established] Cascade with verifier:** escalating only when a weak model's samples disagree matched the strong model at ≈40% of its cost. — [Yue et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03094)
- **[established] Guessing must cost:** confidence-target grading penalises a wrong answer by t/(1−t) at target t. — [Kalai et al., Sep 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04664v1)

### Inferences
- **[inference] Three different "do I know" questions, three estimators.** (a) *Do I know what is being asked?* — Tamyiz's MAP margin, made a guarantee by split conformal calibration on the Isnad ledger (score s = 1 − P(chosen family); q̂ at the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉/n quantile; if the conformal set has more than one buildable family, do not act — the KnowNo rule |C(x)| > 1 ⇒ ask, here "ask" = ask the user, cost ≈ one turn). (b) *Is my stored answer still right?* — the Beta posterior over verified reuses of the item (KQ6 ijaza), with FSRS-6 retrievability as the time discount. (c) *Is the teacher's answer right?* — this is the only place for semantic entropy / self-consistency, and both multiply teacher calls (5–10 samples), so use them only when no verifier exists and only while an item is being *licensed*; once licensed, zero calls.
- **[inference] The ask rule, derived from VoI with MIZAN's quantities.** Let p = P(MIZAN's own answer is correct), q_T = P(teacher's answer, after Sidq/Tathabbut, is correct), U = value of a right answer, C_err = cost of a wrong one, C_ask = teacher price + latency. V(answer alone) = p·U − (1−p)·C_err; V(ask) = q_T·U − (1−q_T)·C_err − C_ask. Ask iff (q_T − p)(U + C_err) > C_ask. With q_T ≈ 1 this is (1−p)(U + C_err) > C_ask; the 28 Oct report's D3 rule (1−p)·C_err > C_ask is the case U = 0. Equivalently answer alone iff p ≥ p* = 1 − C_ask/(U + C_err): a *family-specific* threshold (deen: C_err → ∞ so p* → 1, never answer from the model's words; arithmetic with Tathabbut: C_err small, p* low). Calibrate p conformally so that "p ≥ p*" is a guaranteed selective-risk bound, not a MAP label.
- **[inference] Calibration-set sizes MIZAN can meet.** Per family and per language, n ≈ 100 verified-outcome rows gives ±5% slack at δ = 0.1 (Table 1 of the tutorial); n ≈ 1,000 gives coverage within 0.88–0.92 at α = 0.1. The Isnad ledger plus Ibrah reports supply these rows; where a user's own n is small, borrow the global calibration under the earlier report's D6 DP accounting, and use SConU-style p-values to detect that a user's traffic is not exchangeable with the global set (Hinglish vs Tamil, say) before trusting the borrowed threshold.
- **[inference] Cascade = Wazn with a verifier rung at the bottom.** Rung 0 (free): Hifz/Ta'allum/templates/ilm, accepted when verified or licensed; rung 1: Lisan-cheapest; rung 2+: stronger. FrugalGPT's objective applies with MIZAN's scorer g being *verification* (binary, exact) where a verifier exists and the conformal posterior where it does not; expected cost per request = c_0 + (1−a_0)[c_1 + (1−a_1)c_2 …] where a_i is the acceptance rate at rung i — so the teacher-call share equals Π(1 − a_i) over the free rungs, and *every retained item raises a_0*. FrugalGPT's 59–98% and RouteLLM's 1.4–3.7× are ceilings for a cascade whose bottom rung is a cheaper *model*; a bottom rung that is a *verified store* has acceptance → 1 on repeated request families, which is the only route to zero calls.
- **[inference] Evidential form for Tamyiz.** Tamyiz is already a Dirichlet–multinomial model; expose u = K/S (K families, S = total pseudo-counts after the length exponent γ) as an explicit "I have little evidence" signal distinct from "two families tie" (low margin). Both should feed the conformal score.
- **[inference] CPU cost of knowing:** conformal calibration is a sort of n numbers; the VoI rule is a handful of multiplications; both are µs. The expensive signals (semantic entropy, self-consistency) cost 5–10 teacher calls and should be reserved for licensing (KQ6), never for routine answering.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that combines conformal selective prediction with a *memory* store's reuse decision (coverage of "the stored item still applies"); KnowNo and Yadkori et al. calibrate a model's answers, not a case base.
- Yadkori et al.'s TriviaQA tables were cut off; only Temporal Sequences numbers were captured.
- Self-consistency's reported gains are not labelled absolute vs relative in the abstract.
- No published cost figure for running conformal abstention or VoI on CPU — the costs above are my arithmetic.

## KQ5. Measuring the trajectory: teacher-call share, sample efficiency, learning curves, continual-learning metrics, and proving the learning is real

### Takeaway
The literature supplies the estimators: the power law of practice T(N) = a + b·N^(−c) for "cost per request vs items learned" (with the caveat that individual curves are often exponential and the power law can be an averaging artefact), the GEM/Chaudhry matrix R_{i,j} for average accuracy, backward/forward transfer, forgetting and intransigence, and e-processes/test martingales for anytime-valid A/B tests of whether a change really learned anything. What MIZAN must add is the instrumentation (per-request teacher-call count, family, verification outcome), which the audit found missing.

### Cited Findings
- **[established] Power law of practice (Newell & Rosenbloom 1981):** across ~50 years of experiments, time per trial follows RT = a + b·N^(−c) (a = asymptote, N = trials, c = learning rate), i.e. log RT falls linearly in log N. — [Wikipedia summary](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Power_Law_of_Practice), [TQMP tutorial](https://www.tqmp.org/RegularArticles/vol02-2/p052/index.html)
- **[established] Critique:** Heathcote, Brown & Mewhort (2000) argue the power function is largely an artefact of averaging across individuals and that individual-level learning is better fit by an exponential. — [Heathcote et al. 2000](https://typeset.io/pdf/the-power-law-repealed-the-case-for-an-exponential-law-of-3ur3vq1jx1.pdf)
- **[established] GEM metrics (Lopez-Paz & Ranzato, NeurIPS 2017).** R_{i,j} = test accuracy on task j after observing the last sample of task i; b̄ = accuracies at random initialisation. ACC = (1/T) Σ_i R_{T,i}; BWT = (1/(T−1)) Σ_{i=1}^{T−1} (R_{T,i} − R_{i,i}); FWT = (1/(T−1)) Σ_{i=2}^{T} (R_{i−1,i} − b̄_i). Large negative BWT is catastrophic forgetting. Protocol: many tasks, few examples each, each example seen once. — [arXiv 1706.08840](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840)
- **[established] Forgetting and intransigence (Chaudhry et al., ECCV 2018).** A_k = (1/k) Σ_{j≤k} a_{k,j}; forgetting of task j after training through k: f_j^k = max_{l∈{1..k−1}} a_{l,j} − a_{k,j}, F_k = (1/(k−1)) Σ_{j<k} f_j^k; intransigence I_k = a*_k − a_{k,k}, with a*_k a reference model trained on all data up to k ("the inability of a model to learn new tasks"). — [arXiv 1801.10112](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10112)
- **[established] Anytime-valid inference (Ramdas, Grünwald, Vovk, Shafer, 2022/23):** e-processes and confidence sequences "remain valid at all stopping times", allowing "optional stopping or continuation for any reason"; they rest on test martingales (non-negative martingales starting at one). — [arXiv 2210.01948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01948)
- **[established] Episodic replay improves rather than harms generalisation** even from one example per class (+7–17%). — [Chaudhry et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)

### Inferences
- **[inference] Definitions MIZAN should persist per request (the audit's gap A4):** family f (Fahm recipe or `converse`), language, `lisan_calls` (count of model calls), tokens and cost (Iqtisad), verification outcome ∈ {verified, unverified, corrected, failed}, and `source` ∈ {hifz, taallum, template, ilm, lisan, teacher}. Then:
  - **Teacher-call share** s_f(t) = Σ_{requests in window} 1[lisan_calls > 0] / N_f, with a Wilson interval (already in Imtihan); and the *weighted* share Σ cost / N_f.
  - **Zero-call accuracy** a_f^0 = verified / (requests answered with lisan_calls = 0) — the selective-risk denominator; report (coverage 1 − s_f, risk 1 − a_f^0) as a risk–coverage point per family per week.
  - **Yield** y_f = retained verified items / teacher calls (KQ3).
  - **Sample efficiency** N_f*(τ) = number of verified items in family f after which s_f fell below τ for the first time, estimated from the fitted curve.
- **[inference] Learning-curve fit.** Fit s_f(N) = a_f + b_f·N^(−c_f) (power) *and* s_f(N) = a_f + b_f·e^(−λ_f N) (exponential) per user and family, N = cumulative verified items in f; pick by held-out log-loss, following Heathcote's warning that pooled curves look power-law while individual ones are exponential. a_f is the *irreducible* teacher share of the family — the quantity the owner cares about; a_f ≈ 0 is reachable only for families with a verifier or a finite template space, which is the 28 Oct report's "a small residual escalation budget stays necessary" made measurable.
- **[inference] GEM matrix for MIZAN.** Tasks = request families (or languages); "training on task i" = the week in which family i's verified items were added; R_{i,j} = zero-call accuracy on family j at the end of week i. BWT < 0 means a new Qasd overlay or template interfered with an older family (the "rich-get-richer" recall problem in audit D3 would show here); FWT > 0 means a family was answered without any teacher call before its first week (templates generalising across families); Chaudhry's intransigence I_k = (accuracy a reference Tamyiz fitted on all data would reach) − (what the incremental overlay reached) measures whether online learning is leaving accuracy on the table.
- **[inference] A/B that learning is real, anytime-valid.** Pair requests by family and week (early vs late, or incumbent vs challenger as Tajdid already does) and run the paired e-process on discordant pairs; promote a claimed learning effect only when the e-value reaches 1/α. The same machinery can test "teacher-call share fell" without a fixed horizon, and the owner can look at the dashboard daily without inflating α (Ville's inequality, as MIZAN's `core.adaptive.paired_e_value` already assumes).

### Gaps
- No published agent paper reports a "teacher-call share" or "local share" curve over time with intervals; the metric is defined here, not borrowed.
- No source gives sample-efficiency numbers (items needed to retire a teacher) for intent/recipe learning; MIZAN's own Imtihan data will have to produce the first curve.

## KQ6. Islamic-studies angle, only where it sharpens the design: verification before acting (49:6, 17:36), the sanad as provenance ("I heard from" vs "it reached me"), ijaza as licence to transmit, and Ibn Khaldun on habit by repetition

### Takeaway
The transmission sciences give MIZAN a ready-made grading of *how* a stored item was acquired (direct hearing > reading back to the teacher > written licence > "it reached me"), a licence state (ijaza) that is explicitly about permission to transmit rather than mastery, and a duty to verify before acting (49:6; "it is enough of a lie for a man that he narrates everything he hears"). Ibn Khaldun adds that a habit (malakah) forms only by gradual, repeated practice and decays when practice stops — the same shape as FSRS retrievability and the Beta posterior over verified reuses. All of this is indication (ishara) for design, not ruling.

### Cited Findings
- **[established] Quran 49:6 (Saheeh International):** "O you who have believed, if there comes to you a disobedient one with information, investigate, lest you harm a people out of ignorance and become, over what you have done, regretful." — [quran.com/49:6](https://quran.com/49:6)
- **[established] Quran 17:36:** "And do not pursue that of which you have no knowledge. Indeed, the hearing, the sight and the heart — about all those [one] will be questioned." — [quran.com/17:36](https://quran.com/17:36)
- **[established] Sahih Muslim, Introduction 10:** "It is enough of a lie for a man that he narrates everything he hears"; Malik, same section: "a man who relates everything he hears is not safe, and he can never be an Imam". — [sunnah.com/muslim/introduction/10](https://sunnah.com/muslim/introduction/10)
- **[established, scholarly summary] Modes of reception and their rank:** samāʿ (hearing from the teacher) "occupies the highest rank among the modes of transmission, surpassing other methods such as ʿarḍ/qirāʾah (reading to the teacher), ijāzah (permission to transmit)"; Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ records that qirāʾah is accepted by the majority. — [HRMARS paper on tahammul](https://hrmars.com/papers_submitted/10791/an-introduction-to-the-application-of-reception-tahammul-of-hadith-in-the-digital-hadith-data-development-process.pdf)
- **[established, scholarly summary] The formulas encode the mode:** "Haddathana" is used when the teacher read to the student; "Akhbarana" when the student recited to the teacher (a convention of the later muhaddithun). — [Hadith Answers](https://hadithanswers.com/the-difference-between-haddathana-and-akhbarana/). "Balaghani" (it reached me) marks indirect transmission that "necessitates critical methodological analysis to assess the authenticity of each narration". — [study of Ibn Abi Shaybah's Musannaf, 2025](https://journal.iaipibandung.ac.id/index.php/haddatsana/article/view/454)
- **[established, scholarly summary] Ijaza:** "a formal authorisation granted by a teacher permitting a student to transmit a particular hadith collection, text, or body of knowledge through a recognised chain of transmission (sanad)"; "an ijazah in hadith ... primarily documents transmission rather than mastery". — [Al-Salam Institute](https://alsalam.ac.uk/?p=34624)
- **[established] Ibn Khaldun, Muqaddimah, ch. VI §36 "The right attitude in scientific instruction":** instruction "is effective only when it proceeds gradually and little by little"; the method "requires a threefold repetition" (summary of principal problems → second pass with full commentary → third pass leaving nothing obscure); "For habits are acquired by continuous and repeated activity. When one forgets to act, one forgets the habit that results from that particular action"; confronting the student with "the final results of a discipline at the beginning" confuses him; do not expose him "to two disciplines at the same time". — [muslimphilosophy.com Ch_6_36](https://www.muslimphilosophy.com/ik/Muqaddimah/Chapter6/Ch_6_36.htm) (the site hosts Rosenthal's translation; the page itself does not name the translator)
- **[established] Ibn Khaldun §56 "The linguistic habit is obtained by much memorizing":** "The quality of the resulting habit depends on the quality, type, and amount of the memorized material"; "The habits obtained by the soul are obtained only gradually"; memorised scholarly material can also hinder the habit (Ibn al-Khatib's testimony). — [Ch_6_56](https://www.muslimphilosophy.com/ik/Muqaddimah/Chapter6/Ch_6_56.htm). §7 "Scientific instruction is a craft": "Habit is different from understanding and knowing by memory". — [Ch_6_07](https://www.muslimphilosophy.com/ik/Muqaddimah/Chapter6/Ch_6_07.htm)

### Inferences
- **[inference, ishara] Provenance grades for every retained item, after the modes of reception.** *samāʿ* = MIZAN itself computed or executed and verified it (Hifz, Tathabbut, Qiyas, Fihris quote) — highest; *qirāʾah* = the teacher's answer was read back against an independent check and passed (Sidq, Tathabbut cross-check, two Ahl al-Dhikr witnesses); *ijāza* = licensed for reuse after k verified reuses (below); *balāghanī* = the teacher said it, unverified — shown only with the da'if marking Isnad already uses, never reused silently. This is the Retain step of KQ1 with a typed `how_received` field, and it is what the audit's gap A3 asked for.
- **[inference, ishara] Ijaza as the "reuse without re-asking" licence, made quantitative.** Each retained item carries Beta(a_0 + v, b_0 + w) over verified (v) and failed/corrected (w) reuses across *distinct* requests (the independence the muhaddithun demanded of witnesses). License for silent reuse when P(θ ≥ τ_f) ≥ 1 − α_f, with τ_f and α_f set from the family's p* = 1 − C_ask/(U + C_err) (KQ4); revoke (demote to balāghanī) on a lapse, which FSRS already grades as Again. Because ijaza "documents transmission rather than mastery", a licensed *wording* permits re-saying, not reasoning from it: Lisan may be shown a licensed wording, but MIZAN must not infer new facts from it.
- **[inference, ishara] 49:6 and Muslim's introduction fix the default:** the teacher's answer is "information from a bearer whose reliability is not established" until investigated; nothing unverified enters the store, and nothing stored is narrated back without its grade (Isnad already does the latter).
- **[inference, ishara] Ibn Khaldun's three passes match a three-stage licence:** first encounter (summary: store the case, grade balāghanī/qirāʾah), second (repeat with verification: template induction once two instances agree), third (nothing obscure: licensed, zero-call). His "habits decay when one forgets to act" is the empirical warrant for FSRS retrievability as the time term in the reuse posterior, and his warning against mixing two disciplines is a warrant for *per-family* overlays and calibration sets rather than one pooled learner — matching the GEM BWT check in KQ5.

### Gaps
- The modes-of-reception ranking and the haddathana/akhbarana convention are quoted from secondary scholarly sources, not from Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ's Muqaddimah or al-Khaṭīb al-Baghdādī's al-Kifāya directly; a classical primary citation should be added by the coordinator if the report leans on it.
- No sahih hadith was found that speaks of ijaza as such (it is a later scholarly institution); the Quranic and Muslim-introduction citations cover verification and narration, not licensing. Keep ijaza as analogy only.
