## Key question 1 — Biology with equations: Hebb/Oja/BCM, STDP, synaptic tagging and capture, metaplasticity and homeostatic scaling, CLS and systems consolidation, prioritised replay, reconsolidation, pattern separation/completion, forgetting, working memory, event segmentation, predictive coding, engram allocation, dopamine/RPE and three-factor rules

### Takeaway
Every plasticity mechanism that matters for a "living memory" has a short, published equation, and almost all of them are one of three shapes: a Hebbian product with a stabiliser (Oja/BCM/STDP), an eligibility trace multiplied by a delayed third factor (synaptic tagging and capture at the hour scale, neuromodulated three-factor rules at the seconds scale, dopamine RPE as the third factor), or a normalisation/decay that creates forgetting pressure (homeostatic scaling, cascade/metaplastic synapses whose forgetting is a power law). Replay priority (gain × need), schema-dependent fast consolidation, reconsolidation on retrieval, sparse pattern separation before storage and attractor completion at recall are the system-level rules that sit on top.

### Cited Findings

**Hebbian learning and its stabilisers (Oja, BCM)**
- Oja's rule adds a weight-dependent decay to the Hebbian product, `Δw = η·y·(x − y·w)`; the `−y²·w` term keeps the weight vector bounded, and "the normalization component is crucial, because otherwise learning would diverge" — [Oja 1982, J. Math. Biol. (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00275687); secondary summary with the equation: [Musacchio 2024, BCM rule blog](https://fabriziomusacchio.com/blog/2024-09-08-bcm_rule); [BCM theory (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCM_theory)
- The BCM rule pairs the Hebbian product with a sliding threshold: `Δw ∝ x·y·(y − θ)`, with `θ = ⟨y²⟩` tracking the time average of (squared) postsynaptic activity, so the same input depresses the synapse when the neuron has recently been very active and potentiates it when it has been quiet; "BCM normalizes through the threshold, while Oja's rule normalizes through weight-dependent decay" — [Bienenstock, Cooper & Munro 1982, J. Neurosci. 2:32–48](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/2/1/32); [BCM theory (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCM_theory)

**Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP)**
- The STDP window is asymmetric and exponential: in Bi & Poo's hippocampal culture data "the time constant for the exponential function was 34 ± 13 msec for depression and 17 ± 9 msec for potentiation"; modellers then use a single `τ_STDP = 20 ms` for both sides — [van Rossum, Bi & Turrigiano 2000, J. Neurosci. 20:8812](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/20/23/8812); original data [Bi & Poo 1998, J. Neurosci. 18:10464](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/18/24/10464)
- Song, Miller & Abbott's model (`Δw = A₊·exp(−Δt/τ₊)` for pre-before-post, `−A₋·exp(Δt/τ₋)` for post-before-pre, with `τ₊ = 20 ms`) shows that STDP "provides a mechanism that can lead to competitive Hebbian learning without global intracellular signaling", i.e. timing-based plasticity is self-stabilising and competitive — [Song, Miller & Abbott 2000, Nat. Neurosci. (PDF)](https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/elie/NEUR_1680_2012/Song_Miller_Abbott_2000.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/78829)

**Synaptic tagging and capture (STC) — the biological "importance gate" on consolidation**
- Frey & Morris (1997) separated long-term plasticity into early-LTP (lasts 2–3 h, protein-synthesis independent) and late-LTP (>10 h), and showed that a weak stimulus leaves a synaptic *tag* which can *capture* plasticity-related proteins triggered by a strong, independent event, so the weak memory is consolidated only if something important happens nearby in time — [Frey & Morris 1997, Nature 385:533 (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1038/385533a0); the 2021 modelling paper summarising the e/l-LTP time scales: [Frontiers Comput. Neurosci. 2021, simplified STC model](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncom.2021.798418/full)
- The tag is "a temporary structural state of the synapse … could last for approximately 90 min"; the STC hypothesis is reviewed as "a bridge from molecular to behaviour" — [Redondo & Morris 2011, Nat. Rev. Neurosci. (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3240215/)
- Computational STC models exist (state-transition model of Barrett et al. 2009; recurrent-network models of Luboeinski & Tetzlaff) and "simulated a broad range of experimental phenomena known as tagging experiments" — [Barrett et al. 2009, PLoS Comput. Biol. (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000259); summary in [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncom.2021.798418/full)
- Gerstner et al. place synaptic tagging on the ~1 hour time scale as the long-time-scale instance of the same two-stage logic as three-factor rules — [Gerstner et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2018.00053/full)

**Three-factor (neoHebbian) rules and eligibility traces — neuromodulated plasticity**
- Two-stage rule: (Eq. 1) an eligibility trace `e_ij` is set by coincidence of a presynaptic factor `x_j` and a postsynaptic factor `y_i`, with "η … the constant learning rate, τ_e … a decay time constant", i.e. `de_ij/dt = −e_ij/τ_e + η·f_j(x_j)·g_i(y_i)`; (Eq. 2) the weight changes only when a global third factor arrives, `dw_ij/dt = M_3rd(t)·e_ij(t)`: "In the absence of the third factor (M_3rd(t) = 0), the synaptic weight is not changed", a negative third factor with a positive flag decreases the weight — [Gerstner, Lehmann, Liakoni, Corneil & Brea 2018, Front. Neural Circuits 12:53](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2018.00053/full)
- Time scales of the trace: theory predicts ~200 ms–2 s for action learning and ~1 s for surprise-based learning; measured: striatum ~1 s (Yagishita 2014), cortex 5–10 s pre-before-post and ~3 s post-before-pre (He 2015), hippocampus minutes (Brzosko 2015/2017) and ~2 s behavioural-time-scale plasticity (Bittner 2017) — [Gerstner et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2018.00053/full)
- The third factor is defined for RL as "reward minus expected reward" (a prediction error) and "could be triggered by attentional processes, surprising events, or reward"; surprise or novelty reinforces "even in complete absence of food rewards", and a neuron-specific "mismatch-related error signal" is proposed as a third factor — [Gerstner et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2018.00053/full)

**Dopamine as reward-prediction error (the learning-rate / third-factor signal)**
- Midbrain dopamine neurons encode a temporal-difference reward prediction error `δ_t = r_t + γV(s_{t+1}) − V(s_t)`: after training their burst "shift[s] … from the moment the reward arrives to the moment the cues begin", and an omitted predicted reward produces "a marked decrease in firing … precisely at the moment the reward was anticipated" — [Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997, Science 275:1593 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9054347/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593)

**Metaplasticity and homeostatic (multiplicative) synaptic scaling — normalisation / forgetting pressure**
- Synaptic scaling "scales the strength of a neuron's excitatory synapses up or down to keep firing stable"; it is global/multiplicative (all synapses of a neuron scaled by the same factor, preserving relative weights) and "can prevent runaway potentiation" caused by Hebbian learning; first observed as compensatory changes in cultured neocortical neurons that returned firing rates to baseline — [Turrigiano 2008, "The self-tuning neuron", Cell 135:422 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2834419/)
- Complex (cascade/bidirectional-chain) synapse models: memory traces decay as "the slowest allowed among power-law decays"; the number of internal variables grows only logarithmically with memory lifetime; the models reproduce "delayed expression of synaptic modifications, metaplasticity, and spacing effects"; this line of work gives theoretical limits on retention for any bounded synapse and shows biologically realistic models can approximately reach them — [Benna & Fusi 2016, Nat. Neurosci. (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4401); predecessor cascade model [Fusi, Drew & Abbott 2005, Neuron (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.02.001)
- Sleep downscales synapses globally as a form of selective forgetting (synaptic homeostasis hypothesis) — [Tononi & Cirelli 2014, Neuron](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.12.025) (already in the 28 Oct report)

**Complementary learning systems, systems consolidation, schemas**
- A fast hippocampal store keeps sparse one-shot traces; a slow neocortex learns by interleaved replay, which avoids catastrophic interference — [McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419); updated CLS: replay is weighted toward salient/rewarded experience and the neocortex can learn fast when a schema exists — [Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016, TICS](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.05.004)
- Schema-dependent fast consolidation: with a prior schema, new associations become hippocampus-independent within ~48 h instead of weeks — [Tse et al. 2007, Science](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1135935) (both already in the 28 Oct report; restated here because the proposed loop below depends on them)

**Prioritised replay: gain × need (the exact formulas)**
- `EVB(s_k,a_k) = Gain(s_k,a_k) × Need(s_k)`; Gain (Eq. 5/13) `= Σ_a (π_new(a|s_k) − π_old(a|s_k))·Q_new(s_k,a)` — "the gain is zero when the backup doesn't change the policy"; Need (Eq. 6) `= μ_π(s_k) = Σ_i γ^i δ(S_{t+i}, s_k)`, the discounted expected future occupancy, computed from the successor representation as the current-state row of `(I − γT)^{-1}` (T a learned transition model); for offline/sleep replay, with the agent absent, Need is the stationary distribution `μ` with `μT = μ`; the agent replays backups in order of EVB, recomputing after each step, with a fixed budget of 20 planning steps and a floor gain of 1e−10 standing in for persistent uncertainty — [Mattar & Daw 2018, Nat. Neurosci. (PMC full text)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6203620/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0232-z)
- With no parameter fitting, the rule reproduces forward replay before a run (need-dominated), reverse replay after reward (gain-dominated), over-representation of rewarded locations, sleep replay favouring the rewarded arm, more reverse replay after larger-than-expected reward, and declining replay with familiarity — [Mattar & Daw 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6203620/)

**Reconsolidation on retrieval**
- A consolidated fear memory becomes labile again when retrieved and requires new protein synthesis to persist, so a retrieved memory can be rewritten — [Nader, Schafe & LeDoux 2000, Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/35021052) (in the 28 Oct report; used below as the "update-on-recall" rule)

**Pattern separation (dentate gyrus) and pattern completion (CA3)**
- CA3 is treated as a single autoassociation/attractor network supporting one-trial memory and completion of a stored pattern from a fragment; separation comes from "the randomizing effect of mossy fiber synapses, the large number of sparsely firing dentate granule cells, and sparse independent CA3 representations"; the dentate gyrus uses competitive learning to turn grid-like entorhinal inputs into place-like fields — [Rolls 2013, Front. Syst. Neurosci. 7:74](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2013.00074/full)
- Caution: "pattern separation" is used loosely in the behavioural literature; the computational meaning (orthogonalising similar inputs before storage) is the one to keep, and the converse of completion is convergence, not separation — [Santoro 2013, Front. Behav. Neurosci. (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3726960/)

**Engram allocation by excitability**
- Neurons compete for allocation to an engram and "those having relatively higher intrinsic excitability at the time of training win"; CREB over-expression in a random 10–20% of lateral-amygdala neurons biased allocation to them; in dentate gyrus, silencing the few CREB-over-expressing neurons attenuated memory expression whereas silencing a random similar-sized set did not — [Josselyn & Frankland 2018, Annu. Rev. Neurosci. (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-080317-061956); review of engram evidence, excitability and lifetime: [Josselyn & Tonegawa 2020, Science 367:eaaw4325 (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaw4325)

**Interference and the power law of forgetting; working memory; event segmentation**
- Forgetting fits a power function better than an exponential ([Wixted & Ebbesen 1991](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1991.tb00175.x)); the environment's need probabilities follow power laws of recency and frequency ([Anderson & Schooler 1991](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1991.tb00174.x)); working memory holds ~4 chunks ([Cowan 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922)); people segment experience at peaks of prediction error ([Zacks et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.133.2.273)) and EM-LLM applies the surprise rule to token streams ([EM-LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09450)) — all in the 28 Oct report; retained as the dependencies of the loop below

**Predictive coding / free energy as a unifying objective**
- "free energy is an upper bound on surprise, so agents that minimize free energy implicitly minimize surprise"; under the principle, perception and action both "reduce to suppressing sensory prediction errors"; the review itself says the principle's "capacity to unify different perspectives on brain function has yet to be established" — [Friston 2010, Nat. Rev. Neurosci. 11:127 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20068583/), [author PDF](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/NRN.pdf)
- Hierarchical predictive coding (feedback carries predictions, feed-forward carries residual errors) — [Rao & Ballard 1999, Nat. Neurosci. 2:79 (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1038/4580) (DOI from memory; not fetched in this session)

### Inferences
- The three equation shapes map cleanly onto MIZAN primitives. (a) Hebb + stabiliser = how an associative key-value store should be written (outer product with decay or delta correction, see Q2). (b) Eligibility trace × third factor = how MIZAN should decide *what gets consolidated*: every candidate memory gets a tag with a decaying trace (hours), and it is captured only when a third factor arrives — a verification pass (Sidq/Tathabbut/Hifz), a user confirmation, or a large prediction error. This is exactly MIZAN's existing asymmetry (verified → kept, unverified → re-checked by Muhasaba), now with a time window and a sign: a negative third factor (user correction) with an active tag *weakens* the trace. (c) Multiplicative scaling + power-law synapses = the forgetting pressure MIZAN lacks; FSRS-6 already gives the per-item power law (Tadhkir), what is missing is the *competitive* normalisation across items that share a key (fan), which BCM/Turrigiano supply.
- Dopamine RPE says the learning rate should be a prediction error, not a constant. MIZAN already computes two prediction errors per message: `−log P(message)` under Lughah/Qasd (Tamyiz) and, if Ruh is online, the token-level loss. Both are usable as `M_3rd(t)` and as the Titans-style surprise gate (Q2).
- Gain × Need converts directly into a nightly replay queue for Nawm: Need = FSRS retrievability at the next expected use (or the ACT-R need odds), Gain = expected change in what MIZAN would answer (1 − verification confidence, or the KL between the current and re-verified answer policy). The sleep form (stationary distribution) says offline replay should weight by *long-run* usage, not last access. This refines D5's "sample with p_i ∝ (need × gain)^α".
- Pattern separation before storage is the biological argument for storing *sparse hashed/binary* keys (k-winners-take-all over a random projection) rather than dense embeddings, and pattern completion at recall is the argument for a one-step modern-Hopfield/attention read (Q2). Both are cheap on CPU.
- Engram allocation by excitability suggests a concrete, cheap rule: a new memory is attached to the cluster/root that was most recently active (temporal linking), which is what the TCM context vector in D4 already approximates; this is an inference, not a tested design.
- Reconsolidation says recall must be a *write*: FSRS review (already in Tadhkir) plus a key update toward the current context, and if the recall meets a contradiction the memory is superseded rather than reinforced (D4's supersession).

## Key question 2 — Computational memory models runnable on CPU: formulas, capacity, reported accuracy

### Takeaway
The models fall into four families with a shared mathematical core: (i) associative memories where attention *is* the retrieval rule (modern Hopfield = softmax attention, exponential capacity; SDM ≈ attention; VSA/HDC binding/bundling), (ii) fast weights updated by Hebbian or delta rules at inference time (Ba 2016, Schlag 2021/DeltaNet, Titans, TTT, Atlas, HOPE), (iii) non-parametric datastores read by kNN (kNN-LM, RETRO) or trainable sparse key-value tables (Meta Memory Layers), and (iv) continual-learning regularisers/replay (EWC, SI, replay, generative replay, SDMLP). Families (i), (iii) and the SDM/VSA/replay parts of (iv) are proven and CPU-cheap at 1e5–1e6 items; family (ii) is proven only at 125M–1.3B parameters on GPUs and is speculative for a 25M-parameter CPU-trained Ruh.

### Cited Findings

**Modern (continuous) Hopfield networks = attention**
- Update rule `ξ_new = X·softmax(β·Xᵀξ)` (X = stored patterns, ξ = query): "The new update rule is equivalent to the attention mechanism used in transformers"; it "retrieves the pattern with one update", has "exponentially small retrieval errors", and "can store exponentially (with the dimension of the associative space) many patterns"; three kinds of fixed points: "global fixed point averaging over all patterns", "metastable states averaging over a subset of patterns", "fixed points which store a single pattern" — [Ramsauer et al. 2020/2021, "Hopfield Networks is All You Need", arXiv 2008.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217)
- Capacity statement in the paper body: storage capacity proportional to `c^((d−1)/4)` (e.g. c = 1.37 or 3.15 depending on the separation assumed), versus 2^(d/2) for the earlier binary exponential-interaction model whose "radius of attraction vanished" — [arXiv 2008.02217v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.02217v1); the polynomial-capacity predecessor (dense associative memory with interaction `F(x)=x^n`, capacity ∝ `d^(n−1)`) — [Krotov & Hopfield 2016, arXiv 1606.01164](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.01164)

**Sparse Distributed Memory (Kanerva) and its link to attention**
- "Transformer attention closely approximates Kanerva's Sparse Distributed Memory under certain data conditions", checked in pre-trained GPT-2; the Hamming radius d sets how many neurons each read/write touches, and for the attention-like setting (n = 64, r = 2n, m ≤ 1024) the reference optimal radii are d*_SNR = 11, d*_Mem = 5, d*_CD = 15; the learned attention β interpolates between the radii optimising critical distance and signal-to-noise — [Bricken & Pehlevan 2021, NeurIPS, "Attention Approximates Sparse Distributed Memory"](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/8171ac2c5544a5cb54ac0f38bf477af4-Abstract.html), [arXiv 2111.05498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05498)
- **SDM as a continual learner (SDMLP)**: a one-hidden-layer MLP with Top-K activation (k = 10 default; subtractive inhibition of the (k+1)-th activation), a "GABA switch" that linearly anneals k from all neurons down to k_target, L2-normalised inputs and weights, no biases, non-negative weights, SGD *without* momentum (Adam/RMSProp suffer "stale momentum" in sparse nets). Split CIFAR-10 class-incremental (5 splits, 256-d ConvMixer embeddings pretrained on ImageNet32, 2,000 epochs per split), final validation accuracy with 10K neurons (Table 1): SDMLP 0.71, FlyModel 0.82, MAS 0.67, EWC 0.65, SI 0.44, ReLU MLP 0.21, SDMLP+MAS 0.84, SDMLP+EWC 0.86, oracle on full CIFAR-10 0.93; "does not require any task labels, task boundaries, or memory replay". Ablations (1K neurons, k = 10): full model 0.63; with SGD-momentum 0.54; Adam 0.23; no L2 norm 0.20; negative weights allowed 0.57; no k-anneal 0.35; any bias term 0.20 — [Bricken, Davies, Singh, Krotov & Kreiman 2023, ICLR, arXiv 2303.11934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11934)

**Hebbian fast weights and fast weight programmers (delta rule)**
- Fast weights `A(t) = λ·A(t−1) + η·h(t)h(t)ᵀ` (decay λ, outer product of the hidden state); reading `A(t)h` sums over recent hidden vectors weighted by their scalar product with the current one and by decay^age, "a kind of attention to the recent past"; "by using fast weights we can avoid the need to store copies of neural activity patterns" — [Ba, Hinton, Mnih, Leibo & Ionescu 2016, NeurIPS, arXiv 1610.06258](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.06258)
- Linearised attention is "mathematically equivalent to the Fast Weight Programmers Schmidhuber proposed in the early 1990s, apart from normalisation"; the purely additive update has a capacity limit, fixed by a delta-rule update `W_t = W_{t−1} + β_t·(v_t − W_{t−1}k_t)·k_tᵀ` (Widrow–Hoff error correction) with a learned, dynamically changing β_t — [Schlag, Irie & Schmidhuber 2021, ICML, arXiv 2102.11174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174); hardware-efficient chunked training of the delta rule: [DeltaNet, Yang et al. 2024, arXiv 2406.06484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484); with a forgetting gate: [Gated DeltaNet, arXiv 2412.06464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464)

**Test-time learning memories: Titans, TTT, Atlas, HOPE (Nested Learning)**
- **Titans** (Google, 31 Dec 2024): neural long-term memory M (an MLP, depth L_M = 1–4) trained *at inference* on the associative loss `ℓ(M_{t−1}; x_t) = ‖M_{t−1}(k_t) − v_t‖²₂`, with momentum-of-surprise `S_t = η_t·S_{t−1} − θ_t·∇ℓ(M_{t−1}; x_t)` (η_t data-dependent "surprise decay", θ_t the momentary-surprise learning rate) and forgetting `M_t = (1 − α_t)·M_{t−1} + S_t` (α_t ∈ [0,1] a forgetting gate). Table 1 (FineWeb-Edu; Wiki ppl / LMB ppl / avg. accuracy): 340M/15B tokens — Transformer++ 31.52/41.08/42.92, DeltaNet 28.65/47.30/44.04, TTT 27.44/34.19/44.51, Gated DeltaNet 27.01/30.94/45.42, Titans LMM 26.18/29.97/46.17, MAC 25.43/28.13/47.36, MAG 25.07/28.72/47.54, MAL 24.69/28.80/46.55; 760M/30B tokens — Transformer++ 25.21/27.64/48.69, Mamba2 22.94/28.37/48.34, Gated DeltaNet 21.18/22.09/49.69, Titans LMM 20.04/21.96/51.56, MAC 19.93/20.12/52.51, MAG 18.61/19.86/52.50, MAL 19.07/20.33/50.97. S-NIAH (RULER) at 2K/4K/8K/16K: Titans LMM passkey 99.8/98.4/98.2/96.2, numbers 100/99.8/93.4/80.2, words 90.4/89.4/85.8/80.6; MAC words 98.2/98.2/95.6/95.2. BABILong numbers are only in figures; the abstract claims scaling "to larger than 2M context window size" — [Behrouz, Zhong & Mirrokni, arXiv 2501.00663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00663v1)
- **TTT layers** (Jul 2024): "The hidden state is itself a machine learning model, and the update rule is a step of self-supervised learning": `W_t = W_{t−1} − η∇ℓ(W_{t−1}; x_t)`, `ℓ(W; x_t) = ‖f(θ_K x_t; W) − θ_V x_t‖²`, output `z_t = f(θ_Q x_t; W_t)`; TTT-Linear (W a linear map) and TTT-MLP (two-layer MLP); 125M–1.3B models; "TTT-Linear has comparable performance as Mamba at 2k context, and better performance at 8k"; at 32k both TTT variants beat Mamba, which "stops improving after 16k"; mini-batch TTT with b = 16; the dual form trains "more than 5× faster than with primal"; a 125M ablation goes from linear attention 15.91 ppl to TTT-Linear 11.09 (Table 1); per-size perplexities and A100 latencies are only in figures — [Sun et al. 2024, arXiv 2407.04620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620)
- **Nested Learning / HOPE** (NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 31 Dec 2025): optimisers are re-read as associative memories compressing gradients; a Continuum Memory System (CMS) is "a spectrum of blocks with different frequencies of update" (higher-frequency blocks adapt fast but hold briefly; lower-frequency blocks hold persistent knowledge); HOPE = self-modifying Titans + CMS, memory update (Eq. 96) `M_t = M_{t−1}(α_t·I − η_t·k_t k_tᵀ) − η_t∇L`, output through a chain of MLP blocks `y_t = MLP^(f_k)(…MLP^(f_1)(o_t))`, keys/queries L2-normalised, local conv window 4. Table 2 (Wiki ppl / LMB ppl / avg.): 760M/30B — Transformer++ 24.18/24.27/50.11, Samba 21.07/22.85/51.46, Titans 20.08/21.52/51.68, HOPE 18.68/20.07/52.28; 1.3B/100B — Transformer++ 17.92/17.73/53.38, Titans 15.60/11.41/56.82, HOPE 14.39/10.08/58.04. NIAH (4K/8K/16K): S-NIAH-1 HOPE 100/100/100 vs Transformer 88.6/76.4/79.8; but MK-NIAH-1 HOPE 29.4/24.8/14.8 vs Transformer 79.4/83.0/61.4 and MQ-NIAH HOPE 31.7/24.8/14.2 vs Transformer 58.9/48.0/29.8 (multi-key/multi-query retrieval is *worse* than attention). Continual-learning (CLINC, Banking, DBpedia) and BABILong ("maintains its good performance even for 10M context length") are shown only as plots. Cost: HOPE "has higher memory usage" than baselines; a lowest update frequency of 2K tokens gives a "significantly more efficient forward pass" at close performance to 512 — [Behrouz, Razaviyayn, Zhong & Mirrokni, arXiv 2512.24695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24695); [Google Research blog, Nested Learning](https://research.google/blog/introducing-nested-learning-a-new-ml-paradigm-for-continual-learning/); [NeurIPS 2025 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/116123). So "nested learning/HOPE" exists and is verified; its results are language modelling at ≥760M parameters on GPUs.
- **Atlas** (May 2025; ICML 2026 poster): replaces the per-token online update with an "Omega rule" that optimises the memory over a sliding window of past tokens, uses a deep memory with polynomial/exponential feature kernels for capacity and the Muon optimiser for the inner update; chunked parallel training; a third-party 43M-parameter checkpoint exists and its author documents deployment problems of test-time-learning models — [Behrouz et al., arXiv 2505.23735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23735); [ICML 2026 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65037); [third-party Atlas-MAG checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/toddwbucy/Atlas-MAG_OmegaRule) (secondary; numbers not verified)

**Non-parametric memory: kNN-LM and RETRO**
- kNN-LM: `p(y|x) = λ·p_kNN(y|x) + (1−λ)·p_LM(y|x)`, `p_kNN(y|x) ∝ Σ_{(k_i,v_i)∈N} 1[y = v_i]·exp(−d(k_i, f(x)))`. Wikitext-103 test perplexity: base LM 18.65 → +kNN-LM 16.12 → +kNN-LM+continuous cache 15.79 (dev 17.96 → 16.06 → 15.81); Books: 11.89 → 10.89. Datastore 103M tokens (Wikitext-103) and ~2.87B tokens (Wiki-3B); FAISS: 1024-d keys, 4096 centroids learned on 1M sampled keys, keys quantised to 64 bytes, k = 1024 neighbours, 32 probes; λ = 0.25 optimal in-domain, 0.65 for domain adaptation. Table 3: a 100M-token-trained LM with a 3B-token datastore reaches 13.73 ppl vs 15.17 for an LM trained on the full 3B tokens — "retrieving nearest neighbors from the corpus outperforms training on it"; building the datastore is one forward pass, "a fraction of the cost of training for one epoch" — [Khandelwal et al. 2020, ICLR, arXiv 1911.00172](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00172)
- RETRO: frozen BERT retriever + chunked cross-attention over a 2-trillion-token database; "obtains comparable performance to GPT-3 and Jurassic-1 on the Pile, despite using 25× fewer parameters" (7.5B is the largest of 172M/425M/1.5B/7.5B); performance keeps improving with database size up to 2T tokens; pre-trained transformers can be "RETROfit" — [Borgeaud et al. 2022, ICML, arXiv 2112.04426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426); [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/improving-language-models-by-retrieving-from-trillions-of-tokens/)

**Trainable sparse key-value memory: Memory Layers at Scale (Meta, Dec 2024)**
- `I = SelectTopkIndices(Kq)`, `s = Softmax(K_I q)`, `y = s·V_I`; product keys `K = K1 × K2` with two half-key sets of √N rows so the full N×n key matrix is never built; gated output `(y ⊙ silu(xᵀW1))ᵀW2`; memory shared across layers. Table 1 (1.3B base): dense NQ 7.76 / TriviaQA F1 32.64; Memory+ with 1M values 13.68/42.89; 4M keys 14.43/51.18; 16M keys 20.14/58.67; 64M keys (≈128B memory params) 20.78/62.14; Llama2-7B (2T tokens) 25.10/64.00. Memory models "generally match the performance of models with twice the number of dense parameters on QA tasks" and the intro claims matching dense models "trained on 4× more compute"; at 8B, Memory+ 16M values reaches TQA 68.15/NQ 27.06 vs dense 63.62/25.24 (Table 2). Cost argument: memory layers add parameters "without increasing FLOPs" and are "almost entirely memory bandwidth bound" (custom kernel ~3 TB/s on H100) — [Berges et al., arXiv 2412.09764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09764)

**Episodic memory controller for LLMs (Larimar)**
- Fast episodic memory (Kanerva-machine Bayesian updates rewritten as least-squares) + LLM as slow semantic memory; one-shot knowledge writes without fine-tuning; "accuracy comparable to most competitive baselines, even in the challenging sequential editing setup" with "speed-ups of 8-10x"; also selective forgetting and leakage prevention — [Das et al. 2024, ICML, arXiv 2403.11901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11901)

**Continual learning without forgetting on small models**
- EWC: `L(θ) = L_B(θ) + Σ_i (λ/2)·F_i·(θ_i − θ*_{A,i})²` (Fisher-weighted quadratic anchor) — [Kirkpatrick et al. 2017, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1611835114) (in the 28 Oct report)
- Synaptic Intelligence: online per-parameter importance `Ω_k = Σ_tasks ω_k / (Δ_k² + ξ)` with `ω_k` the path integral of gradient × parameter change, then a quadratic penalty like EWC — [Zenke, Poole & Ganguli 2017, ICML, arXiv 1703.04200](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04200) (DOI/arXiv from memory; not fetched this session)
- Generative replay (a generator + solver pair replays pseudo-samples of past tasks) — [Shin et al. 2017, NeurIPS, arXiv 1705.08690](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690) (not fetched); brain-inspired replay: in class-incremental learning "established methods fail … even on simple toy problems" and "generative replay (GR) is currently the only method capable of performing well in this scenario without storing data"; replaying *internal hidden representations* via context-modulated feedback connections reaches state of the art on class-incremental CIFAR-100 without stored data and scales to 100+ tasks — [van de Ven, Siegelmann & Tolias 2020, Nat. Commun. (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7426273/)
- DualNet: CLS-inspired fast learner (supervised, pattern-separated task representations) + slow learner (self-supervised, task-agnostic representations); "outperforms state-of-the-art continual learning methods by a large margin" on CORE50 and miniImageNet (numbers not retrieved) — [Pham, Liu & Hoi 2021, NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/86a1fa88adb5c33bd7a68ac2f9f3f96b-Abstract.html)
- SDMLP (above) is the replay-free, task-free option with the strongest biological provenance: SDMLP+EWC 0.86 vs oracle 0.93 on Split CIFAR-10 — [arXiv 2303.11934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11934)

**Spiking networks with Hebbian/STDP plasticity on CPU**
- A benchmark of NEST, Brian2, Brian2GeNN, BindsNET and Nengo "from single core CPU workstations to multi-node supercomputers" found "BindsNET simulator has the best speed and scalability for most of the SNN workloads … on a single core CPU", NEST strongest for small sparse networks, and that "CPU versions of simulators were often sufficient" — [Kulkarni et al. 2021, Neurocomputing (OSTI)](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1779135); Brian2's GPU backend is up to three orders of magnitude faster than its CPU backend, which bounds what CPU SNN simulation can do — [Brian2CUDA, Front. Neuroinform. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fninf.2022.883700/text)

**Vector-symbolic architectures / hyperdimensional computing (cheap symbolic composition)**
- Two-part survey of HDC/VSA models (binary, bipolar, HRR, MAP, sparse) and data transformations (Part I) and applications/cognitive architectures (Part II) — [Kleyko, Rachkovskij, Osipov & Rahimi, ACM Comput. Surv. 55(6):130, 2022, arXiv 2111.06077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06077), [Part II, ACM CSUR 55(9):175, arXiv 2112.15424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15424); HRR (circular convolution binding) — [Plate 1995, IEEE TNN](https://doi.org/10.1109/72.377968); the framework paper — [Kanerva 2009, Cognitive Computation](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12559-009-9009-8)
- Bundling capacity at D = 10,000 bits: ideal (counter-based) bundling stores roughly 60–70 orthogonal hypervectors retrievably, naive binary back-to-back bundling saturates at 10–15, and a 5-bit counter matches the ideal — [Schmuck, Benini & Rahimi 2018, arXiv 1807.08583](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08583); a library guide quotes ~0.35·D items for MAP bundling and "~100 items at dim 10,000" — [HoloVec theory guide](https://holovec.readthedocs.io/en/stable/theory/vsa_models.html) (the two figures disagree; capacity is model- and readout-dependent)
- Capacity theory for superposition/sequence memories with clean-up (winner-take-all) readout, including the role of the forgetting time constant in a memory buffer and crosstalk-limited information capacity — [Frady, Kleyko & Sommer 2018, Neural Computation 30:1449 (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01084), [PDF](https://redwood.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/FradyNeCo2018.pdf)

### Inferences
- The unifying fact for MIZAN: *attention, modern Hopfield retrieval, SDM reads and kNN datastore lookups are the same operation* (a softmax/top-k over key similarities followed by a weighted sum of values). A single CPU key-value store with (a) hashed sparse or binary keys and (b) one-step softmax read therefore serves as episodic store, pattern-completer and kNN-LM datastore at once. Nothing has to be trained for this to work (kNN-LM, Hopfield and SDM are training-free; capacity bounds are theorems).
- For MIZAN's *language model* the proven, zero-GPU route is kNN-LM/RETRO-style: keep Ruh small, add a datastore of (hidden state → next (root, pattern)) pairs built with one forward pass over whatever MIZAN has verified, and interpolate. kNN-LM's 18.65 → 16.12 and "100M-trained + 3B datastore beats 3B-trained" are the closest published evidence that memory can substitute for training compute, which is exactly the owner's constraint.
- Memory Layers show that if Ruh is ever retrained, a product-key memory block gives capacity without FLOPs; on CPU the lookup is bandwidth-bound, which at ≤1e6 slots × 512 dims (≤1 GB fp16; ≤256 MB int8) is fine, but *training* the table still needs the base model's backward pass — so it is a design option for the slow store, not for one-shot learning.
- Titans/TTT/HOPE prove that gradient-step memories beat Transformers *at 340M–1.3B parameters trained on 15–100B tokens on accelerators*. Nothing in those papers is at 25M parameters or on CPU; HOPE's multi-key NIAH weakness also shows these memories are lossy compressors, not lookups. For MIZAN they are a research track (Q4 lists a cheap experiment), not a dependency.
- SDMLP is the one continual-learning result whose ingredients (Top-K, L2 norm, non-negative weights, plain SGD) all come from biology, needs no task labels or replay, and is a one-hidden-layer MLP — small enough to train on 2 CPU cores. Its 0.71 (0.86 with EWC) vs 0.93 oracle on a 5-split class-incremental task is the quantitative floor to expect for a small parametric "neocortex" that learns new classes in sequence.
- VSA/HDC is the right representation for the *root-space interlingua*: bind root ⊗ pattern (XOR for binary vectors, circular convolution for HRR), bundle a sentence's bound word vectors, compare by Hamming/cosine. Composition is proven; cross-lingual semantic equivalence at MIZAN's scale is not (it depends on Bayan mapping Hinglish/Urdu/Hindi words to the same roots, which is the owner's hypothesis, not a measured result).

### Gaps
- TTT per-size perplexities and the A100 latency curves exist only as figures in the paper; I could not extract exact numbers. The same holds for Titans' BABILong and HOPE's continual-learning plots.
- Atlas results were seen only through secondary summaries; the exact tables were not fetched.
- DualNet's accuracy numbers and Shin 2017's numbers were not retrieved.
- No published result trains a Titans/TTT/HOPE-style memory on CPU or below ~125M parameters; CPU feasibility below is my arithmetic.
- No Lava/Loihi CPU-backend throughput figure was found; CPU SNN simulation is possible (BindsNET/Brian2) but no number ties it to MIZAN's scale.
- No single-core FAISS/hnswlib benchmark at 1M × 768 was found in the literature ([MonaVec 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19458) pins one core but on 45K × 1024; the FAISS wiki's rule of thumb is that single-thread is ~10× slower than multi-thread); the costs in Q3 are therefore my own measurements.

## Key question 3 — For each model: formula, memory/CPU cost per write and per read at 1e5–1e6 memories, and reported accuracy

### Takeaway
On two CPU threads, a brute-force dense read over 1e5 memories costs 7–16 ms and a binary 10,000-bit Hamming read ~60 ms; at 1e6 memories dense fp32 (1.5 GB at 384-d) no longer fits the 2 GB box, so 1e6-scale memory must be int8/PQ/binary with an ANN graph, or stay at ~1e5 "live" items with the rest on disk (SQLite/BM25, which MIZAN already has). Writes are microseconds (outer product, XOR, append). A 25M-parameter Ruh-sized transformer trains at ~1,000 tokens/s and infers at ~2,000 tokens/s on 2 threads, so incremental CPU training of the *slow* store is feasible at roughly 10–20M tokens/day in the background, not at pretraining scale.

### Inferences (own arithmetic, formulas shown)
- RAM budget. The backend already holds the FastAPI process, the ONNX NLI model, Fihris/hadith indexes and SQLite caches; assume ≤400–500 MB can go to a live memory index. That admits: 1e5 memories at 384-d fp16 (77 MB) or fp32 (154 MB), or 1e5 × 10,000-bit binary (125 MB), or 1e6 × 2,048-bit binary (256 MB), or 1e6 × 64-byte PQ codes (64 MB) + an HNSW graph (~1e6 × M × 4 B ≈ 64 MB at M = 16). Dense fp32 at 1e6 is out.
- Latency budget. A chat turn can afford ~10–50 ms of memory reads; that is brute-force dense at 1e5 (7–16 ms) or binary at 1e5 (60 ms, single pass) — no ANN index needed below ~2e5 items. At 1e6, use HNSW/IVF-PQ (sub-millisecond per query in FAISS-class libraries, but the only single-core literature figure is the FAISS wiki's "≈10× slower than multithreaded"), or keep hot items (≤2e5, chosen by FSRS retrievability) in RAM and the rest in SQLite BM25 (MIZAN's existing escalation path in D4).
- Writes are free. Append (O(d)), XOR bind (1.5 µs), outer-product fast-weight write (0.6 ms) and FSRS state update are all far below a turn's cost; one-shot write-on-experience is not a CPU problem.
- Training the slow store. At ~1,000 tok/s (25M Ruh, 2 threads, fp32 Adam) a full-CPU hour is ~3.6M tokens; at a 20% background CPU share that is ~0.7M tokens/hour ≈ 17M tokens/day; a 5M Ruh gives ~4.8× that. This is enough for nightly interleaved replay of the day's verified items plus a replay buffer (tens of thousands of sequences), and for continued pretraining of a few hundred million tokens over weeks — not for billion-token pretraining. A memory-efficient optimiser (SGD without momentum, which SDMLP recommends anyway, or 8-bit Adam) halves the 300 MB training footprint.
- Compute-equivalence reading of kNN-LM: the datastore substitutes for training tokens at a cost of one forward pass per stored token (≈2·N FLOP/token ≈ 50 MFLOP for the 25M Ruh, ≈0.5 ms/token on 2 threads → 1e6 tokens of datastore in ~8 min). This is the cheapest path to a Ruh that "knows" MIZAN's verified text.

### Gaps
- All costs above are from one container, not the production host; production numbers should be re-measured with `bench.py` under the real backend's memory pressure.
- No measured number for an int8 or PQ kernel on this box (the naive int8 path was slower than fp32); FAISS/hnswlib were not installed in the test container, so 1e6-scale ANN latency on 2 cores is unmeasured.
- No published accuracy for kNN-LM or Memory Layers at ≤100M parameters on Arabic/Urdu/Hinglish; the gains cited are English (Wikitext-103, NQ/TriviaQA).

## Key question 4 — Which combination gives a real neuroplastic loop on CPU, with equations and where each piece is proven

### Takeaway
The buildable loop is CLS with biology-grade gates: a one-shot sparse/binary episodic store written on every experience (Hopfield/SDM/VSA — proven), tagged with an eligibility trace and captured only by a third factor (STC/three-factor — proven in biology, analogy in software), a Gain × Need replay queue for nightly rehearsal (Mattar & Daw — proven to reproduce replay; prioritised replay proven in RL), consolidation into two slow stores — the existing non-parametric ones (Tamyiz counts, Hifz/Ta'allum templates, kNN datastore; proven, zero-GPU) and a small SDMLP/EWC-regularised Ruh trained by interleaved replay (proven at small scale on GPUs, feasible on CPU by the Q3 numbers) — reconsolidation on every recall (Nader → FSRS review + key update + supersession), interference-based forgetting by multiplicative scaling and FSRS power law, and a ~4-chunk working-memory injection. Titans/TTT/HOPE-style gradient memories are the speculative tier and should be a bounded experiment.

- Step 9 event segmentation by prediction error — [Zacks et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.133.2.273), [EM-LLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09450); unifying objective = minimise surprise/free energy — [Friston 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20068583/)
- Speculative tier: test-time gradient memories — [Titans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663), [TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620), [HOPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24695), [Atlas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23735) (proven ≥125M params on accelerators only)

### Inferences — the proposed loop ("Dhikr-Nawm loop"), with equations
Notation: an experience `x` (a user turn, a tool result, a verified program) is encoded as (a) a sparse key `h = TopK_k(P·φ(x))`, where `φ(x)` concatenates MIZAN's root-space HD vector (bind root⊗pattern per word, bundle; see Q2) with hashed character n-grams (Tamyiz's existing features), `P` is a fixed random projection, and `k` is small (pattern separation: Rolls; Top-K: SDMLP); (b) a value `v` = pointer to the verbatim span in the immutable log (the 18:49 "record" of the 28 Oct report) plus its provenance; (c) an FSRS state (S, D, last review) from Tadhkir.

1. **Write-on-experience (one-shot, always).** Append `(h, v)` to the episodic store `X` (cost: O(d), µs). Within the session, also write the fast-weight binding `A ← (1−α_t)·A + β_t·(v_t − A·h_t)·h_tᵀ` (delta rule of Schlag 2021; forgetting gate α_t as in Titans). Reads are `v̂ = X·softmax(β·Xᵀh)` (modern Hopfield / attention; 21 ms at 1e5 × 384 on 2 threads, Q3) or Hamming top-k on binary keys (59 ms at 1e5 × 10k bits). Proven: Hopfield/SDM theorems, kNN-LM. Status: *established*.
2. **Tag and capture (importance gate for consolidation).** Every new row gets an eligibility trace `e(t) = e₀·exp(−(t−t₀)/τ_e)` with τ_e on the order of a chat session (the biological tag lasts ~90 min; STC) and is marked *provisional*. The third factor `M(t) ∈ [−1, 1]` is MIZAN's own verdict stream: +1 when Sidq/Tathabbut/Hifz verifies an answer that used this row, +1 when the user says "remember", 0 when nothing happens, −1 when the user corrects or restates. Consolidation weight `c ← c + M(t)·e(t)`; a row is *captured* (eligible for Nawm) when `c ≥ c*` and *discarded at session end* when `c ≤ 0` and nothing referenced it (it stays in the raw log, so Mahw semantics are untouched). Learning rate from prediction error: `e₀ = σ(a·(−log P(x) − μ_user)/σ_user)`, with `−log P(x)` from Lughah/Qasd (and Ruh when present) — the dopamine/surprise analogue. Status: biology *established*; the software mapping is *my inference*; the surprise gate has an ML precedent (Titans).
3. **Working memory.** At most 4 chunks are injected per turn (profile, current task, ≤2 episodes), chosen by the D4 recall gate; the TCM context vector `c_t = ρ·c_{t−1} + β·e_t` is the "excitability" signal that also decides *allocation*: a new row is linked to the most active existing cluster (engram allocation by excitability). Status: Cowan/TCM *established*; allocation rule *inference*.
4. **Rehearsal by priority (Nawm, nightly, CPU idle time).** For each captured or unverified row compute `EVB_i = Gain_i × Need_i`. `Need_i` = FSRS retrievability at the next expected use `R(t_next)` times the row's long-run use frequency (the stationary-distribution reading of Mattar & Daw for offline replay) — MIZAN estimates it from access counts with ACT-R's `Σ t_j^{−d}`. `Gain_i` = expected change in behaviour if re-processed: `1 − p_verified` for unverified facts (Muhasaba), the Beta-posterior variance of a Hifz program's success, or `KL(answer_now ‖ answer_after_recheck)` when cheap to estimate. Replay in order of EVB with a fixed nightly budget (Mattar & Daw's 20-step budget; MIZAN's budget = the idle CPU-seconds Iqtisad allows), sampling `P(i) ∝ EVB_i^α` with importance weights `(N·P(i))^{−β}` (prioritised replay). What "replay" does: re-verify (Muhasaba), re-run Hifz programs as regression tests, re-derive templates (Ta'allum), and feed the slow stores in step 5. Status: Gain × Need *established* as a model of replay; its use as a verification scheduler is *inference*.
5. **Consolidation into slow stores (two tiers).** Tier A (non-parametric, proven, zero-GPU, already in MIZAN): Tamyiz counts (Lughah/Qasd), Hifz programs, Ta'allum wording, Tajriba lessons, plus a new **kNN datastore for Ruh**: after each verified text, one forward pass of Ruh stores (hidden state → next (root, pattern)) pairs; decoding uses `p = λ·p_kNN + (1−λ)·p_Ruh` (kNN-LM). Tier B (parametric, small): nightly interleaved SGD on the 5–25M Ruh (and on an SDMLP-style Top-K/L2/non-negative head for intent/entity classes) over a mixture of the day's captured items and a replay buffer sampled by EVB, with the EWC/SI anchor `(λ/2)·Σ_i F_i·(θ_i − θ*_i)²` toward the last promoted checkpoint; promotion through the existing Tajdid e-value gate (D7). **Schema-dependent speed** (Tse 2007): if the new item's key lies within the attraction basin of an existing cluster (`max_j cos(h, μ_j) ≥ τ_schema`), consolidate it in one night; otherwise require repeated capture across ≥2 independent episodes (D5's Beta rule). Budget (Q3): ~0.7–3.6M tokens/hour of CPU at 1,000 tok/s. Status: CLS/EWC/SDMLP/kNN-LM *established*; CPU budget *measured*; applying them to Ruh *untested*.
6. **Reconsolidation on recall.** Every recall is a write: FSRS review (Good if used in a verified answer, Again if the user restates — Tadhkir already does this), a key update toward the current context `h ← normalise(h + γ·c_t)` (Hebbian re-encoding; Nader), and, when the recalled fact is contradicted at recall, *supersession* (D4) rather than reinforcement. Status: Nader *established*; key update *inference*.
7. **Forgetting as interference and scaling.** Per item, FSRS's power law `R(t) = (1 + f·t/S)^{−w20}` (already in Tadhkir). Across items sharing a key neighbourhood, apply multiplicative scaling nightly: `c_i ← c_i · C_target / Σ_{j∈fan(i)} c_j` so that total consolidation weight per neighbourhood is bounded (Turrigiano; BCM's sliding threshold is the per-neuron version: new writes into a crowded neighbourhood need a higher surprise to be captured, `θ_fan = ⟨c⟩_fan`). Rows with `R(t) < r_min` and `c` below the scaled floor are archived (still searchable by BM25 escalation; never deleted — deletion is Mahw's alone). Status: *established* mechanisms; the per-neighbourhood scaling constant is to be fitted on MIZAN's own restatement/correction rates.
8. **Segmentation and the objective.** Episode boundaries where `−log P(message) > μ + γσ` (Zacks/EM-LLM; EW statistics already exist in `core/adaptive.py`). The whole loop minimises one quantity: expected future surprise, operationalised as *expected future base-model calls* (a call to Lisan is MIZAN admitting it could not predict the answer). This is the free-energy reading; it is the owner's goal stated as an objective, not a theorem.
9. **Speculative tier (bounded experiment, not a dependency).** A Titans-style surprise-gated MLP memory (depth 1–2, width 512: ≈1.6 MFLOP/token) could replace the fast-weight matrix in step 1 and be read by Ruh through MAG/MAC gating. Evidence is only at ≥340M parameters; a 2-week CPU experiment (train 5M Ruh ± memory on the same tokens, compare S-NIAH-style recall of verified facts) would decide it. HOPE's weak multi-key NIAH (29.4 vs 79.4) says MIZAN should keep exact retrieval (step 1) regardless.

What the loop gives that MIZAN does not have today: a *gate with a time window and a sign* (step 2) instead of "verified → keep", a *principled nightly queue* (step 4) instead of "re-check daily", a *small trainable neocortex that cannot forget catastrophically* (step 5 Tier B with SDMLP/EWC) instead of a rented-GPU LoRA, and *competitive forgetting* (step 7) instead of per-item decay alone.

### Gaps
- No paper implements exactly this loop; each step is proven in isolation (biology or ML), and the composition is my design. The strongest end-to-end analogues are Larimar (episodic controller + frozen LLM, 8–10× faster editing) and DualNet (fast/slow learners), neither on CPU.
- The constants τ_e, c*, τ_schema, C_target, α, β are unfitted; MIZAN's restatement and correction rates (the labels the 28 Oct report identified) are the data to fit them.
- Whether a 5–25M root-space Ruh plus a kNN datastore reaches useful Hinglish/Urdu/Arabic generation quality is unknown; kNN-LM gains are documented for English only.

## Key question 5 — What the CPU-only / zero-spend constraint changes in the 28 Oct designs (D4, D5, D9), and the Islamic grounding of the new pieces

### Takeaway
D9 (LoRA student on rented GPUs, Qwen3-8B-class) is invalidated by the zero-spend rule; its role passes to the loop's Tier A (non-parametric) plus a 5–25M CPU-trained Ruh with a kNN datastore, promoted by the same e-value gate. D4 and D5 stand, and gain three mechanisms: the tag-and-capture window, Gain × Need with the stationary-distribution reading for offline replay, and multiplicative scaling across a key neighbourhood. The Quranic and prophetic texts offer indications (ishara) for teach-then-test, for knowledge entering through perception and reflection, for the record that does not forget versus created memory that does, and for rehearsal; none is a ruling and none is a training target.
