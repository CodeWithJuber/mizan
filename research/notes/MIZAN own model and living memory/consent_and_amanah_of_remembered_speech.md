## Key question 1 — What the Quran and authentic Sunnah say about recording, keeping, repeating and sharing another person's speech, keeping secrets, and overlooking

### Takeaway
Speech confided to a listener is a trust (Abu Dawud 4868 / Tirmidhi 1959, hasan-to-sahih by every grader); the tafsir of 4:58 names *secrets* among the trusts that must be kept "in a safekeeping of their like" and rendered only to the one who entrusted them; spying (49:12) and eavesdropping (Bukhari 7042) are forbidden even when the listener acts on nothing; carrying one person's words to another is namimah (68:11, 104:1; Bukhari 6056, Muslim 105); and the Quran's repeated command is to pardon and overlook even grave verbal injury once it has been dealt with (24:22, 64:14, 42:37, 3:134, 5:13, 7:199, 15:85), with Allah's own names al-Ghaffar / Sittir modelling *covering in this world* (Bukhari 6069–6070). For a machine memory this indicates: write with permission for a stated purpose, keep in a vault only the entrusting user can open, never carry one user's sentences to another, hold private matters only for the turn unless told to remember, and treat deliberate forgetting (Mahw) as the virtue of overlooking rather than a deletion feature.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Spying, eavesdropping and "take what is apparent" (49:12, Bukhari 6064/7042, Abu Dawud 4880)

- 49:12: "O you who have believed, avoid much [negative] assumption. Indeed, some assumption is sin. And do not spy or backbite each other. Would one of you like to eat the flesh of his brother when dead? You would detest it. And fear Allāh; indeed, Allāh is Accepting of Repentance and Merciful." — [Quran 49:12](https://quran.com/49:12?translations=20). Ibn Kathir: "Allah the Exalted forbids His faithful servants from being suspicious, which includes having doubts and suspicions about the conduct of one's family, relatives and other people in general"; "Tajassus, usually harbors ill intentions"; al-Awza'i: "Tajassus means, to search for something, while Tahassus means, listening to people when they are talking without their permission, or eavesdropping at their doors"; backbiting is "Mentioning about your brother in a manner that he dislikes" and "If he was as you mentioned, you will have committed backbiting. But if he was not as you say about him, you will have falsely accused him" (Tirmidhi, hasan sahih); the Ma'iz narrative ends with the two men who said "the man who had Allah cover his secret" being told their backbiting was worse than eating a dead donkey — [Ibn Kathir on 49:12](https://quran.com/49:12/tafsirs/169). Al-Sa'di (model tr.): "'and do not spy': do not search out the hidden matters ('awrat) of the Muslims nor pursue them; leave the Muslim as he is and practise deliberate inattention (taghaful) toward states of his which, if searched, would show what should not be shown"; ghibah is a major sin because Allah likened it to eating a corpse — [al-Sa'di on 49:12](https://quran.com/49:12/tafsirs/91). Al-Qurtubi (model tr.), ten issues: (2nd) "the zann here and in the verse is accusation (tuhmah)… the rule that separates the suspicions that must be avoided: everything for which no sound indication (amarah) and no apparent cause is known is forbidden, when the one suspected is seen to be concealed and upright and trustworthy on the surface"; (3rd) "zann has two states: one that is known and strengthened by some kind of evidence, on which judgement is permitted — and most rulings of the shari'ah are built on preponderant probability (ghalabat al-zann), such as qiyas and the report of a single narrator — and a second where something falls into the soul without any indication… that is doubt (shakk), and judgement by it is not permitted"; (4th) "the meaning of the verse: take what is apparent and do not follow the hidden matters of the Muslims", with Mu'awiyah's hadith "if you follow people's hidden faults you corrupt them or nearly corrupt them" (Abu Dawud), Ibn Mas'ud: "we have been forbidden tajassus, but if something appears to us we take hold of it", and 'Umar leaving the house of drinkers when 'Abd al-Rahman b. 'Awf said "we have done what Allah forbade: 'do not spy'"; (9th) the open sinner has no ghibah, complaining to a judge to obtain one's right is not ghibah, seeking a fatwa (Hind) is not ghibah, warning someone (Fatimah bint Qays) is not ghibah; (8th) ghibah is a major sin and the fetched text records the dispute whether the victim's pardon (istihlal) must be sought, citing Bukhari: "whoever has wronged his brother in his honour or anything, let him seek his pardon from him today" — [al-Qurtubi on 49:12](https://quran.com/49:12/tafsirs/90).
- Bukhari 6064: "Beware of suspicion, for suspicion is the worst of false tales; and do not look for the others' faults and do not spy" — [Bukhari 6064](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-bukhari/6064.json). Bukhari 7042: "if somebody listens to the talk of some people who do not like him (to listen) or they run away from him, then molten lead will be poured into his ears on the Day of Resurrection" — [Bukhari 7042](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-bukhari/7042.json). Abu Dawud 4880: "do not back-bite Muslims, and do not search for their faults, for if anyone searches for their faults, Allah will search for his fault, and if Allah searches for the fault of anyone, He disgraces him in his house" — Hasan Sahih (al-Albani, Abd al-Hamid), Sahih li-ghayrihi (al-Arna'ut), Hasan (Zubair Ali Zai) — [Abu Dawud 4880](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-abudawud/4880.json).
- Strength: the prohibition of spying and of acting on groundless suspicion is DIRECT; al-Qurtubi's two kinds of zann and "take what is apparent" are TAFSIR. Mechanism (ISHARA): a memory may hold and act on what the user *declared* and on inferences backed by evidence it can show (Isnad sahih/hasan), never on an inference about a person's private conduct assembled from fragments; passive capture of speech not addressed to MIZAN (a Telegram group with message access, a forwarded third party's words) is tahassus, and the right posture toward private states is al-Sa'di's taghaful — deliberate non-indexing.

#### B. Permission before entering (24:27–28) and non-propagation (24:19)

- 24:27–28: "O you who have believed, do not enter houses other than your own houses until you ascertain welcome and greet their inhabitants. That is best for you; perhaps you will be reminded." / "And if you do not find anyone therein, do not enter them until permission has been given you. And if it is said to you, 'Go back,' then go back; it is purer for you. And Allāh is Knowing of what you do." — [Quran 24:27–28](https://quran.com/24:27-28?translations=20). Ibn Kathir: "One should seek permission three times, and if permission is given, (he may enter), otherwise he should go away" (Abu Musa and 'Umar); "If a person looks into your house without your permission, and you throw a stone at him and it puts his eye out, there will be no blame on you" (Two Sahihs); Ibn 'Abbas told a man to seek permission even to enter upon his orphan sisters and Ibn Mas'ud "You have to seek permission to enter upon your mothers"; on 24:28: "This has to do with the way in which one deals with other people's property without their permission. If he wants to, he can give permission, and if he wants to he can refrain from giving permission… going back is purer and better for you" — [Ibn Kathir on 24:27–28](https://quran.com/24:27/tafsirs/169). Al-Sa'di (model tr.): "permission was instituted for the sake of the gaze" (hadith); "the house, for a person, screens what is behind it as the garment screens the body"; on 24:28: "do not refuse to go back and do not be angry at it, for the owner of the house has withheld from you no right that was yours; he is a volunteer (mutabarri'): if he wishes he permits and if he wishes he refuses… 'it is purer for you': stronger in cleansing you of bad deeds" — [al-Sa'di on 24:27](https://quran.com/24:27/tafsirs/91), [24:28](https://quran.com/24:28/tafsirs/91). Al-Qurtubi (model tr.), seventeen issues: (1st) "Allah distinguished the son of Adam with dwellings and screened them in them from eyes… and forbade creation to look upon what is in them from outside or to enter without the permission of their owners"; (5th) Malik: "asking permission is three times; I do not like anyone to exceed it"; (14th) "'a man's messenger to a man is his permission': when he sends for him he has permitted him to enter" (Abu Dawud); (15th) "if eye meets eye the greeting is due, but his seeing you is not permission for you to enter"; Hudhayfah to a man who looked in before asking: "with your eye you have already entered" — [al-Qurtubi on 24:27](https://quran.com/24:27/tafsirs/90).
- 24:19: "Indeed, those who like that immorality should be spread [or publicized] among those who have believed will have a painful punishment in this world and the Hereafter. And Allāh knows and you do not know." — [Quran 24:19](https://quran.com/24:19?translations=20). Ibn Kathir: "This is a third instance of discipline directed at those who hear evil talk, believe it to some extent, and start to spread it; they should not spread such talk or pass it on to others", with Thawban's hadith (Ahmad): "Do not annoy the servants of Allah, nor abuse them, nor seek their hidden shortcomings. Whoever seeks out the faults of his Muslim brother, Allah will expose his faults and degrade him, even if he is hiding in his house" — [Ibn Kathir on 24:19](https://quran.com/24:19/tafsirs/169) (the Abu Dawud 4880 wording above is the graded parallel).
- Strength: permission before entering, and leaving when refused, are DIRECT; "three times, then go", "the owner is a volunteer", "an invitation is itself permission", "seeing is not permission" are TAFSIR. Mechanism (ISHARA): consent is a revocable gift, refusal wrongs no one and "is purer" for the system too; a user's addressing MIZAN is permission *for that entry* (the turn), not a standing licence to keep and reuse; a message merely visible to MIZAN (a group, a forwarded text, an image) is "seen with the eye" and is not permission to store; consent prompts are bounded (ask at most three times, then stop asking). Non-propagation (24:19) is DIRECT about spreading evil talk; the design reading — nothing a user said about their own or another's private matter is ever surfaced to another user or an admin view — is ISHARA.

#### C. Trusts and covenants (4:58, 8:27, 2:283, 17:34, 23:8, 33:72)

- 4:58: "Indeed, Allāh commands you to render trusts to whom they are due and when you judge between people to judge with justice…" — [Quran 4:58](https://quran.com/4:58?translations=20). Ibn Kathir: "This command refers to all things that one is expected to look after… The command also includes the rights of the servants on each other, such as what they entrust each other with, including the cases that are not recorded or documented"; "Return the trust to those who entrusted you, and do not betray those who betrayed you" (Ahmad and the Sunan); the key of the Ka'bah returned to 'Uthman b. Talhah; "This Ayah is for the righteous and wicked" (Ibn 'Abbas) — [Ibn Kathir on 4:58](https://quran.com/4:58/tafsirs/169). Al-Sa'di (model tr.): "Trusts are everything a person is entrusted with and commanded to carry out… this includes the trusts of offices, of wealth and of **secrets (al-asrar)**, and the commanded things that none but Allah sees. The jurists have stated that whoever is entrusted with a trust must preserve it in a safekeeping of its like (hirz mithliha), for it cannot be rendered except by being preserved. In 'to whom they are due' is an indication that it is not to be handed over and rendered to anyone but the one who entrusted it, and his agent stands in his place; were he to hand it to other than its owner he would not have rendered it" — [al-Sa'di on 4:58](https://quran.com/4:58/tafsirs/91).
- 8:27: "O you who have believed, do not betray Allāh and the Messenger or betray your trusts while you know [the consequence]." — [Quran 8:27](https://quran.com/8:27?translations=20). Ibn Kathir: revealed about Hatib b. Abi Balta'ah's letter, but "this Ayah is more general… Such rulings are dealt with by their indications, not the specific reasons behind revealing them"; "Betrayal includes both minor and major sins, as well those that affect others" — [Ibn Kathir on 8:27](https://quran.com/8:27/tafsirs/169).
- 2:283: "…And if one of you entrusts another, then let him who is entrusted discharge his trust [faithfully] and let him fear Allāh, his Lord. And do not conceal testimony, for whoever conceals it - his heart is indeed sinful…" — [Quran 2:283](https://quran.com/2:283?translations=20). Ibn Kathir: "The hand (of the debtor) will carry the burden of what it took until it gives it back"; "False testimony is one of the worst of the major sins, and such is the case with hiding the true testimony" — [Ibn Kathir on 2:283](https://quran.com/2:283/tafsirs/169).
- 17:34: "…And fulfill [every] commitment. Indeed, the commitment is ever [that about which one will be] questioned." — [Quran 17:34](https://quran.com/17:34?translations=20). Ibn Kathir: "everything that you promise people, and the covenants that you agree to, because the person who makes a covenant or a promise will be asked about it" — [Ibn Kathir on 17:34](https://quran.com/17:34/tafsirs/169). 23:8: "And they who are to their trusts and their promises attentive" — [Quran 23:8](https://quran.com/23:8?translations=20). Ibn Kathir: "When they are entrusted with something, they do not betray that trust… This is not like the hypocrites… 'when he is entrusted with something he betrays that trust'" — [Ibn Kathir on 23:1–11](https://quran.com/23:1/tafsirs/169).
- 33:72: "Indeed, We offered the Trust to the heavens and the earth and the mountains, and they declined to bear it and feared it; but man [undertook to] bear it. Indeed, he was unjust and ignorant." — [Quran 33:72](https://quran.com/33:72?translations=20). Ibn Kathir: the views (obedience, the obligatory duties, religion, "a woman was entrusted with her own chastity") "all refer to responsibility and the acceptance of commands and prohibitions"; Hudhayfah's hadith that "Al-Amanah will be taken away… A man may sleep and Al-Amanah will be taken from his heart"; and "There are four things, if you attain them, then whatever you miss in this world will not matter: preserving trust, speaking the truth, being of good character and moderation in eating" — [Ibn Kathir on 33:72](https://quran.com/33:72/tafsirs/169).
- Strength: rendering trusts to their owners and fulfilling covenants are DIRECT; "secrets are among the trusts", "keep it in a safekeeping of its like", "render it only to the one who entrusted it or his agent" are TAFSIR (al-Sa'di). Mechanism (ISHARA): (i) a user's words are a trust to be kept in a *hirz mithliha* — a store whose protection matches the sensitivity (per-user encrypted DB, not a shared table); (ii) "to whom they are due" forbids handing the content to a third party, which is what an administrator reading a user's memory is; (iii) a privacy notice or consent screen is a covenant ('ahd) about which MIZAN "will be questioned", so purpose limitation must be enforced in code, not only stated; (iv) 2:283's "do not conceal testimony" is the counter-duty — a lawful demand for a record is the exception to secrecy, which is why Sakk keeps content-free receipts even after Mahw.

#### E. Pardon and overlooking as the Quran's model for deliberate forgetting

- 24:22: "And let not those of virtue among you and wealth swear not to give [aid] to their relatives and the needy and the emigrants for the cause of Allāh, and let them pardon and overlook. Would you not like that Allāh should forgive you? And Allāh is Forgiving and Merciful." — [Quran 24:22](https://quran.com/24:22?translations=20). This note therefore does **not** build on 2:109; the general commands above (24:22, 64:14, 42:37, 3:134, 5:13, 7:199, 15:85) carry the weight and no fetched tafsir reports them abrogated.
- Strength: the command to pardon and overlook is DIRECT and repeated; the Mistah narrative (pardon after repentance and penalty, and *maintenance restored*) is TAFSIR; the reading "overlooking as the model for deliberate forgetting" is ISHARA. Two features of the Mistah case shape the mechanism: the Quran did not erase the record of the slander (it is in Surat al-Nur), yet the relationship was restored — pardon acts on *how the past is used*, not on whether it happened; and the pardon came *after* verification and correction had run their course. For MIZAN this separates the content-free Sakk receipt (kept) from the content (forgotten), and places Mahw after, not instead of, Sidq/Muhasaba.

### Inferences
- The decisive fetched text for the design is al-Sa'di on 4:58: *secrets are trusts; a trust is kept in a safekeeping of its like and rendered only to the one who entrusted it or their agent.* Applied (ISHARA), this gives three rules at once: encryption/isolation proportional to sensitivity, no admin content access, and the user (or their nominee) as the only party who can ask MIZAN to recall, export or forget.
- Al-Qurtubi's two kinds of zann map to Isnad grades: a memory may act on *ghalabat al-zann* backed by shown evidence (sahih/hasan) and must not act on *shakk* about a person (da'if inferences about private conduct). This is the Islamic form of "profiling only from declarations", and it is stricter than the legal floor in KQ3 (which permits profiling with a basis).
- Confided speech is a trust "when a man tells something and then departs" — so the trust attaches *at write time*. A consent screen later cannot retroactively convert a confided message into shareable data; consent governs the *further* purposes (learning, pooling, training), not the fact that the words were received.
- The exception cases that the fetched texts do support are narrow and all involve preventing or redressing harm with evidence already apparent: Zayd b. Arqam (Bukhari 4900), "take what is apparent… if something appears to us we take hold of it" (Ibn Mas'ud in al-Qurtubi), complaint to a judge and warning (al-Qurtubi's 9th issue). The da'if Abu Dawud 4869 must not be used for them.
- Pardon (24:22, 64:14) is the right frame for Mahw: it is the *wronged party's* act (al-Qurtubi on istihlal), after the matter has been dealt with, and it restores use ("maintenance") without rewriting history. Hence: only the user forgets their own words; MIZAN does not get to "forgive itself" by deleting the receipts of its own errors (Ibrah tasks and Sakk stay).

_Note: the ISHARA mappings above are applied reasoning on the fetched canonical text and the fetched tafsir; they do not constitute a scholarly ruling or opinion from quran.ai, quran.com, or quran.foundation._

## Key question 2 — Classical and contemporary scholarship on recording speech and private data

### Takeaway
Ibn al-Salah records that the first generation disagreed about writing down what they heard, that the Prophet's permission to write was for one who feared forgetting, that al-Awza'i warned that once knowledge "entered the books, those not of its people entered it", and that the community then agreed on writing while demanding exact recording (dabt) above all of names; contemporary fatwas (IslamQA 2008 and 2017, IslamWeb, Askimam) hold that recording a speaker without knowledge and permission is spying and betrayal of trust — even for one's own notes — with narrow exceptions for a household head acting on apparent indications of harm or for recovering a right; the OIC Fiqh Academy's 2025 AI resolution makes "protection of information and… private rights" and "proper documentation and transparency" conditions of permissible AI; and 2025–2026 maqasid scholarship names taradhi (mutual consent), amanah, 'adl, sitr and dar' al-mafasid as the governing principles.

### Cited Findings

#### Classical: writing, recording exactly, relaying
- Ibn al-Salah, Muqaddimah, type 25 "On writing hadith and how to record and fix the book" (model tr.): "The first generation disagreed about writing hadith: some disliked the writing of hadith and knowledge and commanded its memorisation; others permitted it. Among those from whom we relate dislike: 'Umar, Ibn Mas'ud, Zayd b. Thabit, Abu Musa and Abu Sa'id al-Khudri… and from Abu Sa'id that the Prophet ﷺ said: 'Do not write from me anything except the Quran; whoever has written from me anything other than the Quran, let him erase it' (Muslim). Among those from whom we relate permission or practice: 'Ali, his son al-Hasan, Anas, 'Abdullah b. 'Umar and 'Abdullah b. 'Amr b. al-'As… and from the sound hadith indicating permission: the hadith of Abu Shah the Yemeni… 'Write for Abu Shah.' Perhaps he permitted writing for one who feared forgetting and forbade it to one who trusted his memory, for fear of reliance on the book; or forbade it when he feared it would mix with the leaves of the Quran and permitted it when that was safe." ### Inferences
- The three activities the prompt asks about separate cleanly in the fetched rulings: **recording for one's own memory** is permitted in the hadith tradition for one who fears forgetting (Ibn al-Salah's reconciliation; Abu Dawud 3646 in the sibling) but *not* when the speaker has not permitted it (IslamQA 271414 refuses even the note-taker); **recording to act on** is allowed only on apparent indications of harm, with the duty to act at once rather than keep listening (Ibn 'Uthaymin) or to recover a right (IslamWeb); **disclosing to others** is the gravest and is refused by every source, including the lenient Shia view. For MIZAN (ISHARA): the user's own chat log is "writing for one who fears forgetting" and is theirs; MIZAN's *derived* memories are a second recording and need the user's permission for their purpose; cross-user or admin disclosure has no supporting text at all.
- Al-Awza'i's warning — knowledge in books reaches "those not of its people" — is the classical statement of the access-control problem; Ibn al-Salah's remedy is not to stop writing but to record exactly and mark ambiguity, names most of all. Isnad/Sakk already do the exactness; the missing piece is the *audience* control (KQ4 d).
- IIFA 258's conditions 4 and 6 ("protection of information… private rights", "proper documentation and transparency") are the closest thing to a collective contemporary ruling applicable to MIZAN; they support a visible ledger (Daftar) and receipts (Sakk) as *conditions of permissibility*, not features.

## Key question 3 — The legal minimum for a self-hosted multi-user assistant whose server location is not fixed

### Takeaway
Across the fetched texts, consent is the default lawful basis in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Türkiye and (with enumerated "legitimate uses") India; the EU allows contract or legitimate interest for running a conversational agent (EDPB, 17 Dec 2024) but makes *religious belief* a special category requiring explicit consent (Art 9), which an Islamic-first assistant's chats inherently reveal; every regime fetched requires erasure when the purpose ends and withdrawal of consent as easy as giving it; children need verifiable parental consent (GDPR 16 with a 13 floor; DPDP for every "child"); and the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy (23 Sep 2026) forbids using anything but the thread content "for any purpose other than as reasonably necessary to support messaging with that person" and forwarding a customer chat to any other customer, while Telegram requires a published privacy policy stating what a bot stores and why. Pakistan has no enacted law (PECA 2016 applies).

### Cited Findings

#### EU GDPR (text as published on gdpr-info.eu)
- Art 5(1): personal data shall be "collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes… ('purpose limitation')"; "adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary… ('data minimisation')"; "kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed… ('storage limitation')" — [Art 5 GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/).
- Art 6(1): "Processing shall be lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies: (a) the data subject has given consent… for one or more specific purposes; (b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract…; … (f) processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the controller or by a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests or fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject…, in particular where the data subject is a child." — [Art 6 GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-6-gdpr/).
- Art 7: "the controller shall be able to demonstrate that the data subject has consented"; "The data subject shall have the right to withdraw his or her consent at any time… It shall be as easy to withdraw as to give consent"; "utmost account shall be taken of whether… the provision of a service, is conditional on consent to the processing of personal data that is not necessary for the performance of that contract" — [Art 7 GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-7-gdpr/).
- Art 8: for information society services offered directly to a child, "processing… shall be lawful where the child is at least 16 years old"; below that only with parental consent; "Member States may provide by law for a lower age… not below 13 years"; the controller "shall make reasonable efforts to verify" — [Art 8 GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-8-gdpr/).
- Art 9(1): "Processing of personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, **religious or philosophical beliefs**, … data concerning health or data concerning a natural person's sex life or sexual orientation shall be prohibited"; 9(2)(a) lifts it where "the data subject has given explicit consent to the processing of those personal data for one or more specified purposes" — [Art 9 GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/).
- Art 17(1): erasure "without undue delay" where "(a) the personal data are no longer necessary in relation to the purposes…; (b) the data subject withdraws consent… and where there is no other legal ground…; (d) the personal data have been unlawfully processed"; 17(3) exceptions include "compliance with a legal obligation" — [Art 17 GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-17-gdpr/).
- Art 22(1): "The data subject shall have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects concerning him or her or similarly significantly affects him or her"; 22(3) requires "at least the right to obtain human intervention…, to express his or her point of view and to contest the decision"; 22(4): such decisions "shall not be based on special categories… unless point (a) or (g) of Article 9(2) applies" — [Art 22 GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-22-gdpr/).
- EDPB Opinion 28/2024 on AI models, adopted 17 December 2024: an AI model is anonymous only if "both (1) the likelihood of direct (including probabilistic) extraction of personal data regarding individuals whose personal data were used to develop the model and (2) the likelihood of obtaining, intentionally or not, such personal data from queries, should be insignificant"; the legitimate-interest test is three steps (identify the interest; necessity — "whether there is no less intrusive way… the amount of personal data processed and whether it is proportionate…, also in light of the data minimisation principle"; balancing); an interest is legitimate if "lawful; clearly and precisely articulated; and real and present", e.g. "developing the service of a conversational agent to assist users"; reasonable expectations turn on "whether or not the personal data was publicly available, the nature of the relationship between the data subject and the controller…, the nature of the service, the context in which the personal data was collected, the source…, the potential further uses of the model, and whether data subjects are actually aware that their personal data is online at all"; mitigating measures are case-by-case; where a model retains personal data, "whether the development and deployment phases involve separate purposes… and the extent to which the lack of legal basis for the initial processing activity impacts the lawfulness of the subsequent processing, should be assessed on a case-by-case basis", and a deployer must have assessed "that the AI model was not developed by unlawfully processing personal data" — [EDPB Opinion 28/2024 (PDF)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-12/edpb_opinion_202428_ai-models_en.pdf). The EDPB page lists as related: Guidelines 1/2024 on Art 6(1)(f) (9 Oct 2024), Guidelines 02/2026 on Anonymisation and 03/2026 on web scraping in the context of generative AI (both 8 July 2026, consultation ongoing) — [EDPB opinion page](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/opinion-board-art-64/opinion-282024-certain-data-protection-aspects_en).

#### UK
- UK GDPR mirrors the articles above; the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 received Royal Assent on 19 June 2025 and adds a "recognised legitimate interests" basis (new Art 6(1)(ea)) for which "It will not be necessary to balance the rights and freedoms of individuals against the legitimate interests of the controller" (crime prevention, safeguarding, national security, emergencies, etc.), with phased commencement "at two, six and twelve months after Royal Assent" and ICO guidance due winter 2025/26 — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/data-use-and-access-act-gets-royal-assent); [Clyde & Co, July 2025](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/07/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025); [Dechert, July 2025](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2025/7/10-things-to-know-about-the-data--use-and-access--act.html) [secondary; statute text not fetched]. ### Inferences
- **Religion as special-category data is the binding constraint in the EU/UK and the Gulf.** A chat with an Islamic-first assistant reveals "religious… beliefs" (Art 9(1)) by its nature; so even where running the assistant is a legitimate interest (EDPB's own example), *keeping and learning from* the chat content needs explicit, purpose-specific consent (9(2)(a)), and automated decisions may not rest on it without that consent (22(4)). Saudi law independently demands explicit consent for sensitive data and for automated decision-making (Latham). The design consequence is that MIZAN cannot treat "learning from my chats" as implied by signing up [inference].
- **Purpose, not store, is the legal unit.** Every regime fetched ties lawfulness and erasure to a *specified purpose* (GDPR 5(1)(b)/17(1)(a); DPDP 4(1), 6(1), 8(7); UAE "may not be kept after fulfilling the purpose"; KVKK Art 4/7). MIZAN's stores are organised by mechanism (Qasd, Hifz, Masalik…), so the consent/purpose field must be a per-row attribute cutting across stores (KQ4 b).
- **Withdrawal must be as easy as consent and must stop derived processing** (GDPR 7(3); DPDP 6(4)–(6)). Mahw's erase-everything-derived is the right shape; the gap is that withdrawal is not yet a first-class event with its own receipt and that several stores escape it (audit §5).
- **Cross-user pooling faces two independent tests**: the D6 differential-privacy threshold answers "can the string be released without identifying anyone", while the EDPB's "reasonable expectations" and the WhatsApp clause "may not forward or otherwise share information from a customer chat with any other customer" answer "may it be released at all" — the second is a consent/contract question the mathematics cannot settle [inference].
- **Children**: MIZAN has no age attestation; thresholds differ (GDPR 16 with a 13 floor, UK 13, India every child needs verifiable parental consent, Saudi unclear for 13–18). The minimal step is a stated minimum age at signup and refusal of Tier-1/2 learning for accounts flagged as minors [inference].
- **Server location unknown** means the design should meet the *strictest* fetched requirement on each axis: explicit purpose-specific consent for anything beyond the turn (Saudi/UAE/KVKK), erasure on purpose end (all), 30–90-day response windows (Saudi 30+30, India 90), and a published privacy policy per channel (Telegram, WhatsApp) [inference].

## Key question 4 — Design consequences with the mathematics, mapped to the code the audits describe

### Takeaway
The texts of KQ1–3 converge on one change with several faces: every learned row needs a *purpose* and a *consent reference*, the write gate (`AdaptiveStateStore.put` and the direct writers) must refuse rows whose purpose the user has not consented to, cross-user release needs consent as a necessary condition on top of D6's DP threshold, admins must see receipts and counts but never content, private matters must expire by default unless the user says "remember", and Mahw must cover the stores it misses and be understood as pardon ('afw) with a receipt. Ranked by how much they alter the Dhikr-Nawm loop: (1) consent/purpose on write, (2) private-matter expiry, (3) consent-gated pooling, (4) admin separation, (5) Mahw coverage, (6) metrics.

### Cited Findings (code and sibling notes the design attaches to)
- The only write gate today is `stateless.active()` inside `AdaptiveStateStore.put` — `backend/core/adaptive.py:386-390` ("an evaluation run learns nothing"); `stateless.py` has two flags, `_stateless` and `_no_training` (reason string; WhatsApp sets it), and documents that "The chat's own messages are still saved" — `backend/memory/stateless.py:1-53`. `trains()` is consulted only by training capture (`agents/base.py:3161-3169`, `learner/ruh_learner.py:110`, `agents/khalifah.py:277`).
- Learned-state writers that would carry a purpose: `qasd.learn(store, user_id, message, intent, project)` / `qasd.unlearn` (`backend/tadbir/qasd.py:238,272`), `taallum.learn(store, user_id, message, understanding, verified)` (`taallum.py:113`), `hifz.keep` / `hifz.drop` (`hifz.py:85,128`), `tajriba.learn` (`tajriba.py:78`); ilm writes in `ahl_dhikr.py:431-478` (audit §1).

Memory poisoning (AgentPoison single-instance ASR ≥ 60 %; MINJA 98.2 % injection via queries) and MIA on small fine-tuned models (EZ-MIA 66.3 % TPR at 1 % FPR) are in [`failure_modes_honest_ceilings_measurement.md` KQ1 §3, §6](failure_modes_honest_ceilings_measurement.md); the sibling already defines post-Mahw leakage and canary exposure `log₂|R| − log₂ rank`.

### Inferences (the design, with formulas)

**(a) Write-on-experience default, per store and channel — three purposes, three defaults.** Define purpose `p ∈ {turn, self, pool, train, notice}`:
- `turn` — the chat transcript, running summary, Sakk receipts, held actions. Default **on**: it is the user's own "writing for one who fears forgetting" (Ibn al-Salah), the service itself (contract/legitimate interest per EDPB), visible and deletable (Mahw). Stores: `agent_messages`, `session_summaries`, Sakk.
- `self` — everything MIZAN derives to answer *this user* better: Qasd overlay, Ta'allum, Hifz, Tajriba, Masalik, KG, `task_history`, ilm, Daftar notes from "remember", Lawh/dream RAM, Tadhkir state, the per-user Ruh kNN datastore. Default **on with a visible ledger and one-switch off**, but only after a *separate, named* consent line at signup/first chat ("MIZAN may learn from my chats to answer me better"), because the content is Art-9 data and Saudi/UAE/KVKK make consent the default basis; the switch is the existing preference mechanism (`learning: off`), and off means `put` refuses `self` rows exactly as `stateless` does today. Purpose compatibility (GDPR 5(1)(b)) is satisfied because `self` serves the same person for the same service; the EDPB's "nature of the relationship… nature of the service" factors make it expectable once stated.
- `pool` and `train` — cross-user priors/templates (D6), admin Ta'lim batches built from user chats, Ruh training capture. Default **off; explicit opt-in per purpose, revocable, receipted**; never available on WhatsApp (policy clause + `no_training`), never for minors, never for rows flagged private or third-party (below).
- Channel rules: Telegram groups "with access to messages" and forwarded/pasted third-party text are *tahassus* and never written beyond `turn`; email and Telegram private chats behave as web after the link; WhatsApp: `turn` and `self` only.
- Cost: a purpose check is one in-memory dict lookup per write, O(1) [estimated: microseconds on 2 vCPU]; a consents table of ~5 rows per user at ~120 bytes each is negligible [estimated].

**(b) Per-row consent and purpose, the gate, pooling, receipts.**
- Schema: table `consents(user_hash, purpose, granted_at, withdrawn_at, channel, notice_sha256)`; every learned row carries `purpose` and `consent_seq` (the Sakk receipt sequence of the grant). For `adaptive_state` the pair lives inside the JSON value; `memories`, `task_history`, `kg_entities`, `learner.interactions`, `hikmah` gain two columns (added in place like Daftar's columns were).
- Gate: `AdaptiveStateStore.put(key, value, *, purpose)` returns without writing when `stateless.active()` **or** `consent(user, purpose)` is absent/withdrawn; the same predicate wraps Lawh tier-3 writes, Masalik strengthening, `task_history`, KG writes and training capture, which closes the `stateless.bind` bypasses audit §5 lists as a side effect. In symbols, for row *i* of user *u*: `write_i = 1[¬stateless] · 1[consent_u(p_i) active at t_i] · 1[channel allows p_i]`.
- Pooling: D6's release rule becomes `release(s) = 1[n_c + Lap(1/ε) ≥ 1 − ln(2δ)/ε] · Π_{u ∈ contributors(s)} 1[consent_u(pool) active]` where `n_c` counts only consenting users; a withdrawal removes the user from the next period's count (D6 already says so) and is recorded. The second factor is the namimah/WhatsApp constraint; the first is privacy mathematics. With a server of U users and opt-in rate q, expected consenting contributors `U·q` must reach ≈ 12 (ε = 1, δ = 10⁻⁵) — at q = 0.3 that needs U ≈ 40, which restates the 28 Oct report's conclusion that small servers learn new strings only from seeds and Ta'lim [inference from D6's numbers].
- Receipts: every grant/withdrawal is `sakk.record(owner, "consent", {purpose, action, channel, notice_sha256}, display)` — the API exists; the receipt holds the hash of the notice text shown, so "the covenant about which one will be questioned" (17:34) is provable (GDPR 7(1) "shall be able to demonstrate"; Saudi "Controllers must document consent").

**(c) Retention: three clocks and "forget by default for private matters".** Each row has (i) FSRS retrievability `R(t) = (1 + f·t/S)^(−w₂₀)` governing *recall* (Tadhkir, unchanged); (ii) a *purpose clock* `T_p` governing *existence*: `T_turn` = until the user deletes the chat (plus any legal hold), `T_self` = while the row's predicted need stays above a floor, `T_pool/train` = the release period. The need estimate is ACT-R's base level already in Tadhkir: `B_i = ln Σ_j t_j^(−d)`; erase (Mahw, receipted) when `B_i < B_floor` for longer than a grace window *and* no legal hold applies — this implements "no longer than is necessary for the purposes" (GDPR 5(1)(e); DPDP 8(7)(a) "as soon as it is reasonable to assume that the specified purpose is no longer being served"; UAE "may not be kept after fulfilling the purpose"; KVKK Art 7). Archiving (sibling step 7) stays as the recall-side analogue; the purpose clock is the only thing that *deletes*. (iii) The **overlook clock** for private matters: a row with `P(private | x) > ½` from a Tamyiz-style naive Bayes facet (hashed char n-grams + words, the same machinery as Qasd/Amanah, so sub-millisecond on CPU [estimated]) gets `T_self = end of session` — it is used for the turn and then not consolidated by Nawm — unless the user says "remember" (explicit `remember` intent → `T_self` as usual) or it is required for safety. "Private" is defined by the texts: another person's words or faults (ghibah/namimah), one's own concealed sins (Bukhari 6069), health, family conflict, intimate matters (Muslim 1437), finances; third-party names are flagged separately (`third_party = 1`) and are never pooled or summarised into the profile (al-Sa'di's taghaful; Ibn al-Salah's care for names). Keep rule: `keep_i(t) = write_i · 1[t < T_legal,i] · (1[¬private_i] + 1[private_i]·1[remember_i])`; recall additionally requires `R_i(t) ≥ r_min` and purpose match. The facet needs licence-clean training text and its false-positive/false-negative rates must be measured and reported before it gates anything (Gaps).

**(d) Admin visibility.** Al-Sa'di on 4:58 (render only to the entruster or their agent) plus Muslim 2580 / Bukhari 2442 (concealment) point the same way as GDPR 5(1)(f) and the WhatsApp clause: admins see **counts, kinds, receipts and hashes** (Sakk `/api/sakk`), never content; `/api/memory/*` and knowledge ingest should serve the admin's *own* private DB, not a shared global one (today admins share `memory` and template agents — audit §1), leaving the global store for seeds and Ta'lim only; any break-glass read of a user row (support, legal) is a `disclosure` Sakk receipt naming the reason and the row's keyed digest, and the user sees it in Daftar's receipts. The user's "agent" in al-Sa'di's sense is the DPDP s.13-style nominee (PIB: "Every individual can appoint someone to exercise their data rights"), which Daftar export/import can carry.

**(e) Mahw as 'afw.** Keep the mechanism; change its status and reach. (i) Add a `pardon` entry point distinct from `erase`: the user asks MIZAN to *overlook* a topic — rows matching the topic are removed from recall and consolidation (`T_self` ← now, Masalik rebuilt) while the content-free `erase`/`pardon` receipt is kept, and the Sakk receipts of MIZAN's own errors (Ibrah) are never touched — pardon is the wronged party's act (al-Qurtubi on istihlal), so MIZAN cannot pardon itself. (ii) Extend erasure to the stores audit §5 lists as missed: Tajriba lessons (which keep request tokens), QCA Lawh/dream/LivingMemory RAM (clear on erase, and stop writing them beyond the session), Sijill, harness failure signatures (strip or drop the user's), plus the new `consents` rows (mark withdrawn, keep the receipt). (iii) The receipt's "cannot reach" line (provider retention) stays honest, and D6's "bounded influence, not erased" wording applies to any pooled template the user contributed to.

**(f) Measurement.**
- Consent-coverage share `C = N_consented / N_learned` over all rows with purpose ≠ turn, with a Wilson interval; target 1.0; any uncovered row is a bug, not a statistic.
- Purpose-validity share `V = #{i : t < T_p,i ∧ consent active} / N_learned`, reported per store nightly by Nawm.
- Disclosure canary: plant *k* private statements through *k* synthetic users (e.g., "my brother Hamid owes me 3,400 rupees"), then query every other user's recall path, the admin views and the pooled templates; exposure `= log₂|R| − log₂ rank` must be undefined (no retrieval) for all *k*. With *k* = 50 and zero hits, the 95 % upper bound on the per-canary leak probability is `1 − 0.05^(1/50) ≈ 5.8 %` (rule of three ≈ 6 %); *k* = 300 is needed to bound it below 1 % [inference, exact binomial].
- Post-Mahw leakage (defined in the sibling and in `mahw.py`) extended to the newly covered stores; overlook rate = share of `private` rows that expired at session end without a `remember`; third-party-name leakage = canaries containing a third party's name found in any summary or profile.
- Poisoning check specific to consent: a `pool` release must be reproducible from the consenting users' rows alone; a release that depends on a non-consenting row is a failed test (MINJA's threat is a user poisoning *others*; consent-gating shrinks the attacker to consenting accounts, which D6's τ already forces to be many).

**Ranking by effect on the Dhikr-Nawm loop** (sibling Q4): (1) the purpose/consent gate changes step 1 from "write always" to "write for consented purposes" and step 5 to "consolidate only consented, non-private rows" — largest, because it alters what Nawm ever sees; (2) private-matter expiry changes step 2's capture (`c ← c + M·e` applies only to non-private or remembered rows) and step 7's archive into deletion by purpose clock; (3) consent-gated pooling changes D6/step 5 Tier B's global priors; (4) admin separation changes no loop step (access layer only); (5) Mahw coverage changes none (completeness); (6) metrics are read-only.

What the owner must decide (the UL2 outcome the critic says was never recorded): whether `self` learning is default-on-with-ledger (this note's recommendation, defensible under every fetched regime once the consent line is separate and withdrawal is one switch) or opt-in (strictly safer; costs the cold-start that Hifz/Ta'allum/Qasd depend on). The texts do not force opt-in for `self`; they do force it for `pool`/`train`.

_Note: this design section is applied reasoning and does not constitute a scholarly ruling or legal advice._
