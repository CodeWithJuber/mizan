## 1. Quranic vocabulary of knowing: exact ayat (Saheeh International)

### Takeaway
The Quran gives a complete, layered vocabulary of knowing: the learner is born knowing nothing and is given hearing, sight and af'ida (hearts/intellect) for which it is accountable (16:78, 17:36); knowledge is taught (2:31, 96:4-5), increased by asking (20:114), received gradually (17:106, 25:32), verified before acting (49:6), separated from zann (53:28), graded by certainty (102:5-7, 69:51, 56:95), recorded without omission (18:49, 36:12), subject to forgetting and to divine erasure/confirmation (20:115, 13:39), and turned into action through reflection (tafakkur, tadabbur, tadhakkur) by "people of understanding" (ulul albab).

### Inferences
_Note: this applied reasoning incorporates reasoning beyond the fetched canonical text and does not constitute a scholarly ruling or opinion from quran.ai, quran.com, or quran.foundation._
- The Quran's own pipeline of knowing reads as: perception (sam', basar) -> heart/intellect (fu'ad, qalb, 'aql: 22:46 places reasoning in the heart) -> reflection (tafakkur on creation, tadabbur on text) -> being reminded (tadhakkur) -> action, with accountability for every faculty (17:36). For MIZAN this suggests every claim should carry which "faculty" produced it (heard from user/source, seen by a tool or observation, inferred by the model) because 17:36 makes each channel separately answerable.
- 'Ilm al-yaqin / 'ayn al-yaqin / haqq al-yaqin (102:5-7, 69:51, 56:95) map naturally onto three evidence levels: inferred/described, directly observed (tool output, executed result), and realized/established (canonical source or independently re-verified). MIZAN's Isnad grades (da'if/hasan/sahih) already approximate this; the Quranic triad gives a principled name for the observation layer.
- Zann is never promoted to 'ilm by repetition or majority (6:116, 53:28, 10:36): a learned estimate (Tamyiz posterior, model wording) should never overwrite a verified fact, and "most sources say" is not by itself verification.
- 2:106 frames forgetting/replacement as permissible only when something equal or better replaces it; 13:39 pairs erasure (mahw) with confirmation (ithbat) over an unchanging "Mother of the Book". Architecturally: an immutable canonical core (Quran text, hadith text, signed receipts) plus a revisable learned layer where entries are erased or confirmed, and a deletion is acceptable when its replacement is at least as good.
- 2:282 gives a recording discipline that maps onto MIZAN's Sakk/Daftar: write small and large items alike, omit nothing, keep the record "stronger as evidence and more likely to prevent doubt", and use two witnesses "so that if one of them errs, then the other can remind her", which is the same idea as Tathabbut's two independent programs.
- 4:82 (no contradiction) and 67:3-4 (look again, twice) give Quranic grounding for internal-consistency checks and repeated inspection before an output is trusted.
- Human "creating" in the Quran is craft that is taught (21:80), measured (34:11 "calculate [precisely] the links"), done under observation and guidance (11:37) and judged by righteousness (34:11). Badi' (origination with no precedent, 2:117) belongs to Allah. MIZAN's creativity is best framed as measured, verifiable craft (generate within a measure, then verify), not as unbounded origination; novelty in religion itself is out of scope (57:27, 46:9).

## 2. What the classical tafsir (al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi, plus al-Sa'di) say these imply for how a learner learns, verifies, remembers, revises and forgets

### Takeaway
The mufassirun read these ayat as a concrete method: do not speak without knowledge, which explicitly includes claiming "I saw/heard/know" when one did not (Qatadah via al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir on 17:36); give a report respite and do not hasten to accept it until its soundness is known, which is why hadith scholars reject narrators of unknown reliability (al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir on 49:6); refer uncertain news to those able to extract conclusions, and istinbat means ijtihad where there is no text or consensus (al-Qurtubi on 4:83); receive knowledge in stages (Ibn Kathir on 25:32, 20:114); hearts hold knowledge according to capacity, and doubt is the foam that vanishes while certainty remains (Ibn Abbas via Ibn Kathir on 13:17); a forgetter is not blamed, and a forgotten "if Allah wills" is said when remembered (Ibn Kathir on 18:24, al-Sa'di on 18:73); and the Musa–Khidr story yields an explicit list of rules for learners, including deliberateness and verification before judging (al-Sa'di).

### Cited Findings

**On speaking/acting without knowledge (17:36)**
- Ibn Kathir: Ibn Abbas: "do not say (anything of which you have no knowledge)"; Qatadah: "Do not say, 'I have seen', when you did not see anything, or 'I have heard', when you did not hear anything, or 'I know', when you do not know, for Allah will ask you about all of that." Ibn Kathir concludes "Allah forbids speaking without knowledge and only on the basis of suspicion, which is mere imagination and illusions", linking it to 49:12 and to the hadith "Beware of suspicion, for suspicion is the falsest of speech" and to "What an evil habit it is for a man to say, 'They claimed...'" (Abu Dawud). "Each of those ones" means the faculties of hearing, sight and the heart, about which the person will be asked. — [Ibn Kathir on 17:36](https://quran.com/17:36/tafsirs/169)
- al-Tabari (model translation): he records two readings of "la taqfu" (do not say / do not accuse) and judges them close in meaning "because saying what the speaker does not know includes false testimony, accusing people falsely, and claiming to have heard what he did not hear and seen what he did not see"; his preferred reading is "do not say about people what you have no knowledge of". On "hearing, sight and heart... will be questioned": Allah will ask these organs about what their owner claimed to have heard, seen or known, and they will testify truthfully against him. — [al-Tabari on 17:36](https://quran.com/17:36/tafsirs/15)

**On verification of reports (49:6)**
- al-Tabari (model translation): the Madinan readers read "fa-tathabbatu" (with tha'), and it is so pointed in the mushaf of Abdullah [ibn Mas'ud]; others read "fa-tabayyanu", meaning "give it respite until you know its soundness; do not hasten to accept it", "and that is also the meaning of fa-tathabbatu". Al-Tabari: both are known readings close in meaning, and whoever reads either is correct. He reports the occasion (al-Walid b. 'Uqba's false report about Banu al-Mustaliq) through several chains, including Qatadah's account that the Prophet sent Khalid b. al-Walid "and ordered him to verify (yatathabbat) and not hasten", that Khalid sent scouts at night, heard their adhan and prayer, and reported back. "Lest you harm a people out of ignorance" = so you do not strike innocent people with a charge out of your ignorance and then regret it. — [al-Tabari on 49:6](https://quran.com/49:6/tafsirs/15)
- Ibn Kathir: "Allah the Exalted ordered investigating the news that sinners and the wicked bring, to make sure of its authenticity. Otherwise, if the sinner's word is taken for granted and a decision is based on it... the authorities will be taking the lead of the sinners... This is why groups of the scholars of Hadith refuse to accept narrations from narrators whose reliability is unknown, for they might be from among the wicked people, in reality." — [Ibn Kathir on 49:6](https://quran.com/49:4/tafsirs/169)

**On referring news and istinbat (4:83)**
- al-Qurtubi (model translation): those addressed "spread it, made it public and talked about it before they established its reality"; "those of authority" are "the people of knowledge and fiqh" (from al-Hasan, Qatadah and others; al-Suddi and Ibn Zayd: the governors); "those who extract it (yastanbitunahu)" would know what should be published and what should be kept back. "Istinbat is taken from extracting water [from a well]... istinbat in language is extraction, and it indicates ijtihad when there is no text and no consensus (ijma')." — [al-Qurtubi on 4:83](https://quran.com/4:83/tafsirs/90)

**On taking lessons (i'tibar, 59:2)**
- al-Qurtubi (model translation): "fa'tabiru" = "take admonition, O people of minds and intellects"; also said: "O you who saw that with your eyes". Among the lessons: they sought protection in fortresses against Allah and He brought them down; "whoever does not take a lesson from others takes a lesson in himself", and the sound proverb "the fortunate one is admonished by others". — [al-Qurtubi on 59:2](https://quran.com/59:2/tafsirs/90)

**On asking the people of knowledge (16:43)**
- Ibn Kathir: through Ibn Abbas (via al-Dahhak and Mujahid) "Ahl adh-Dhikr" here are "the People of the Book": ask them whether the earlier messengers were humans or angels. The Prophet was sent "so that you may explain in detail what has been mentioned in brief, and explain what is not clear", and "so that perhaps they may reflect... they should examine themselves and be guided by it". — [Ibn Kathir on 16:43](https://quran.com/16:43/tafsirs/169)

**On increase in knowledge, not hastening, and gradual reception (20:114, 75:16-19, 25:32, 17:106)**
- Ibn Kathir on 20:114: Ibn Abbas: "the Messenger of Allah used to go through great pains to retain the revelation. In doing so he used to move his tongue rapidly with its recital"; Allah "guided him to that which was easier and lighter"; 75:17 means "We will gather it in your chest, then you will recite it to the people without forgetting anything of it"; the command is "to listen quietly: 'Then, when the angel (Jibril) completes reciting to you, you recite it after him.'" "My Lord! Increase me in knowledge" = "Give me more knowledge from You"; Ibn 'Uyaynah: "The Prophet did not cease increasing (in knowledge) until Allah... took him." — [Ibn Kathir on 20:114](https://quran.com/20:113/tafsirs/169)
- Ibn Kathir on 25:32: the Quran "was revealed in stages over twenty-three years, according to events and circumstances, and whatever rulings were needed, in order to strengthen the hearts of the believers"; Qatadah on "rattalnahu tartila": "We have explained it"; Ibn Abbas (Nasa'i): it was sent down all at once to the first heaven on Laylat al-Qadr, then revealed over twenty years. — [Ibn Kathir on 25:32](https://quran.com/25:32/tafsirs/169)

**On forgetting and what is kept (87:6-7, 18:24, 2:31)**
- Ibn Kathir on 87:6-7: a promise "that He will teach him a recitation that he will not forget"; Qatadah: "The Prophet did not forget anything except what Allah willed"; the exception covers what Allah "removes and there is no sin on your leaving it off (not retaining it)" (abrogation). On 87:9 "remind where reminding is beneficial": "From here we get the etiquette of spreading knowledge, that it should not be wasted upon those who are not suitable or worthy of it", with Ali's statements "You do not tell people any statement that their intellects do not grasp except that it will be a Fitnah (trial) for some of them" and "Tell people that which they know." — [Ibn Kathir on 87:1-19](https://quran.com/87:1/tafsirs/169)
- Ibn Kathir on 18:23-24: the etiquette of referring future plans to Allah's will; "if you forget to say 'If Allah wills', then say it when you remember" (Abu al-'Aliyah, al-Hasan al-Basri); Ibn Abbas held it may be said "even if it is a year later"; Ibn Jarir (al-Tabari) agreed but held "this does not make up for breaking the oath"; "say: It may be that my Lord guides me to a nearer way" means "if you (O Prophet) are asked about something you know nothing about, ask Allah about it, and turn to Him so that He may guide you to what is right." — [Ibn Kathir on 18:23-24](https://quran.com/18:23/tafsirs/169)
- Ibn Kathir on 2:31-33: Ibn Abbas: Allah taught Adam "the names that people use... including the names of the other species", even of the plate and pot; "Allah taught Adam the names of everything, their proper names, the names of their characteristics, and what they do"; the angels affirm "no creature could ever acquire any part of Allah's knowledge, except by His permission, nor could anyone know anything except what Allah teaches them"; Adam's superiority over the angels was shown in knowledge. — [Ibn Kathir on 2:31](https://quran.com/2:31/tafsirs/169)

**On capacity, doubt and certainty (13:17)**
- Ibn Kathir: "each valley taking its share according to its capacity... This Ayah indicates that hearts differ, for some of them can retain substantial knowledge while others cannot"; Ibn Abbas: "the hearts carry knowledge from Him, and certainty according to the amount of doubt... the foam... refers to doubt... that which is for the good of mankind remains... in reference to certainty... Allah accepts certainty and discards doubt." He pairs it with the Prophet's rain parable (Two Sahihs) of land that absorbs, land that holds, and barren land. — [Ibn Kathir on 13:17](https://quran.com/13:17/tafsirs/169)

**On erasure and confirmation (13:39)**
- Ibn Kathir: "Allah blots out what He wills of the divinely revealed Books, and confirms, until the Qur'an... abrogated them all"; Mujahid: "Except life and death, misery and happiness... for they do not change"; at Laylat al-Qadr Allah "decides what provisions and disasters will occur in the next year... He then brings forward or back (or blots out) whatever He wills. As for the Book containing the records of the happy... and the miserable..., it does not change." — [Ibn Kathir on 13:38-39](https://quran.com/13:38/tafsirs/169). The Thawban hadith he cites ("only supplication changes al-Qadar") is Ibn Majah 4022, graded Hasan by al-Albani, Hasan li-ghayrihi by Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut, Da'if by Zubair Ali Zai — [hadith-api eng-ibnmajah](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-ibnmajah.min.json).

**On the faculties (16:78) and self-reproach (75:2, 75:14)**
- Ibn Kathir on 16:78: hearing "to recognize voices", sight "to see visible things and hearts - meaning reason - whose seat, according to the correct view, is the heart, although it was also said that its seat is the brain. With his reason, a person can distinguish between what is harmful and what is beneficial. These abilities and senses develop gradually in man." — [Ibn Kathir on 16:77-78](https://quran.com/16:77/tafsirs/169)
- Ibn Kathir on 75:2: al-Hasan al-Basri: "every believer blames himself. He says (questioning himself), 'What did I intend by my statement? What did I intend by my eating?...' However, the sinner proceeds ahead and he does not blame himself"; Mujahid: "He is sorry for what he missed (of good deeds) and he blames himself for it"; on 75:14, Qatadah: one "sees the shortcomings of the people and their sins, yet he is heedless of his own sins". — [Ibn Kathir on 75](https://quran.com/75:1/tafsirs/169)

**On certainty levels (102:5-7, 69:51)**
- al-Qurtubi on 102:7 (model translation): "'ayn al-yaqin" = direct witnessing (mushahada); one view: "if you knew today in this world with 'ilm al-yaqin what is ahead of you... you would see Hell with the eyes of your hearts; for 'ilm al-yaqin shows you Hell with the eye of your fu'ad, which is that the stages of the Resurrection are pictured for you... then you will see it with 'ayn al-yaqin, i.e., at direct sight with the eye of the head." — [al-Qurtubi on 102:7](https://quran.com/102:7/tafsirs/90)
- al-Qurtubi on 69:51 (model translation): "haqq al-yaqin" means the Quran is revelation from Allah; Ibn Abbas: it is like saying "'ayn al-yaqin" and "pure certainty"; the addition of "haqq" to "yaqin" is explained grammatically by the two words differing. — [al-Qurtubi on 69:51](https://quran.com/69:51/tafsirs/90)

**On the bee (16:68-69)**
- Ibn Kathir: "What is meant by inspiration here is guidance"; the bee is guided to build solid hexagonal combs, follows "the ways which Allah has made easy for it", and "each bee comes back to its hive without swerving to the right or left"; honey's colours vary "depending on the different things that the bees eat"; Ibn Jarir held both readings of "dhululan" correct. — [Ibn Kathir on 16:68-69](https://quran.com/16:68/tafsirs/169)

**On Musa and al-Khidr (18:60-82)**
- Ibn Kathir (citing Bukhari via Ubayy b. Ka'b): Musa was asked "Who is the most learned person?" and said "I am"; "Allah rebuked him because he did not refer the knowledge to Allah"; al-Khidr: "I have some of Allah's knowledge which He has bestowed upon me but you do not know it; and you too, have some of Allah's knowledge which He has bestowed upon you, but I do not know it"; the bird dipping its beak: "My knowledge and your knowledge, in comparison to Allah's knowledge, is like what this bird has taken out of the sea"; "In the first instance, Musa asked Al-Khidr because he had forgotten his promise"; the Prophet: "We wish that Musa was patient so that Allah would have told us more about both of them." — [Ibn Kathir on 18:60-65](https://quran.com/18:60/tafsirs/169)
- Ibn Kathir on 18:66: "This is a question phrased in gentle terms, with no sense of force or coercion. This is the manner in which the seeker of knowledge should address the scholar"; al-Khidr's condition: "do not initiate any discussion of the matter... until I initiate the discussion"; "I have knowledge of Allah's wisdom and the hidden interests which I can see but you cannot." — [Ibn Kathir on 18:66-70](https://quran.com/18:66/tafsirs/169)
- al-Sa'di on 18:82 (model translation of his list of lessons): the virtue of knowledge and of travelling for it, "and that it is the most important of matters", since Musa left teaching Bani Isra'il to increase his own knowledge; "beginning with what is most important": increasing one's knowledge comes before teaching without provision, and combining both is most complete; knowledge Allah teaches is of two kinds: "acquired knowledge that the servant attains by his effort and striving (ijtihad), and ladunni knowledge that Allah gives to whom He favours"; adab with the teacher and addressing him in the gentlest way, and "humbling oneself to the teacher and showing need of his teaching is among the most beneficial things for the learner"; the superior person learns from one below him in a discipline he has not mastered (so a jurist weak in grammar should learn it from a grammarian); attributing knowledge to Allah; "beneficial knowledge is knowledge that guides to good (rushd)... whatever is other than that is either harmful or without benefit"; "whoever lacks patience in accompanying the scholar and in knowledge loses much knowledge"; "the great cause of patience is a person's encompassing knowledge and experience of the matter", since 18:68 makes lack of khubr the cause of impatience; "the command to deliberateness (ta'anni) and verification (tathabbut) and not hastening to judge a thing until what is intended by it and its purpose is known"; "resolving to do a thing is not the same as doing it", since Musa resolved patience but did not achieve it; a teacher may tell a learner not to start questions about some things when the learner's understanding is limited or the question is off-topic; "the forgetter is not held accountable for his forgetting, neither in the right of Allah nor in the rights of people" (18:73); judgments run on the outward (zahir); "the great rule: a greater harm is averted by committing a lesser harm, and the greater of two benefits is preserved by giving up the lesser"; adab of attributing defects to oneself and good to Allah (18:79 vs 18:82). — [al-Sa'di on 18:82](https://quran.com/18:82/tafsirs/91)

### Inferences
_Note: this synthesis incorporates reasoning beyond the fetched canonical text and does not constitute a scholarly ruling or opinion from quran.ai, quran.com, or quran.foundation._
- Verification is source-weighted, not uniform: 49:6 targets reports from a fasiq, and Ibn Kathir draws from it the hadith scholars' refusal of narrators of unknown reliability. For MIZAN: keep a reliability posterior per source (a jarh-wa-ta'dil score, e.g., Beta posterior updated by verified/refuted outcomes), and require verification before action in proportion to that source's unreliability and to the harm of acting wrongly ("lest you harm a people out of ignorance").
- Tathabbut is temporal as well as evidential: al-Tabari glosses it as "give it respite until you know its soundness; do not hasten to accept it", and the Khalid report models a concrete verification protocol (independent scouts, direct observation of a decisive signal, report back before acting). This fits MIZAN's hold-then-verify design (held actions, Tathabbut cross-check, Shahid observation receipts).
- 17:36 as read by Qatadah forbids claiming "I saw/heard/know" falsely; for an agent this is the strongest Quranic basis for never claiming an action, observation or citation that did not happen (MIZAN's mawdu' grade and Sidq gate).
- 4:83 + al-Qurtubi: (a) do not broadcast unverified news; (b) route uncertain matters to those competent to extract (istinbat); (c) ijtihad is the fallback only where there is no text and no consensus. This gives a three-tier answer policy: quote text verbatim where it exists; report consensus where it exists; otherwise label reasoning as reasoning, and for religious rulings refer to qualified people.
- 13:17 (via Ibn Abbas) gives a retention principle: doubt is the foam that dissipates, what benefits remains. A memory policy can let unverified, unused items decay while verified, useful items persist, which is a Quranic image for utility-weighted forgetting.
- 13:39 (via Ibn Kathir and Mujahid) separates a mutable layer (yearly decrees that can be brought forward, back or blotted out) from an immutable core (the Mother of the Book). This argues for MIZAN keeping canonical sources and signed receipts immutable while learned items are erasable (Mahw) or confirmable.
- Forgetting is not culpable (2:286, 18:73, al-Sa'di), but once reminded one must act (6:68), and a forgotten step is repaired when remembered (18:24, Ibn Kathir). Operationally: a missed check is not a failure to hide; it is re-run when detected (Muhasaba's daily re-check is in this spirit).
- The Musa–Khidr lessons (al-Sa'di) read as a learner protocol for an agent: attribute knowledge to its source; learn from whoever has mastery in that narrow domain; accept a teacher's conditions; do not judge outcomes before understanding purpose (ta'anni, tathabbut); recognise that resolve is not performance (evaluate by doing, not by self-report); weigh harms and benefits (greater harm averted by lesser); and never claim the second kind of knowledge (ladunni). MIZAN can only claim acquired, verifiable knowledge.
- Ibn Kathir on 87:9 and Ali's sayings support audience-adapted explanation: say what the listener can grasp; MIZAN's Lisan wording could be calibrated to user level without changing content.

### Gaps
- al-Tabari was fetched only for 17:36 and 49:6; his treatment of 20:114, 2:31, 13:39, 16:68 and 18:60-82 was not retrieved directly (only as reported by Ibn Kathir).
- al-Qurtubi's discussion of 59:2 as evidence for qiyas (frequently cited in usul works) did not appear in the retrieved passage; a link between i'tibar and qiyas is therefore not sourced here.
- The systematic three-level doctrine of yaqin (e.g., Ibn al-Qayyim's *Madarij al-Salikin*) was not retrieved; only al-Qurtubi's gloss on 102:7 and 69:51 is grounded.
- Qatadah's "tabayyun is from Allah and haste is from Shaytan", as recorded by al-Tabari, is a mursal report (Qatadah is a Successor); its grade as a Prophetic hadith was not verified, so it should not be quoted as an authenticated hadith.
- Ibn Kathir identifies Ahl adh-Dhikr in 16:43 as the People of the Book; the wider usul use of this ayah as a general command to ask scholars was not sourced here.

## 3. Authentic hadith on seeking knowledge, verifying reports, consistency, reviewing, intention and humility, with grades; weak and fabricated narrations to exclude

### Takeaway
A strong, authentic core exists in Bukhari and Muslim for every learning behaviour MIZAN needs: intention (Bukhari 1), understanding as a sign of good (Bukhari 71), paced teaching and repetition (Bukhari 68, 95), moderation and consistency (Bukhari 39, 6464; Muslim 783), reviewing what is memorised or it escapes (Bukhari 5031-5033), never lying on the Prophet (Bukhari 110) and not narrating everything one hears (Muslim, Introduction 5), saying "Allah knows best" when one does not know (Bukhari 4809, Ibn Mas'ud), knowledge lost when ignorant people give verdicts without knowledge (Bukhari 100), ijtihad rewarded even when wrong (Bukhari 7352), and asymmetric recording of intentions (Bukhari 6491). Several famous sayings must be excluded: "seek knowledge even in China" (judged fabricated/baseless by Ibn Hibban, Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Albani), "the wise word is the believer's lost property" (Tirmidhi 2687, very weak), the full Ibn Majah 224 text (very weak), "the first thing Allah created was the intellect" (rejected by Ibn Taymiyyah), "whoever knows himself knows his Lord" (not a hadith per al-Nawawi and Ibn Taymiyyah), and "an hour of reflection is better than a year of worship" (not established as Prophetic).

## 4. Classical Islamic epistemology and methods that map to AI (hadith sciences, usul al-fiqh, al-Ghazali, Ibn al-Haytham, Ibn Sina, Ibn Khaldun)

### Takeaway
Classical scholarship gives MIZAN tested, nameable methods: the hadith sciences grade every report by its chain and content (five conditions of sahih, multiple graders who can disagree); usul al-fiqh extends a ruling from a known case to a new case only through a shared effective cause ('illa), with ijtihad reserved for where text and consensus are silent; al-Ghazali defines certain knowledge as that which leaves no room for doubt, tests the senses and reason, and distinguishes the heart, spirit, soul and intellect; Ibn al-Haytham makes the seeker of truth an "enemy of all that he reads" who also suspects himself; Ibn Sina maps internal faculties (common sense, retentive imagination, compositive imagination/cogitation, estimation, memory) onto a processing pipeline; and Ibn Khaldun treats knowledge as a habit (malaka) formed by gradual, repeated practice and clear discussion, not by memorisation.

### Cited Findings

**Hadith sciences (isnad, grading, jarh wa ta'dil)**
- Ibn al-Salah's definition of sahih has five conditions: continuous chain (ittisal), uprightness of narrators ('adalah), precision (dabt), freedom from irregularity (shudhudh), and freedom from hidden defect ('illah); a chain can be sound while the hadith is still irregular or defective; the last two are hardest to verify since they require gathering all chains — [SeekersGuidance: What Is an Authentic Hadith](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hadith/what-is-an-authentic-hadith-al-hadith-al-sahih/); [IslamQA 122507: "a saheeh hadith" vs "its isnaad is saheeh"](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/122507)
- Ibn Hajar distinguishes sahih li-dhatihi (sound in itself) from sahih li-ghayrihi (strengthened by other chains) — [SeekersGuidance](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hadith/what-is-an-authentic-hadith-al-hadith-al-sahih/) (secondary summary)
- Muslim's Introduction records the origin of isnad scrutiny: Ibn Sirin "They would not ask about the chains of narration, and when the Fitnah occurred, they said: 'Name for us your men'", and Ibn al-Mubarak "The chain of narration is from the Dīn..." — [thehouseofislam.com Muslim Introduction](https://www.thehouseofislam.com/hadiths/muslim/introduction)
- Graders disagree and the disagreement is preserved: e.g., Abu Dawud 3641 is Sahih per al-Albani but Da'if per Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut and Zubair Ali Zai; Tirmidhi 2499 is Hasan per Shakir and al-Albani, Da'if per Zubair Ali Zai — [hadith-api eng-abudawud](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-abudawud.min.json), [eng-tirmidhi](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-tirmidhi.min.json)
- Ibn Kathir derives the refusal of narrators "whose reliability is unknown" from 49:6 — [Ibn Kathir on 49:6](https://quran.com/49:4/tafsirs/169)

**Usul al-fiqh (qiyas, 'illa, istinbat, ijtihad)**
- Qiyas has four pillars (per Kamali's *Principles of Islamic Jurisprudence*, as summarised by Habib): the original case (asl), the new case (far'), the effective cause ('illa) "due to which a certain legal ruling is materialised", and the ruling (hukm); worked example: the wine prohibition extended to other intoxicants with intoxication as the 'illa — [Habib, critical arguments of Zahiris against qiyas (PDF)](https://zt1.gcuf.edu.pk/ZTQ/2012-Vol-2,%20Issue-1/A%20critical-arguments-of-zahiris-arguments%20against%20the%20methodology%20of%20Qiyas.%20Dr.%20Muhammad%20Rafiq%20Habib.pdf). Conditions include that the 'illa be clearly indicated, explicitly or implicitly; the 'illa is an attribute (wasf) of the asl common to both cases — [islamonweb: Qiyas in Islamic jurisprudence](https://en.islamonweb.net/qiyas-in-islamic-jurisprudence), [COMSATS HUM112 slides](https://lib.vcomsats.edu.pk/files/HUM112/MLU%207.1/MLO%20Slides/HUM112_Slides_MLO-7.1.6.pdf?download=1)
- The Zahiri school rejected qiyas (Habib's paper is a critique of their arguments), i.e., analogy itself was contested — [Habib (PDF)](https://zt1.gcuf.edu.pk/ZTQ/2012-Vol-2,%20Issue-1/A%20critical-arguments-of-zahiris-arguments%20against%20the%20methodology%20of%20Qiyas.%20Dr.%20Muhammad%20Rafiq%20Habib.pdf)
- al-Qurtubi: istinbat "indicates ijtihad when there is no text and no consensus" — [al-Qurtubi on 4:83](https://quran.com/4:83/tafsirs/90)
- Ijtihad's reward structure: two rewards if right, one if wrong (Bukhari 7352) — [hadith-api eng-bukhari 7352](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-bukhari/7352.json)

**al-Ghazali (al-Munqidh min al-Dalal; Ihya 'Ulum al-Din, Book 21 'Aja'ib al-Qalb)**
- Definition (Abulaylah translation): "Certain knowledge is that in which the thing known reveals itself without leaving any room for doubt", and the heart cannot allow even the possibility of error or illusion; test: even if someone turned "a stick into a serpent", "I know very well that ten is more than three... I would not doubt my knowledge" — [ghazali.org, Deliverance from Error (Abulaylah)](https://ghazali.org/books/md/IIA-02main.htm). McCarthy's translation renders it "the thing known is made so manifest that no doubt clings to it" (per search summary) — [search result summary](https://ghazali.org/?p=615)
- Doubt about the senses: "Sight is the most powerful of our senses", yet it sees a star "reduced to the size of a coin" while geometry shows it larger than the earth; doubt about reason: "Perhaps there is something beyond reason which would show that reason in turn is in error", like waking from a dream; resolution "not by well-ordered or methodical argument, but by means of a light God the Almighty cast into my breast", after which "The self-evident principles of reason again seemed acceptable" — [ghazali.org (Abulaylah)](https://ghazali.org/books/md/IIA-02main.htm)
- Ihya vol. 3 (Ghazali.org English): qalb has two meanings, the physical organ and a subtle immaterial entity (latifa); ruh likewise physical vapour vs immaterial soul; nafs in its praiseworthy form is the calm soul (mutma'inna), self-accusing (lawwama) when calm is imperfect, and ammara when it yields to evil; 'aql means the intellect by which the true nature of things is known, and also the power to grasp the sciences — [ghazali.org Ihya vol. 3](https://ghazali.org/ihya/english/ihya-vol3-C1.htm) (via search summary). The "heart as a mirror" theme is described in secondary book descriptions only — [Fons Vitae listing](https://fonsvitae.com/?p=271)

**Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen): doubt toward authorities and self-suspicion**
- Sabra's translation (quoted from the Optics context, also associated with *al-Shukuk 'ala Batlamyus*): "Truth is sought for itself..."; the seeker after truth "submits to argument and demonstration, and not to the sayings of a human being"; the duty of one who investigates scientists' writings is "to make himself an enemy of all that he reads" and to "suspect himself as he performs his critical examination of it" so he does not fall into prejudice or leniency — [The Marginalian, quoting Sabra's translation](https://www.themarginalian.org/2022/11/14/alhazen-truth/); the "seeker after the truth is not one who studies the writings of the ancients and... puts his trust in them, but rather the one who suspects his faith in them" — [Harvard Magazine (search excerpt)](https://harvardmagazine.com/2003/09/ibn-al-haytham-html). Sabra's translation says truths are immersed in uncertainties and authorities like Ptolemy are not immune from error — (same search summary)
- Note: the two sources attribute the passage differently (the Doubts against Ptolemy vs the Optics); the exact provenance should be checked in Sabra's edition.

**Ibn Sina (Avicenna): faculties of the soul (SEP, Ivry)**
- Common sense "coordinates the impressions received by the individual senses"; retentive imagination (khayal, quwwa musawwira) "retains these forms fully"; compositive imagination (mutakhayyila), called cogitative (mufakkira) when working on concepts, combines and separates impressions; estimation (wahm) grasps a "non-sensible intention (ma'nan) that is intrinsic to the object" (the sheep sensing danger in a wolf) and can overreach into fantasy and false metaphysical judgments; memory (hafiza) and recollection (dhikr) "retains the estimative intentions ready for recall"; "Avicenna located the internal senses in three ventricles of the brain" (front: common sense, imagination; middle: estimation, cogitation; rear: memory) — [SEP: Arabic and Islamic Psychology and Philosophy of Mind](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arabic-islamic-mind/)
- Intellect stages: material/potential -> in habitu (bi'l-malaka; acquired intelligibles not in use, which "merely serves to facilitate their reacquisition from the Agent Intellect") -> acquired (mustafad), "an active state of cognition"; intuition (hads) is "the key step in obtaining certain knowledge"; the Agent Intellect is the source of intelligibles, "not abstracted from the imagination" — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arabic-islamic-mind/)
- Averroes rejected Avicenna's "elevation of wahm, estimation, to the status of an additional internal sense" — [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arabic-islamic-mind/)

**Ibn Khaldun (Muqaddimah, ch. 6): knowledge as habit (malaka)**
- Rosenthal (abridged Dawood ed.): "The easiest method of acquiring the scientific habit is through acquiring the ability to express oneself clearly" in "discussing and disputing scientific problems"; students "More than is necessary, they are concerned with memorizing" and some believe "scientific habit is identical with memorized knowledge. But that is not so." — [Children and Youth in History, Ibn Khaldun primary source (Rosenthal tr., ed. Dawood, pp. 340-1)](https://cyh.rrchnm.org/primary-sources/461.html)
- Teaching is itself a craft/malaka; knowledge becomes rooted through repetition at close intervals with concrete examples; "three repetitions" mark useful education; most teachers ignore gradual progression from simple to complex (secondary) — [IslamOnline: Ibn Khaldun's education theory](https://islamonline.net/en/ibn-khaldun-education-theory-methods/); repeated action forms habits that, once rooted, are hard to uproot (secondary) — [IIUM Intellectual Discourse (1995)](https://journals.iium.edu.my/intdiscourse/index.php/id/article/download/358/pdf/662); gradual learning (tadarruj) and repetition (takrar) as core methods — [Jurnal Pendas (2023)](https://journal.unpas.ac.id/index.php/pendas/article/view/57841)

### Inferences
_Note: this synthesis goes beyond the sources and is not a scholarly ruling._
- Isnad -> per-claim provenance record with the five sahih conditions as checks: continuity (every hop from source to answer is logged, Sakk), 'adalah (source reliability posterior), dabt (exact-quote fidelity: verbatim match against the source), absence of shudhudh (the claim does not contradict a stronger source: Ahl al-Dhikr's contradiction check), absence of 'illa (hidden-defect audits such as cross-checks and re-execution). Preserving disagreement between graders (as the hadith-api data does) suggests MIZAN should store multiple verifier verdicts rather than collapse them.
- Qiyas -> MIZAN's existing Qiyas recipe for ARC (Lisan proposes `transform(grid)`; MIZAN accepts it only if it reproduces every example) is structurally the asl/far'/'illa/hukm scheme: examples are asl, the test grid is far', the program is the hypothesised 'illa, and reproduction of every example is the test that the 'illa is effective. Extending a learned rule to a new user request should likewise require an explicit shared 'illa, not surface similarity.
- Ijtihad scope: al-Qurtubi's "no text and no consensus" condition defines when model reasoning may answer at all; where text exists, MIZAN quotes it; for religious rulings, it refers (consistent with the current `ruling` facet referral).
- Ibn al-Haytham -> the default stance toward any retrieved page or model output is adversarial verification ("enemy of all that he reads") and self-suspicion (the verifier must also be checked: Tathabbut's independent second program, and evaluations that test MIZAN against the base model).
- Ibn Sina's internal senses map to an agent pipeline: common sense = fusion of channels (text, voice, image); retentive imagination = short-term buffer of raw inputs; compositive imagination/cogitation = generative recombination (the creative faculty, which must be gated); estimation (wahm) = fast learned priors/intent classifier (useful but prone to "overreach into fantasy", i.e., hallucination, so it must not be the final judge); memory/recollection = long-term store with retrieval. The intellect-in-habitu stage (stored but not active, easy to reacquire) resembles cached verified programs (Hifz).
- al-Ghazali's certainty test ("ten is more than three" survives any spectacle) suggests MIZAN's highest certainty tier be reserved for what no further evidence can overturn (computed arithmetic, verbatim canonical text), and that persuasive-seeming contrary evidence (an injected instruction, a confident page) must not move it.
- Ibn Khaldun -> evaluate skills by performance (agentic tasks), not recall; form skills by gradual, repeated practice with close intervals; and the "clear expression in discussion" route parallels requiring MIZAN to explain and defend a verified result.

## 5. How Muslim scholars and recent academic work (2015-2026) discuss AI in light of these concepts

### Takeaway
Recent work treats AI as conditionally permissible and frames its ethics through maqasid/maslaha, virtue ethics, and Quranic values such as ihsan and amanah; Yaqeen Institute (Dec 2025, updated Oct 2026) insists humans remain responsible for truth, that AI content be verified by scholars, and that its own assistant answers only from verified, referenced research and gives no rulings; and a 2025-2026 wave of benchmarks shows LLMs still misquote and misattribute Quran and hadith (IslamicEval 2025; an agent-based evaluation finding even the best model short of reliable citation). I found no peer-reviewed work that uses the Quranic vocabulary of knowing (yaqin levels, tathabbut, isnad, nisyan) as an engineering architecture for an agent, which is the space MIZAN occupies.

### Inferences
_Note: this synthesis goes beyond the sources and is not a scholarly ruling._
- The scholarly consensus direction (Yaqeen, maqasid papers) is: AI may assist, but truth, rulings and accountability stay with qualified humans; answers must be referenced and verified. MIZAN's current design (no rulings, scholar referral, verbatim Quran/hadith from sources, Sidq and Isnad) is aligned with this and can cite Yaqeen's AQSA policy as external precedent.
- The 2025-2026 benchmarks measure exactly the failure that 49:6, 17:36 and Muslim Intro 5 forbid (unverified quotation and misattribution). MIZAN's Imtihan could add IslamicEval-style tasks (detect and correct misquoted ayat/hadith; provenance correctness) to measure its Quranic verification faculties against the base model.
- Most academic work is normative ethics (what AI should value), not cognitive architecture (how an AI should know). A MIZAN write-up that maps Quranic epistemic terms to concrete mechanisms (yaqin tiers, tathabbut, isnad grading, nisyan/mahw, tawbah, malaka) appears to be novel relative to what I found.
