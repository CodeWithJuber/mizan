# Measuring MIZAN against Google DeepMind's 10-faculty Cognitive Taxonomy: human baselines, benchmarks and a defensible radar profile (as of 28 Oct 2026)

Context for the report writer: MIZAN's current Imtihan ("AGI test") has 85 question tasks in 13 JSONL blocks (abstention 6, arc 8, code 3, consistency 12, exactness 6, honesty 4, ibrah 13, injection 6, knowledge 8, learning 3, multilingual 4, safety 6, scripture 6), plus 10 agentic sandbox tasks, 3 ARC-AGI-3-style games and the hash-locked eval. The index in `backend/imtihan/report.py` uses Artificial Analysis v4 weights: agents 0.30, coding 0.20, reasoning 0.20, general 0.30. (Read from the local repo: `/backend/imtihan/report.py` and `backend/imtihan/tasks/*.jsonl`, 28 Oct 2026.) The small block sizes matter for every confidence-interval point below.

## Q1. What does the DeepMind paper (and blog) define, how does it propose measuring, which human baselines and levels does it use, and what does it say about radar profiles?

### Takeaway
The paper is real but its arXiv title is **"Measuring Progress Toward AGI: A Cognitive Framework"** (arXiv:2605.28405, v1 27 May 2026, 13 authors led by Ryan Burnell, with Shane Legg last). The "Cognitive Taxonomy" is the paper's 10-faculty component, and the blog calls the paper by that name. Its protocol has three stages: held-out cognitive tasks per faculty, human baselines from a demographically representative adult sample under identical conditions, and a per-faculty percentile ("percentage of people from the human sample the system outperforms") plotted as a **radar cognitive profile** (Figure 2). It does not restate the Levels of AGI percentile bands and does not set a single AGI threshold. Instead it describes profile patterns: below the human median on some faculty, above the median on all ten, above the 99th percentile on all ten.

**The ten faculties (blog one-line definitions, verbatim)**
- Perception: "extracting and processing sensory information from the environment". Generation: "producing outputs such as text, speech and actions". Attention: "focusing cognitive resources on what matters". Learning: "acquiring new knowledge through experience and instruction". Memory: "storing and retrieving information over time". Reasoning: "drawing valid conclusions through logical inference". Metacognition: "knowledge and monitoring of one's own cognitive processes". Executive functions: "planning, inhibition and cognitive flexibility". Problem solving: "finding effective solutions to domain-specific problems". Social cognition: "processing and interpreting social information and responding appropriately in social situations" — [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/measuring-agi-cognitive-framework/)
- Eight faculties are basic building blocks and two (problem solving, social cognition) are **composite** faculties that draw on the others — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1); [Singularity Hub (secondary)](https://singularityhub.com/2026/03/20/google-deepmind-plans-to-track-agi-progress-with-these-10-traits-of-general-intelligence)

**Sub-faculties (from the paper's taxonomy appendix, Section 7)** — all from [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1):
- **Perception**: visual low-level (light, contrast/edge, colour, depth, motion, shape detection); visual high-level (object recognition, visual spatial localisation, visual counting, static and dynamic scene understanding, word segmentation, reading comprehension); auditory low-level (loudness, pitch, sound discrimination, sound localisation, speech segmentation, rhythm); auditory high-level (speech comprehension, speaker identification, sound recognition, auditory scene understanding, listening comprehension); text low-level (symbol discrimination, word segmentation); text high-level (language comprehension, code comprehension); other modalities (touch, smell, temperature) named but not detailed; **multi-sensory integration** to be measured for each modality combination.
- **Generation**: text (natural-language generation: grammaticality, lexical selection; code generation); audio (speech: clarity, grammar, lexical selection, prosody control, emotional expression); action (motor control, **computer control**, **tool use**); **thought generation** (internal thoughts that guide decisions, described as hard to evaluate directly).
- **Attention**: attention capacity; selective attention / attentional control (sustained attention, perceptual inhibition, attention shifting); stimulus-driven (bottom-up) attention.
- **Learning**: concept formation, associative learning, reinforcement learning (operant), observational learning, procedural learning, language learning (including coding languages and tool-use frameworks). A footnote says "For many current systems, learning occurs only during training or in-context," and the paper says systems "should be able to learn (and retain) new knowledge and skills over time."
- **Memory**: semantic/world knowledge (commonsense: general, causal, temporal, spatial, intuitive physics; domain: linguistic, STEM, coding, legal, financial, medical, historical, socio-cultural); episodic (sensory, temporal, spatial memory of events); procedural; **prospective** (remembering planned actions when a cue appears); **forgetting** (removing outdated or irrelevant information).
- **Reasoning**: deductive, inductive, abductive, analogical, mathematical.
- **Metacognition**: metacognitive knowledge (of limitations, learning processes, metamemory, behavioural patterns); monitoring (confidence calibration, defined as "The ability to accurately estimate the likelihood that an action will be successful or that a response will be correct"; judgments of learning; error monitoring; source judgments); control (error correction, learning-strategy selection).
- **Executive functions**: goal setting and maintenance, planning, inhibitory control, cognitive flexibility, conflict resolution, working memory.
- **Problem solving** (composite): fluid reasoning, mathematical, algorithmic, commonsense (temporal, spatial, causal, intuitive physics), knowledge discovery.
- **Social cognition** (composite): social perception ("The ability to interpret social cues based on perceptual information"), theory of mind, social skills (cooperation, negotiation, deception, persuasion).

**Evaluation protocol**
- Blog wording of the three stages: (1) evaluate AI systems on a broad suite of cognitive tasks covering each ability "using held-out test sets to prevent data contamination"; (2) collect human baselines for the same tasks from "a demographically representative sample of adults"; (3) map each system's performance relative to the distribution of human performance in each ability — [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/measuring-agi-cognitive-framework/)
- Task design criteria (Section 3.1): targeted to specific abilities, held out from training data, independently verified, "Varied in difficulty for humans", varied in structure and format. It includes tasks that are easy for humans and hard for AI, and "Evaluations should ideally use private, held-out test sets to prevent contamination" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)
- Human baseline (Section 3.2): "a demographically representative sample of adults with at least the equivalent of an upper secondary education" (footnote: roughly a US high-school degree), with the same instructions, response format and "access to external tools" as the AI — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)
- Scoring (Section 3.3): "we can calculate the percentage of people from the human sample the system outperforms". Aggregation within a faculty can be simple, but "building an Item-Response Theory model of system capabilities" could be more robust. Uncertainty should be quantified from task quality, construct validity and stochasticity — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)
- Harness (Section 4.3): "We therefore think the best approach is to evaluate the system as a whole, including any built-in tools or modules". The paper also cautions: "If we allow AI systems to have access to any and all tools during evaluations, these tools could muddy the interpretation of the findings" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)
- Coverage (Section 4.1): good benchmarks exist for problem solving (cites Phan et al. 2025, Humanity's Last Exam), perception (Patraucean et al. 2023, Perception Test) and world knowledge (FACTS, Cheng et al. 2025; SimpleQA Verified, Haas et al. 2025). It also cites ARC-AGI-2 for tasks easy for humans and hard for AI. "there are large coverage gaps in areas such as metacognition, attention, learning, and social cognition" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)
- Other works in the reference list: GDPval (Patwardhan et al. 2025), Remote Labor Index (Mazeika et al. 2025), IRT in AI (Martínez-Plumed et al. 2019), metacognition classics (Nelson 1990 metamemory; Rhodes 2016 judgments of learning; Yeung & Summerfield 2012 confidence and error monitoring; Son & Schwartz 2002; Metcalfe 2017), ToM (Leslie et al. 2004), and propensities (Romero-Alvarado et al. 2026; Taubenfeld et al. 2026) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)

**Radar profile and thresholds**
- Figure 2 is a **radar plot** with three hypothetical panels: (A) a system with significant weaknesses, (B) a system above the human median on all faculties, (C) a system above the human maximum. Human scores are standardised per dimension for illustration — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)
- The paper gives no single AGI threshold. Its example patterns: below the human median on one or more faculties means significant weaknesses; above the median on all ten means matching at least 50% of the sample; above the 99th percentile on all ten means "Such a system is demonstrating it can match almost anyone from the human sample across the entire cognitive space." It notes that beating the sample maximum is a stronger test whose uncertainty is hard to quantify — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)
- The blog page itself has no radar chart. Its only graphic is a bubble diagram of the ten faculties — [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/measuring-agi-cognitive-framework/)
- Stated limits: the taxonomy is "a starting point"; the link from faculty weaknesses to real-world performance is still unproven empirically; most public benchmarks are contaminable; stochasticity; harness confounds; a representative sample "is unlikely to capture the full scope of human capabilities"; processing speed, propensities, creativity and end-to-end deployment are discussed but not incorporated; "cognitive benchmarking is not a substitute for applied, end-to-end evaluations" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)

**Levels of AGI (Morris et al.)**
- "Levels of AGI for Operationalizing Progress on the Path to AGI", arXiv:2311.02462, by Morris, Sohl-Dickstein, Fiedel, Warkentin, Dafoe, Faust, Farabet, Legg. Published in Proceedings of ICML 2024 (position paper). Versions: v1 4 Nov 2023, v2 5 Jan 2024, v3 22 May 2024, v4 5 Jun 2024, v5 24 Sep 2025 (current) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02462)
- Performance levels (v5 Table 1): 0 No AI; 1 Emerging, "equal to or somewhat better than an unskilled human"; 2 Competent, "at least 50th percentile of skilled adults"; 3 Expert, "at least 90th percentile of skilled adults"; 4 **Exceptional** (formerly "Virtuoso"), "at least 99th percentile of skilled adults"; 5 Superhuman, "outperforms 100% of humans" — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.02462v5)
- Six principles: capabilities not processes; generality and performance; cognitive and metacognitive, not physical, tasks; potential not deployment; ecological validity; the path to AGI, not a single endpoint. Generality is "the range of tasks for which an AI system reaches a target performance threshold", and "an AGI benchmark should be a living benchmark" — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.02462v5)
- The 2026 paper cites Morris et al. (2024) and treats intelligence as "a continuous, multidimensional construct", but it does not restate the level names or percentile bands — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)

### Inferences
- The protocol fits MIZAN's existing design well. "Evaluate the system as a whole, including any built-in tools or modules" justifies the base-alone vs MIZAN comparison: MIZAN is the system, and the base model is a second system. The human arm must get the same tool access as MIZAN, or the comparison is not like-for-like.
- Because the paper gives no AGI threshold, MIZAN should report per-faculty human percentiles and map them onto Levels of AGI bands only as a convenience. The bands are defined against "skilled adults" (Morris), while the 2026 protocol samples "adults with upper-secondary education". These are different reference populations and should not be mixed silently.
- The radar is illustrative in the paper (standardised human scores). It is not a validated scoring method, so MIZAN should treat the radar as a display of per-faculty percentiles with intervals.

## Q2. Which existing benchmarks measure each faculty, and which have published human baselines?

### Takeaway
Human baselines are good for perception (BLINK 95.7%, Perception Test 91.4%, MMMU experts 76–89%), reasoning (ARC-AGI-2 panel ~60%, GPQA experts 65%/74% and non-experts 34%) and interactive learning (ARC-AGI-3, where every environment is solved by humans, with a per-level human action count). They are thin to absent for attention (RULER and NIAH have none), long-term memory (LoCoMo has a human QA score, LongMemEval appears to have none), metacognition (no matched human meta-d' on the same items) and much of ToM (CogArena found matched human accuracies for only False Belief and EPITOME among 13 paradigms). That matches the gaps DeepMind names.

### Cited Findings

**Perception**
- BLINK (Fu et al., ECCV 2024): 14 classic vision tasks recast as 3,807 multiple-choice questions. Humans average **95.70%**, GPT-4V 51.26% and Gemini 45.72%, only 13.17 and 7.63 points above chance — [ECCV 2024](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/html/3356_ECCV_2024_paper.php); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12390v1)
- Perception Test (Patraucean et al., NeurIPS 2023, cited by DeepMind): 11.6k real videos (~23 s) across video, audio and text. Human **91.4%** vs 46.2% for the best model (abstract). Splits are public under CC-BY, with a held-out test split on a challenge server — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13786); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/8540fba4abdc7f9f7a7b1cc6cd60e409-Abstract.html)
- MMMU: human experts at **76.2% (worst) / 82.6% (medium) / 88.6% (best)** on validation, added to the leaderboard in January 2024 and not part of the original paper; the original paper involved 90 college students. Splits are 900 validation and 10.5k test; by mid-2026 top models reach about 85–87% on validation (secondary) — [MMMU paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.16502); [MMMU-Pro](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02813); [benchmarkingagents (secondary)](https://benchmarkingagents.com/mmmu-benchmark)

**Attention**
- RULER (NVIDIA, Apr 2024): 13 synthetic tasks in 4 categories (retrieval, multi-hop tracing/variable tracking, aggregation, QA). Models are near-perfect on vanilla NIAH but drop sharply with length, and only half of the models claiming ≥32K context are effective at 32K. There is **no human baseline** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654v2); [GitHub](https://github.com/averypai/RULER)
- CogArena (arXiv 2607.24999, 27 Jul 2026) built procedurally generated text versions of Stroop, Flanker and Go/No-Go (cognitive control). Flanker signatures replicated in 18/20 model checkpoints, text Stroop in only 7/20 — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24999.md)

**Learning**
- ARC-AGI-3 (technical report arXiv:2603.24621 v2, 17 Apr 2026): an interactive turn-based game benchmark with no instructions. It has 25 public demo, 55 semi-private and 55 fully private environments. "Exactly 10 members of the public are tested on each environment", and "We defined the human baseline as the upper-median best human by number of actions used". "All ARC-AGI-3 environments are verified to be 100% solvable by humans with no prior task-specific training", and only environments fully solved by ≥2 of the 10 testers were included. Scores at release: Opus 4.6 (Max) 0.50%, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview 0.40%, GPT 5.4 (High) 0.20%, Grok-4.20 0.10% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621)
- ARC-AGI-3 RHAE scoring: per-level S = min(1.15, h/a)², level weights w_l = l over n = 5 levels, environment score capped by the fraction of levels completed, total = mean over environments — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621)
- Secondary sources describe the baseline as the "second-best human" and report 486 participants. A July 2026 top result of ~30% (secondary) was not verified on the official leaderboard — [the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/arc-agi-3-offers-2m-to-any-ai-that-matches-untrained-humans-yet-every-frontier-model-scores-below-1/); [capitalandcompute (secondary)](https://capitalandcompute.net/ai-benchmarks/arc-agi-3/)
- MTOB (Tanzer et al., ICLR 2024), learning to translate Kalamang from one grammar book: the human baseline (the first author, presented as a lower bound) scored **51.6 chrF kgv→eng and 57.0 eng→kgv** vs the best LLM (Claude 2) at 44.7 / 45.8. LLMs score near zero with no context — [ICLR 2024 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/52d63f9e4b81f866bf69fb3c834aad47-Paper-Conference.pdf); [HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Groq/mtob/blob/main/README.md)
- LearningBench (Kaggle grand prize) measures whether models learn the rules of new systems from scratch within one conversation. No paper or human baseline was found — [Ground Truth](https://groundtruth.day/news/kaggle-agi-hackathon-winners-announced.html)

**Memory**
- LongMemEval (Wu et al., ICLR 2025): 500 hand-written questions over extensible chat histories, testing five abilities: information extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, **knowledge updates** and **abstention**. Variants are S (~40 sessions, ~115k tokens), M (~500 sessions) and oracle. Commercial assistants show a ~30% accuracy drop over sustained interactions — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813v2); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/28290)
- LoCoMo (Maharana et al., ACL 2024): conversations of ~300 turns and ~9K tokens over up to 35 sessions, with QA, event summarisation and multimodal dialogue tasks. Models "still substantially lag behind human performance". Secondary figures: human QA F1 ≈ 88 vs ≈ 37–42 for LLMs, and human 92.6 on temporal questions — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17753v1); [Emergent Mind (secondary)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/locomo)
- CogArena's episodic-memory group uses DRM false memory, Source Monitoring and CVLT, and its working-memory group uses Digit Span, N-back and Operation Span — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24999.md)
- Hendrycks et al. score long-term memory storage at 0% for GPT-4 and GPT-5 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18212v3)

**Reasoning**
- ARC-AGI-2: the human panel average is **60%** (pass@2, ~$17/task) from controlled testing of 400+ participants, and every eval task was solved by ≥2 humans within 2 attempts — [ARC Prize](https://arcprize.org/arc-agi-2). A critique says the paper's own statistic is that test pairs were solved by 75% of people who attempted them, and estimates average human performance at ≤53% — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DX3EmhmwZjTYp9PBf/arc-agi-2-human-baseline-surpassed-updated)
- GPQA (Rein et al., arXiv 2311.12022): 448 questions. PhD experts score **65%** (74% after discounting errors they identified). Non-experts score **34%** despite spending more than 30 minutes with unrestricted web access. GPT-4 with few-shot CoT scored 39%. The Diamond subset has 198 questions — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12022v1); [Artificial Analysis](https://www.artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gpqa-diamond)

**Metacognition**
- AbstentionBench (Meta FAIR, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2506.09038): 20 datasets and 35k+ unanswerable questions covering unknown answers, underspecified queries, false premises, subjective and outdated questions. Scaling helps little, and reasoning fine-tuning **degrades abstention by 24%** on average. Code is at facebookresearch/AbstentionBench — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09038); [HF papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.09038)
- Signal-detection metacognition: a 2026 preprint proposes the M-ratio (meta-d′/d′) as complementary to ECE, Brier and AUROC. Over 224,000 factual-QA trials on four open LLMs, metacognitive efficiency varied even at similar Type-1 sensitivity, and temperature moved the criterion while meta-d′ stayed stable for two models — [HF papers 2603.25112](https://huggingface.co/papers/2603.25112.md); [arXiv 2603.29693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29693v1)
- Steyvers et al., "What large language models know and what people think they know" (Nature Machine Intelligence 2025; arXiv 2401.13835), defines the **calibration gap** and **discrimination gap** between model confidence and human confidence in model answers. Default explanations make people overestimate accuracy, and confidence-matched wording narrows both gaps — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13835); [UCI news](https://www.socsci.uci.edu/newsevents/news/2025/2025-01-22-steyvers-uci-study-finds-mismatch-between-human-perception-and-reliability-of-ai-assisted-language-tools.php)
- Survey: "Metacognition in LLMs: Foundations, Progress, and Opportunities" (Liu, Gani, Lu, Thomas, Steyvers, Cohan; arXiv 2607.11881, 13 Jul 2026) covers measurement methods and benchmarks — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11881)
- CogArena's metacognition group: Confidence Calibration and Wagering paradigms — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24999.md)

**Executive functions**
- PlanBench (Valmeekam, Stechly, Kambhampati): o1-preview solved 587/600 (97.8%) zero-shot Blocksworld and o1-mini 340/600 (56.6%). Performance on obfuscated "Mystery Blocksworld" lags far behind — [arXiv 2409.13373](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13373v1)
- Tower of Hanoi: in Apple's "Illusion of Thinking", reasoning models collapse beyond a complexity threshold even when given the algorithm. A rebuttal (arXiv 2506.09250) shows failures coincide with output-token limits, and "Rethinking the Illusion of Thinking" (arXiv 2507.01231) finds they are partly token limits and partly real limits — [HF papers 2506.09250](https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.09250); [arXiv 2507.01231](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01231v1)
- CogBench (Coda-Forno, Binz, Wang, Schulz; ICML 2024): 10 behavioural metrics from 7 classic cognitive-psychology experiments, text-based and procedurally generated (limits leakage), run on 35 LLMs — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/coda-forno24a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18225)

**Problem solving**
- The DeepMind paper names Humanity's Last Exam (Phan et al. 2025), GDPval (Patwardhan et al. 2025) and Remote Labor Index (Mazeika et al. 2025) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)

**Social cognition**
- Strachan et al., "Testing theory of mind in large language models and humans", Nature Human Behaviour 8:1285–1295 (2024): GPT-4 performed at or above human level on indirect requests, false beliefs and misdirection but struggled with faux pas, while Llama 2-70B outperformed humans on faux pas. The authors caution that matching humans does not mean the model has ToM — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v8y2024i7d10.1038_s41562-024-01882-z.html); [Princeton](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/testing-theory-of-mind-in-large-language-models-and-humans/)
- ToMBench (ACL 2024): 8 tasks, 31 social-cognition abilities, built bilingually from scratch, multiple choice. GPT-4 trails humans by "over 10% points" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15052)
- FANToM (EMNLP 2023): conversation-based, with information asymmetry. SOTA LLMs are "significantly worse than humans" even with CoT or fine-tuning — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.15421)
- OpenToM: LLMs approach human-level ToM on physical-state questions but lag on psychological states (secondary summary) — [Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/opentom)
- CogArena: among its 13 paradigms, matched human accuracies exist only for False Belief and EPITOME (empathy) — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24999.md)

**Generation (creativity)**
- Divergent Association Task vs 100,000 humans (Jerbi and colleagues, Scientific Reports, Jan 2026; article s41598-025-25157-3): GPT-4 scored significantly above the human mean, GeminiPro was indistinguishable from it, and the upper half of humans beat every model's mean. The study also tested haiku, synopses and flash fiction, where the best work was still mostly human (secondary coverage) — [Silicon Canals (secondary)](https://siliconcanals.com/n-ai-just-beat-the-average-human-on-a-creativity-test-involving-100000-people-and-the-researchers-say-the-real-question-is-whether-the-gap-between-average-and-exceptional-will-hold/); [letsdatascience (secondary)](https://letsdatascience.com/news/large-language-models-reach-average-human-creativity-beae504e)

### Inferences
- Faculty → candidate MIZAN measure (MIZAN parts in parentheses):
  - **Perception**: a BLINK or Perception Test subset (Basar images; Sama voice) with published human accuracy.
  - **Generation**: text and code are already partly covered by the `code` block and agentic tasks. Writing needs raters or a DAT-style automatic score.
  - **Attention**: RULER-style needle/aggregation tasks at increasing lengths, plus irrelevant-distractor variants of the `exactness` items. No published human norm exists, so collect one.
  - **Learning**: the `learning` block, the 3 games scored with exact RHAE against MIZAN's own 10-person panel, and an MTOB-style "learn from a document" task (Ta'lim/Taallum/Hifz are learning components).
  - **Memory**: a LongMemEval-S subset covering knowledge-update, abstention and temporal questions (Dhikr, Daftar, context_engine). **Forgetting** maps directly onto Mahw: does an erased fact stay unrecallable?
  - **Reasoning**: `arc` and `exactness` against the ARC-AGI-2 panel and GPQA expert/non-expert baselines.
  - **Metacognition**: `abstention` and `honesty` plus ECE/Brier already exist. Add AUROC₂ / meta-d′, confidence-before-answering ("feeling of knowing"), and MEDLEY-style resistance to social pressure (Isnad and Sidq grade claims and could feed monitoring).
  - **Executive functions**: PlanBench Blocksworld and Mystery variants, Tower of Hanoi with step budgets, text n-back/WCST/Stroop (Tadbir planning, Hudud inhibition).
  - **Problem solving**: the agentic sandbox tasks and `code`.
  - **Social cognition**: a ToMBench or Strachan-style battery with human data, plus Amanah/injection framed as "detecting deception".
- The existing `injection` and `safety` blocks do not map cleanly onto any faculty. They are propensity or safety measures, which the DeepMind paper explicitly sets aside ("system propensities"), so keep them as a separate safety panel outside the radar.

### Gaps
- Exact human numbers for FANToM, Hi-ToM and OpenToM were not verified here. Hi-ToM's human baseline was not found at all.
- BABILong, FrontierMath, SWE-bench Verified, CLadder and SocialIQA were not searched in this session, so their human baselines or lack of them are unverified. FrontierMath and SWE-bench are believed to lack a per-item human accuracy baseline; this is from prior knowledge and not re-verified.
- LongMemEval appears to have no human baseline; this was not confirmed in the paper text.
- The LoCoMo human F1 (~88) comes from a secondary summary, not the original paper.
- TruthfulQA's human baseline (the abstract reports 94% human truthfulness vs 58% for the best model; arXiv 2109.07958) and METR's human-timed task horizons (arXiv 2503.14499) are from prior knowledge and were not re-fetched in this session.

## Q3. Which can be run cheaply, offline and rule-scored with a published human baseline, and which need human raters?

### Takeaway
Cheap, offline, rule-scored and with a published human number: BLINK (MCQ, 95.7%), Perception Test (MCQ, 91.4%), MMMU (MCQ, expert range), GPQA (MCQ, 65%/34%), ARC-AGI-2 public eval (exact grid match, ~60% panel), ARC-AGI-3 public games (action counts, human per-level baseline, runs locally via the toolkit), MTOB (chrF, human lower bound) and Strachan or ToMBench-style ToM MCQs. Rule-scored but with no usable human norm, so MIZAN would need its own panel: RULER/NIAH, LongMemEval, AbstentionBench, PlanBench, and text n-back/Stroop/WCST. Human or LLM-judge raters are needed for writing quality, creativity beyond DAT, open-ended social skills (negotiation, persuasion) and speech generation.

### Cited Findings
- Perception Test releases public splits under CC-BY and keeps a held-out test split on a challenge server (offline-usable splits plus a contamination-resistant one) — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/8540fba4abdc7f9f7a7b1cc6cd60e409-Abstract.html)
- BLINK is entirely multiple choice (3,807 questions) with a reported human average — [ECCV](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/html/3356_ECCV_2024_paper.php)
- CogArena's 13 paradigms are procedurally generated with "deterministic and rule-based" scoring and no LLM judge, but matched human anchors exist for only 2 paradigms. "Human and LLM performance do not share a common scale" — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24999.md)
- CogBench experiments are text-based, procedurally generated (limits leakage) and have decades of human data, but the human data are behavioural signatures rather than one accuracy number — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18225); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/coda-forno24a.html)
- ARC-AGI-3 has a public demo set of 25 environments and an SDK and toolkit for agents ("Docs + SDK", docs.arcprize.org). The private sets are not public — [arcprize.org](https://arcprize.org/arc-agi/3); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621)
- AbstentionBench is open source (facebookresearch/AbstentionBench), but its paper reports no human abstention baseline in the sources read — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09038)
- MTOB's human baseline is a single person (the first author), presented as a lower bound — [ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/52d63f9e4b81f866bf69fb3c834aad47-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- The DeepMind protocol wants **private, held-out** test sets and its own human sample collected under identical conditions. Public benchmarks with published baselines are therefore a fallback, not the ideal — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)
- DAT creativity scoring is automatic (semantic distance between ten words) and has a 100,000-person human distribution, which makes it the rare generation measure with a large norm — [Silicon Canals (secondary)](https://siliconcanals.com/n-ai-just-beat-the-average-human-on-a-creativity-test-involving-100000-people-and-the-researchers-say-the-real-question-is-whether-the-gap-between-average-and-exceptional-will-hold/)
- Human neuropsychological norms exist for WCST, Stroop and digit span, but are stratified by age and education. For example, NEURONORMA-Plus WCST norms cover 700 participants aged 18–90, and education stratification is argued to be "essential" — [Barcelonaβeta](https://www.barcelonabeta.org/en/node/1029); [Redalyc (Brazilian norms)](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/3395/339539880006.pdf)

### Inferences
- A practical MIZAN plan: for each faculty, include one public set that has a published human baseline (an external anchor) and one private MIZAN-written set, held out and hash-locked like `mizan-eval-v1`. Collect a small human panel on the private set; the DeepMind protocol prescribes this. With 10 people per item set, as ARC-AGI-3 does per environment, percentiles are coarse, but they are honest when reported with intervals.
- Offline constraints: BLINK, MMMU and Perception Test require images, video and audio, so they test Basar and Sama rather than the text-only path. Perception Test video is heavy; BLINK is the cheapest perception anchor.
- Raters: GDPval-style paired expert preference, or a rubric with two raters and an inter-rater κ, is the defensible route for generation and social-skill items. LLM-as-judge should be reported separately and never mixed into rule-scored faculties.

### Gaps
- Kaggle's Community Benchmarks platform terms (whether tasks can be exported and run offline, and which models) were not verified.
- No source gave per-item human response data that is downloadable for BLINK, GPQA or ARC-AGI-2. Building percentiles (not just a mean) needs per-person scores. Whether those are public is unknown.

## Q4. How to compute a defensible radar profile: normalisation to human baselines, intervals, contamination, IRT, aggregation pitfalls

### Takeaway
Score each faculty as the percentile of the human sample the system outperforms (DeepMind's definition), with an interval from a bootstrap over items and human participants. Use clustered standard errors when items share a passage, game or template. Use paired differences for base vs MIZAN. An IRT model, or at least a multi-item latent score, is preferred over raw means, but faculty labels must be validated: CogArena found one broad factor explaining ~50% of variance and no stable faculty structure in LLMs. Radar area and shape are not quantities to rank on.

### Cited Findings
- DeepMind: the faculty score is "the percentage of people from the human sample the system outperforms". IRT is suggested over simple aggregation, and uncertainty should cover task quality, construct validity and stochasticity — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)
- Clustered SEs: Miller (Anthropic), "Adding Error Bars to Evals" (arXiv 2411.00640, Nov 2024). Clustered standard errors can be more than 3× the naive ones on popular evals; cluster on the unit of randomisation; for model comparisons report mean differences, SEs, CIs and correlations, since paired designs shrink the SE of a difference — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00640); [Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals)
- `evalci` (arXiv 2607.04429, 2026) is a Python library packaging these methods for comparing LM evaluations — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04429)
- IRT for LLMs: tinyBenchmarks (Polo et al., ICML 2024) estimates MMLU performance from 100 curated items — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14992v2). Fluid Benchmarking (Hofmann et al., COLM 2025) uses IRT ability estimates plus adaptive item selection; mislabeled items in a 100-item eval fell from 0.75 to 0.01 — [Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2509.11106). ATLAS (arXiv 2511.04689) is adaptive testing on 3PL IRT with Fisher-information item selection; it notes that tinyBenchmarks and metabench report no fit statistics — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04689v2)
- Martínez-Plumed et al. (2019), "Item response theory in AI", is the IRT reference cited by the DeepMind paper — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)
- The Epoch Capabilities Index uses IRT to stitch benchmarks onto one linear scale, inferring difficulty from overlapping model results (41 benchmarks per Epoch docs; anchored Claude 3.5 Sonnet = 130, GPT-5 = 150). It warns that benchmark-specific optimisation inflates estimates — [Epoch ECI docs](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation); [methodology](https://epoch.ai/data/eci-documentation/methodology). A third-party post extends it to a four-axis Bayesian multidimensional IRT model with human baselines (exploratory, not Epoch's) — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/gbJYR8TMaQKsvjrkh/a-four-axis-bayesian-epoch-capabilities-index-with-human)
- Faculty structure caveat (CogArena, 20–55 LLMs): the first principal component explains 49.8% of variance and correlates r = .99 with mean accuracy. The within-group vs cross-group advantage is small and unstable (δ = .081, p = .057; it shrinks to .011 with within-family centring and reverses under construct-native scoring). Targeted scaffolds show only a tiny selective effect (Γ = .0199), and held-out-family prediction fails. Recommendation: treat groupings as "organizing labels", report replicated paradigms, and use family-aware inference — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24999.md)
- Criticism of the taxonomy: the categories may not be natural kinds for transformers because the evidence comes from human cognition (opinion piece) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/josef_albers_fc59b610c5de/on-measuring-progress-toward-agi-2jko)
- Contamination: DeepMind asks for private held-out sets and notes that most public benchmarks are contaminable — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1). ARC-AGI-3 keeps 55 semi-private and 55 fully private environments — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621). Procedural generation (CogBench, CogArena) is another defence — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18225)
- Baseline choice changes conclusions. ARC-AGI-3 uses the upper-median best of 10 humans per level, and critics note this sits above a typical person — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621); [the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/new-arc-agi-3-benchmark-shows-that-humans-still-outperform-llms-at-pretty-basic-thinking/). ARC-AGI-2's "60%" vs "75%" vs "≤53%" shows the same problem — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DX3EmhmwZjTYp9PBf/arc-agi-2-human-baseline-surpassed-updated)
- Equal-weight aggregation across domains, as in Hendrycks et al.'s AGI Score (each of 10 domains weighted equally into 0–100%), differs from DeepMind, which reports a profile and declines to give a single number — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18212v3); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28405v1)

### Inferences
- A defensible MIZAN recipe:
  1. Per faculty f and arm (base, MIZAN), estimate ability θ_f from item responses. Use a Rasch/2PL model per faculty if there are at least ~30 items; otherwise use the mean with clustered SE.
  2. Score the same items on a human panel, giving one ability per person.
  3. Report the percentile P_f = share of humans with θ below the system's θ.
  4. Get the 95% interval by bootstrap: resample humans, items (clustered by template, game or passage) and the system's repeated runs (k samples per item, which MIZAN already has via pass^k).
  5. Show the radar with interval bands, and show the base-vs-MIZAN paired difference per faculty with its CI.
- With today's block sizes (3–13 items), Wilson intervals are roughly ±25–45 percentage points. A faculty axis built from one block cannot separate base from MIZAN unless the effect is very large. Each faculty probably needs at least 40–60 items before its axis means anything; this is an inference from Wilson widths, not a sourced figure.
- Do not rank systems by radar area. Area depends on axis order and scale, and DeepMind's Figure 2 is illustrative. Use per-axis intervals and a "faculties above human median" count with its uncertainty.
- Keep the Artificial-Analysis-weighted index as a separate headline number. The radar should not be collapsed into it, because DeepMind deliberately avoids a single score.
- Contamination control for MIZAN: MIZAN learns per user (Hifz, Taallum, Qasd.learn), so evaluation must keep running under `stateless.bind()`, as Imtihan already does, and private items must never enter learning stores. Otherwise the "learning" faculty and the held-out property contaminate each other.

### Gaps
- No source compared radar-area metrics against per-axis reporting. The axis-order artefact is standard data-visualisation reasoning and is not cited here.
- No published guidance was found on the minimum human panel size for stable percentiles in AI-vs-human cognitive profiles. ARC-AGI-3 uses 10 per environment and ARC-AGI-2 used 400+ overall.
- Burnell et al. (2023, Science, "Rethink reporting of evaluation results in AI"; Burnell is also the DeepMind paper's first author) is relevant to instance-level reporting but was not fetched in this session.

## Q5. Cognitive science tests adaptable to agents (n-back, WCST, Stroop, false belief) and their human norms

### Takeaway
All four have long human literatures, and text versions have been run on LLMs. Their human "norms" are mostly behavioural signatures (n-back accuracy falls at n = 3; Stroop interference; WCST perseverative errors; false-belief pass rates by age) or age- and education-stratified clinical norms, not one adult accuracy figure. CogArena shows several replicate poorly in text form (text Stroop replicated in 7/20 models). MIZAN should treat them as executive and working-memory probes with signature-based scoring, plus its own adult panel.

### Cited Findings
- **n-back (working memory)**: Gong et al., "Working Memory Capacity of ChatGPT: An Empirical Study" (AAAI 2024) ran verbal and spatial n-back. ChatGPT's verbal capacity is about n = 3, "strikingly similar" to humans, whose performance "drops significantly when n = 3". GPT-4 showed higher capacity than the other LLMs — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/28868); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.03731v3)
- **WCST (set shifting) and Stroop (inhibition)**: a Psychological Bulletin review lists WCST as set shifting, Stroop (incongruent vs congruent vs neutral trials) as inhibition and n-back as updating (via search summary; [PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3436964/table/T1)). Normative studies: Spanish NEURONORMA-Plus Modified WCST (700 participants aged 18–90; age and education matter, sex does not) — [Barcelonaβeta](https://www.barcelonabeta.org/en/node/1029); Brazilian WCST, Stroop and Digit Span norms for adults aged 19–75 with ≥5 years of education — [Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/3395/339539880006.pdf)
- **Text Stroop, Flanker and Go/No-Go in LLMs**: CogArena found Flanker and DRM signatures replicate in 18/20 checkpoints but text Stroop in only 7/20 — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24999.md)
- **False belief (Sally-Anne)**: Baron-Cohen, Leslie and Frith (1985, Cognition 21:37–46) tested 20 autistic children, 14 children with Down syndrome and 27 typically developing children. Autistic children largely failed the belief question (~20% passed). A 2002 replication found the deficit was not specific to autism — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally%E2%80%93Anne_test); [Kissgen & Schleiffer 2002 (PDF)](https://www.hf.uni-koeln.de/data/ps/File/Kissgen%20&%20Schleiffer%202002.pdf); [The Transmitter](https://thetransmitter.org/spectrum/1985-paper-on-the-theory-of-mind)
- **Adult ToM battery with human comparison**: Strachan et al. (2024) compared LLMs with human participants on false belief, irony, faux pas, hinting (indirect requests) and strange stories (misdirection). GPT-4 was at or above human level except on faux pas — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v8y2024i7d10.1038_s41562-024-01882-z.html); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/05/20/1092681/ai-models-can-outperform-humans-in-tests-to-identify-mental-states/amp/)
- **Tower of Hanoi / planning**: a classic executive-function task, now an LLM puzzle with the token-limit confound noted above — [HF papers 2506.09250](https://huggingface.co/papers/2506.09250); [arXiv 2507.01231](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01231v1)
- **Batteries**: CogBench (7 experiments, 10 metrics, 35 LLMs; ICML 2024) — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/coda-forno24a.html). CogArena (13 paradigms in 5 groups: working memory, cognitive control, episodic memory, ToM, metacognition; deterministic scoring) — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24999.md)
- **Metacognition norms**: the meta-d′ framework (Fleming & Lau 2014 tutorial is the standard human reference) is argued to be the "gold standard" for AI metacognitive sensitivity. No paper reports meta-d′ for humans and LLMs on the same items with a formal comparison — [arXiv 2603.29693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29693v1); [HF papers 2603.25112](https://huggingface.co/papers/2603.25112.md)
- **Classic false-belief tests are likely contaminated**: Sally-Anne and similar vignettes are widely published. That is why ToMBench was written from scratch, FANToM uses new conversations, and CogBench and CogArena generate items procedurally — [ToMBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15052); [FANToM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.15421); [CogBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18225)

### Inferences
- Agent adaptations for MIZAN, all rule-scored and procedurally generated so they stay held out:
  - **n-back**: a stream of letters or numbers delivered as separate chat turns, which tests MIZAN's context engine and memory rather than one prompt. Score d′ (hits vs false alarms) at n = 1–4 and check for the human-like drop near n = 3.
  - **WCST**: hidden sort rule (colour/shape/number), feedback only, unannounced rule switch after 10 correct. Score categories completed and perseverative errors. This fits Tamyiz/Qasd-style online Bayesian updating and tests cognitive flexibility.
  - **Stroop / Flanker**: conflicting cue vs instruction, e.g. "answer with the ink word, not the written word", in text or Basar images. Score the interference cost as an accuracy difference (latency is unreliable for LLMs). Text Stroop replicates poorly, so prefer Flanker-style tasks.
  - **Go/No-Go**: a clean measure of inhibition and of Hudud/approval behaviour, e.g. "do not act on these items".
  - **False belief**: fresh second-order vignettes plus FANToM-style multi-party chats with information asymmetry. Score by exact choice.
  - **Prospective memory**: "remind me when X appears" cues planted across sessions; Muraqabah watchers make this directly testable.
  - **Forgetting**: after a Mahw erase, probe recall and require a 0% leak. This is a sub-faculty DeepMind lists, and almost no benchmark covers it.
- Human norms: clinical WCST and Stroop norms are age- and education-stratified, and their trial formats differ from an agent adaptation. They are not directly transferable. MIZAN should run the exact same adapted items on its own adult panel (upper-secondary education, per DeepMind) and use the clinical norms only as a sanity check on direction and magnitude.

### Gaps
- Specific adult accuracy norms for 2-back/3-back (for example from the Owen et al. 2005 meta-analysis), WCST perseverative-error means and Stroop interference sizes were not retrieved in this session.
- The ~85% pass rate often quoted for typically developing children on Sally-Anne in 1985 could not be verified from the sources found.
- No source was found that ran a full WCST on frontier agents in 2025–2026 with human comparison.
